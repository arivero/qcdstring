# What the vortex doublet predicts on a Seiberg–Witten electric string

**Codex (GPT-6.2 Astra) · qcdstring programme**

*QCDS-95/S1, November 1995. Odd-year corollary to QCDS-92/1. Historical cutoff 30 November 1995.*

## Abstract

The local vortex calculation of QCDS-92/1 predicts a pair of scalar poles with masses $m_\gamma(\nu_0\pm w/2+O(w^2))$ and equal limiting shares of one two-phonon trace residue. We determine which electric string realizes the hypotheses of that result. In pure $\mathcal N=2$ $SU(2)$ with a small adjoint mass $\mu$, a fundamental Wilson source has unit pairing with the condensed monopole. The resulting string has a controlled two-derivative dual description. In the constant-coupling action, matching the magnetic period fixes its vector mass, its leading tension, and the soft parameter $w=g^2|\mu|/(2m_\gamma)$. The even mass difference is consequently $g^2|\mu|/2$ to leading order in that action. The same string has nonzero subthreshold odd spectral weight; in photon units the critical odd mass is $0.881744$ and the lowest bulk mass tends to one. An additional neutral odd state is degenerate at the critical point. We calculate its relative squared-mass displacement in the constant-coupling action and project the analytic gauge-kinetic mixing on both localized waves. Individual odd residues can be redistributed even as the bulk gap stays finite. Finally we compare the even separation with the partial width and with finite-length mixing along the microscopic trajectory. The existence of a controlled electric flux sector and the resolution of two separate scalar peaks pose different conditions. For the two spatial spin structures of the microscopic fermions, the four Goldstinos of the string contribute $\pi/(2L)$ to the difference of the vacuum-subtracted energies on a circle of length $L$. We prove that the two translations are the only gapless bosons at the BPS point; that the Goldstinos exhaust the gapless fermions, and hence the total coefficients, rests on one stated pairing hypothesis. No continuation to nonsupersymmetric Yang–Mills is assumed.

## 1. Why an electric-string corollary is needed

A line defect carries a calculated spectrum only after specifying which line is prepared. A unit vortex of a Higgs field carries magnetic flux of the connection that acts on that field. The corresponding Higgs phase screens electric charge coupled to the same connection. This raises a concrete question for the local prediction in [1]. If the observable in a microscopic gauge theory is a Wilson line for an external electric charge, does that line actually create the vortex whose local modes were calculated? A coincidence of tension scales would not fix the charge. Nor would attaching the name “dual photon” to an arbitrary vector field determine its period or its coupling to a fundamental source.

The solution for pure $\mathcal N=2$ $SU(2)$ and its softly broken vacua [2] supplies the missing information. A massless magnetic particle exists in a particular patch of the moduli space. A weak perturbation condenses it and gives a mass to the magnetic photon. At distances long compared with the inverse mass of the other charged states, an ordinary local description of the condensate exists. The topological linking number between an external electric source and the monopole fixes how many periods of its connection must leave the source. The same special coordinate that vanishes at the monopole point determines the coefficients of the local superpotential. Both pieces of information enter the corollary. One identifies the defect, the other determines how it approaches the critical vortex when the adjoint mass is small.

We study a very long, straight unit tube, either far from two fixed external sources or in a periodic flux sector. We ask about internal excitations at zero spatial momentum along it and about their coupling to a right and a left transverse translation. On the infinite string an internal state can decay into lighter sheet excitations. The one-particle continuum in the *bulk* starts at a positive energy in the condensed vacuum. These thresholds concern different Hilbert spaces. An internal mode can be a discrete eigenfunction in the transverse plane and still contribute a resonance in two-dimensional scattering. In particular the value of its transverse norm and the residue of the translation source are meaningful classical matching data even in a theory whose exact infinite-string correlator has no stable massive pole on the real axis.

The historical order matters for interpreting this example. The mass-slope theorem in [1] was derived entirely with a local Abelian action, without a Seiberg–Witten curve or a non-Abelian confinement assumption. Here that theorem is used as an input, and the static, spectral and scale assumptions are tested on a particular gauge-theory trajectory. Our extra calculations address two effects that the local trace theorem alone cannot decide. The microscopic limit correlates scales that were independent in the local problem. In addition, fields that are invisible to the leading phonon source can have the same discrete quantum numbers as a visible state. We calculate how close such a field is in the odd sector and what one specified microscopic interaction would do to its pole weight.

All the estimates below are at declared orders in the weakly coupled dual description. We use a canonical kinetic normalization when stating a mass or scattering residue. We keep the integer charge normalization when comparing sources and fluxes. A useful dimensionless combination of a residue and a mass will cancel the gauge coupling at fixed local shape. This cancellation does not assert that all strings with this shape have the same mass in units of their tension. The latter ratio retains an important dependence on the running coupling in the actual monopole vacuum.

## 2. Which source ends the tube?

Consider the theory without fundamental dynamical matter. Choose an electric Cartan normalization in which the heavy adjoint electric root carries one unit. A fundamental external probe then has weights $\pm1/2$. Equivalently, in conventional semiclassical electromagnetic units its charge has magnitude $g_{\rm el}/2$, and the flux of a smooth primitive monopole is $4\pi/g_{\rm el}$. The product is $2\pi$. This integer Dirac pairing concerns transport of a line around magnetic flux; it remains meaningful as one continues to the patch in which that monopole becomes light. The numerical value of the original semiclassical electric coupling does not enter the integer. We now choose the local dual connection $A$ to give the monopole charge $+1$.

One can obtain the factor directly from the first-order gauge action. The electric-magnetic change of variables in Eqs. (3.12)–(3.14) of [2] uses a Lagrange multiplier for the Bianchi identity with coefficient $1/(4\pi)$ in differential-form notation. On varying the independent field strength this equates the electric displacement two-form, up to orientation, to $F/(4\pi)$ in the dual variables. The electric field equation in the presence of a Wilson source of weight $n_e$ then gives $|\int_{S^2} F|=4\pi|n_e|$ on a linking two-sphere. The fundamental weight supplies $2\pi$ and the adjoint root $4\pi$. Dirac pairing and this direct variation agree. If a convention with unit *fundamental* electric weight is used instead, the electric displacement normalization changes together with that weight. The number of dual periods remains one for the fundamental line.

A useful operational definition uses a large rectangular Wilson loop in the fundamental representation. Its two timelike sides prepare static sources separated by $R$. For Euclidean time $T_E$ large compared with all the relevant inverse excitation gaps at that finite $R$, define
$$
 V_{\rm fund}(R)=-\lim_{T_E\to\infty}\frac1{T_E}\log\langle W_{1/2}(R,T_E)\rangle .
$$
The additive rest energies of the two probes require a prescription. They drop out of the asymptotic force. The linking-sphere condition can be met by dual magnetic flux confined in the interior of a tube of finite thickness. In the condensed phase the photon and bulk scalar masses make longitudinally uniform vacuum departures expensive far from the tube. For the regular unit branch, the leading energy of the long central section is $\sigma R$, while endpoint adjustments contribute terms subleading in $R$. This identifies the coefficient in the area dependence of the loop with the vortex tension of the matched low-energy action to the stated accuracy. The statement is semiclassical and local to the weak monopole regime; an exact Wilson loop for arbitrary coupling is unnecessary for this identification.

The microscopic restriction on screening is central to this example. A fundamental test source cannot be screened by any product of adjoint dynamical fields. The distinction in the full theory is a $\mathbb Z_2$ center charge. A vortex of the low-energy magnetic $U(1)$ has an integer winding because the charged condensate has a compact phase. At energies where electrically charged ultraviolet states must be retained, two units of flux can be added or removed without changing the center class. Thus the unit vortex has a distinguished microscopic role, while the several-winding calculations of [1] are results on specified circular branches of the local theory. A long-lived even-winding configuration in an action that omits heavy charges is insufficient evidence for an additional exact confining sector in the parent. For general gauge groups there are further charge and tension questions [3]. For $SU(2)$ a single odd center charge suffices to make the proposed spectroscopy precise.

The unit tube has an orientation in the Abelian description, fixed by the sign of its flux and the ordering of the external probes. A trace of a fundamental Wilson loop sums over equivalent choices of Cartan basis; it does not select the sign of a dual photon field as a microscopic local observable. Accordingly, a defect insertion can be chosen invariant under simultaneous reversal of the dual charge and field conventions. The odd insertion constructed below has this property. A two-phonon polarization experiment can also be defined with geometric displacements of the tube that leave its center charge unchanged. There is no need to postulate that a particular ultraviolet gluonic local density equals a dual field component pointwise.

## 3. The monopole period and the local parameters

Write $u=\langle\mathrm{Tr}\,\Phi^2\rangle$ for the Coulomb-branch coordinate and take the singularity $u=\Lambda^2$, with a choice of phase for $\Lambda$. The magnetic period $a_D$ vanishes there. In a low-energy Wilsonian description with light monopoles kept explicitly, use the dual vector multiplet and two chiral fields $q,\tilde q$ of charges $+1,-1$. The superpotential in [2, §5.6] is
$$
 W=\sqrt2\,a_D q\tilde q+\mu u(a_D).
                                                                    \tag{1}
$$
The first term fixes the mass $\sqrt2|a_D|$ of an uncondensed monopole hypermultiplet. The second is the representation of the microscopic adjoint-mass source. In a supersymmetric condensed vacuum the two monopole equations impose $a_D=0$ and the remaining F-equation fixes $q\tilde q=-\mu u'(0)/\sqrt2$. The D-equation imposes equal magnitudes. With $v^2=|q|^2+|\tilde q|^2$, this gives $v^2=\sqrt2|\mu u'(0)|$. The other singularity selects a different dual patch and condensing charges. We make the calculation in one CP-invariant vacuum with real phases chosen as below.

The two coefficients of the period expansion needed here can be obtained from the same integral. Let $d=(u-\Lambda^2)/\Lambda^2$ and set $x=1+dt$ in the dimensionless magnetic period integral (6.23) of [2]. Select the cycle sign giving a positive imaginary leading period for positive $d$. Restoring its dimension,
$$
 a_D=\frac{i\Lambda d}{\pi}\int_0^1\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{t}}\,
                      (1+dt/2)^{-1/2}\,dt
     =\frac{i\Lambda d}{2}\left[1-\frac{d}{16}+O(d^2)\right].
                                                                    \tag{2}
$$
The zeroth and first moments of the weight are $\pi/2$ and $\pi/8$. Inverting gives $u=\Lambda^2-2i\Lambda a_D-a_D^2/4+O(a_D^3/\Lambda)$. The linear source in (1) yields $v^2=2\sqrt2|\mu\Lambda|$. The quadratic coefficient is the source of soft breaking within the dual model built on this condensate. If written $\mu_D a_D^2/2$, then $\mu_D=\mu u''(0)=-\mu/2$ before phase redefinitions. Counting the coefficient of $a_D^2$ as its second derivative instead would shift the physical deformation by a factor of two. The parameter that can be inferred from the bulk mass spectrum is the coefficient of the *canonically normalized* neutral mass.

It is useful to separate holomorphic data from real kinetic data in this matching. The period determines where the monopole condenses and which superpotential sources are present. Kinetic normalization determines the photon mass for given $v$, the normalization of the neutral scalar and the absolute string excitation energy. Near the singularity the Coulomb-branch effective coupling has a logarithm due to the light monopole. Keeping the monopoles in (1) and inserting that singular, monopole-integrated-out coupling at $a_D=0$ would count the same light loop twice. We instead use a nonsingular Wilsonian coupling $g$ at the light matching scale, and discuss the analytic term in its field dependence in §8. The distinction matters even if a dimensionless spectral product happens to have a finite $g\to0$ limit.

In the leading two-derivative action take the dual gauge kinetic term as $-F^2/(4g^2)$, the neutral kinetic coefficient as $1/g^2$, and the monopole kinetic coefficients as one. On rephasing the neutral coordinate and the product $q\tilde q$, the bosonic scalar potential through quadratic superpotential order can be written
$$
 V=\frac{g^2}{2}(|q|^2-|\tilde q|^2)^2
  +g^2\left|\sqrt2(q\tilde q-v^2/2)+MZ\right|^2
  +2|Z|^2(|q|^2+|\tilde q|^2),\qquad M=|\mu_D|.                   \tag{3}
$$
Here $Z$ denotes the rephased local magnetic coordinate. The spectrum in the real trace sector will be the spectrum of [1] at the $v,g,M$ determined here. A D-type constant auxiliary term which continued to be aligned so as to support a BPS string in the presence of a neutral mass would be a different deformation. In (3) the constant and the linear-in-$Z$ terms occur in the same complex square. It is their nonzero cross term inside the core that drives a new neutral profile. The placement of the source is part of the matching data.

The definitions and the microscopic relations are
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 m_\gamma&=\sqrt2gv, & w&=g^2 M/m_\gamma,\\
 \frac{m_\gamma}{|\Lambda|}&=2\,2^{1/4}g\sqrt{|\mu/\Lambda|},
 &w&=\frac{g}{4\,2^{1/4}}\sqrt{|\mu/\Lambda|}.
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{4}
$$
In particular $m_\gamma/|\Lambda|=8\sqrt2 w$ in this convention. The conditions $|\mu|/|\Lambda|\ll1$ and $m_\gamma/|\Lambda|\ll1$ give a soft perturbation and a derivative expansion in the scale of the string core relative to the heavy scale. The dimensionless loop parameter of the dual theory also becomes small in the asymptotic regime, albeit only logarithmically. It is consistent to expand at small $w$ at fixed photon units in the classical action. A numerical input such as $w=0.3$ in that action would correspond to $m_\gamma>|\Lambda|$ if interpreted using (4), so it cannot establish a physical endpoint on the microscopic trajectory.

These relations already exhibit a property relevant to an interpretation as a hadronic string. At leading order $\sqrt\sigma$ will be proportional to $v$, while $m_\gamma$ is proportional to $gv$. Thus $m_\gamma/\sqrt\sigma$ goes to zero as the dual coupling decreases. The radius of the string core in tension units diverges in this limit. It is still possible to choose an energy below all one-particle bulk masses and to excite a localized mode there; the useful variable for doing so is energy in units of $m_\gamma$. A string described at all scales below $\sqrt\sigma$ by its two translations alone would have a different regime of validity.

## 4. One vortex, its tension and the even corollary

Let $\rho=m_\gamma r$, $q=H/\sqrt2$, $\tilde q=H^*/\sqrt2$, $H=v f(\rho)e^{i\theta}$, $A=a(\rho)d\theta$, $Z=gv s(\rho)$. These fields are invariant under charge exchange with complex conjugation at fixed $A$. Their real action is stationary against the other bosonic variations by that symmetry when the gauge kinetic term is constant. At $w=0$, $s=0$ and the energy admits the standard vortex-square completion. Its saturated boundary term fixes the tension and its transverse profiles obey
$$
 f_0'=(1-a_0)f_0/\rho,\qquad b_0=a_0'/\rho=(1-f_0^2)/2,
 \qquad \sigma_{\rm B}=2\pi v^2=4\sqrt2\pi|\mu\Lambda| .            \tag{5}
$$
The subscript on $\sigma_{\rm B}$ denotes the leading Bogomolny tension evaluated at the same condensate scale. It is not the tension of the undeformed microscopic theory at $\mu=0$. At $\mu=0$ the Coulomb branch is restored; the limit in (5) is taken on the sequence of *condensed* vacua with positive $|\mu|$. A nonzero $\sigma_{\rm B}$ at a given member and a vanishing tension as the sequence approaches zero are compatible statements.

One can keep track of the leading adjustment of the full background with very little extra calculation. At nonzero $w$ the normalized energy is
$$
 \tau=\frac{\sigma}{2\pi v^2}=\int_0^\infty\rho\,d\rho\left[
 f'^2+(1-a)^2f^2/\rho^2+(a'/\rho)^2+s'^2+f^2s^2+
 (f^2-1+2ws)^2/4\right].
                                                                    \tag{6}
$$
The lower boundary of $f,a$ is zero with $f=O(\rho)$, $a=O(\rho^2)$ and $s'(0)=0$; the upper boundary is $(f,a,s)=(1,1,0)$. Varying $s$ gives $(L_f+w^2)s=w(1-f^2)/2$, where $L_f=-\partial_\rho^2-\partial_\rho/\rho+f^2$. The Maxwell equation gives $L_f(1-a)=2a'/\rho$. Combining the two with (5) yields $s=w(1-a_0)/2+O(w^3)$ on the centered branch. The operator $L_{f_0}$ has positive quadratic form and no regular decaying zero mode; there is no unspecified homogeneous part in this solution. On a centered circular vortex the charged Hessian also has no radial zero at fixed flux and radial gauge. Thus the expansion through this order has a well-defined branch. It does not fix the motion of the transverse translation zero modes.

There is a particularly simple way to see why eliminating the neutral field matters even for the tension. The signed change $w\mapsto-w$ reverses $s$ and leaves $f,a$ even. The order-$w^2$ charged deformations have zero first variation against the critical energy. Minimizing in the remaining neutral direction gives the coefficient $-\frac12\int_0^\infty(1-a_0)a'_0 d\rho=-1/4$. Therefore
$$
 \tau=1-w^2/4+O(w^4),\qquad
 \sigma_{\rm tree}=4\sqrt2\pi|\mu\Lambda|-
          \pi g^2|\mu|^2/16+O(\sigma_{\rm B}w^4).                    \tag{7}
$$
The minus sign is a variational relaxation; a trial retaining $s=0$ at the same flux cannot lower its energy along that direction. The coefficient is a result for (3) at its matched couplings. It does not include the full nonholomorphic and quantum corrections to the tension of a non-BPS object. At fixed $v,g$ and finite positive $w$, the stationary identity $w\,d\tau/dw=-2\int\rho(s'^2+f^2s^2)d\rho$ provides a further check. On varying the microscopic parameter $\mu$ the leading scale $2\pi v^2$ also moves, and the logarithmic running must be included when assigning a derivative to a dimensional tension.

The doublet theorem requires a radial vector mode of $L_{f_0}$ with isolated eigenvalue $\lambda_0=\nu_0^2<1$. Section 5 identifies its physical source on the electric string. In the real even sector the normalized critical shape/neutral basis can be chosen as $S=(f_0u_0,-u'_0,0)/\nu_0$ and $N=(0,0,u_0)$ with $\int 2\pi\rho u_0^2d\rho=1$. The third slot is the neutral scalar along its real background. Under $w$ the linearized off-diagonal is $w\langle u_0,f_0^2(2-a_0)u_0\rangle/\nu_0$. The virial identity of [1] sets the matrix element to $w\nu_0$. The two eigenvectors tend to $(S\pm N)/\sqrt2$ with equal canonical norms; the trace source couples to $S$ at zero deformation. This is the only input we require from the detailed spectral proof. In particular, a measurement of the two *bulk* chiral masses does not replace the calculation of that radial matrix element.

Substitution of (4) gives a dimensional prediction on this branch of the gauge theory:
$$
 \begin{aligned}
  m_{T,\pm}&=m_\gamma\nu_0\pm g^2|\mu|/4 +O(m_\gamma w^2),\\
  m_{T,+}-m_{T,-}&=g^2|\mu|/2+O(m_\gamma w^2),\qquad
  R_{T,\pm}/R_A\longrightarrow\tfrac12 .
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{8}
$$
The subscript $T$ denotes the transverse-polarization trace of a right and a left phonon. The residues refer to normalized states and the leading constant-coupling action. Eq. (8) is a corollary for the original *electric* flux sector, with $g$ defined by dual canonical normalization. Doubling a monopole charge in the local action at fixed physical source would change the coefficient inferred for (8); a corresponding inverse rescaling of the connection and $g$ is necessary under any change of integer basis.

Eliminating the unnormalized soft parameter between (7) and (8) gives a check on the use of the same action:
$$
  \lim_{w\downarrow0}
    \frac{[(m_{T,+}-m_{T,-})/m_\gamma]^2}{1-\sigma/\sigma_{\rm B}}=4 .
                                                                    \tag{9}
$$
This limit compares two changes, including a small difference of tensions. A numerical calculation can measure the tension loss more accurately by subtracting the energy of the critical solution computed at the same cutoff. The [1994 radial printout](../1994-monopole-string/results/spectrum.json) gives ratios $3.99959,3.99744,3.98970$ for $w=0.01,0.025,0.05$ when its independent charged/neutral Hessian and its tensions are combined. Only the small-$w$ limit is asserted. A fit using the same measured mass gap to calibrate $w$ and then quoting (8) as a prediction would discard the content of the comparison. In the period matching, $\mu$, $\Lambda$ and the kinetic normalization have definitions independent of the two string levels. A physical use of (9) needs the reference tension at those definitions and control of its corrections at the small difference being measured.

## 5. An odd particle of the unit electric tube

A string selected by the electric Wilson line has magnetic flux in the *dual* variables. Its longitudinal electric oscillation therefore refers in this section to an electric component of $F^c=F/g$ in the monopole patch. This does not reverse the interpretation of the static line. The magnetic background sets the hole in the local condensate; the oscillating component and the static one are different tensor components of the same field. An observable even under reversal of the dual charge convention is the product of the two. This supplies a way to discuss a pseudoscalar in an electric confining system without assigning a sign to a fundamental weight inside a traced Wilson loop.

At rest on the sheet, set $\delta A_z^c=\psi(r)b_z(t)$ and all other field variations to zero. Since the static fields have $D_z=0$, neither the charged scalar magnitudes nor the neutral scalar acquire a source linear in this variation. The perturbed Gauss constraint has $\partial_z F^{z0}=0$. The transverse gauge equations also have no linear source at zero longitudinal momentum. For a propagating wave the covariant completion is $\delta A_a^c=\psi b_a$ with $a=0,3$ and $\partial_a b^a=0$. In the linearized scalar equations the would-be source from $\partial_a b^a$ then vanishes. These statements use the full bosonic equations of the constant-coupling theory, including the neutral gradients that support the relaxed core in (6). It is unnecessary to enforce vanishing scalar fluctuations by an external constraint.

Write $\psi=m_\gamma u(\rho)$ and $m_b=m_\gamma\nu_b$. The normalization and transverse equation are
$$
 2\pi\int_0^\infty\rho u^2 d\rho=1,\qquad
 L_f u=\nu_b^2 u,\qquad (\Box_{1,1}-m_b^2)b_a=0 .                 \tag{10}
$$
This is a massive vector in one space and one time dimension, with a single physical polarization. It can be represented on shell by the pseudoscalar dual to $G_{03}=\partial_0 b_3-\partial_3 b_0$. The full field-strength perturbation $F^c_{03}=\psi G_{03}$ is gauge invariant. A gauge choice linear in the longitudinal coordinate that sets $A^c_z$ to zero would induce other field variations and could not remove this tensor. The worldsheet oscillator here has positive mass and transverse norm; it is distinct from a static holonomy around a periodic direction. The bound longitudinal vector in the Abelian Higgs theory has a prior literature [4,5] and was matched to polarized phonons in [6].

There is a direct variational proof that (10) binds. On the radial minimizing branch at $w\ge0$ take $0\le f\le1$ and $s\ge0$. Replacing $f$ by $\min(|f|,1)$ and $s$ by its positive part can only reduce the two gradient and angular terms and preserves the boundary data. To check the potential, in the quadrant $f^2>1$, $s<0$ the replacements send it to zero. In the other quadrants each replacement decreases or preserves the two scalar contributions in (6). The minimizing critical solution has $f=0$ at the axis and $f\to1$ at infinity. For a smooth branch so obtained the multiplication potential is bounded and differs from one only by a function tending to zero at infinity. The exterior continuum of $L_f$ starts at one. A radial test wave equal to one on a core disk and falling logarithmically to zero at a large outer radius has gradient cost tending to zero and a fixed strictly negative core expectation of $f^2-1$. A negative threshold-subtracted Rayleigh quotient gives a discrete eigenvalue below one. Positivity of $L_f$ excludes an actual zero mode, and the lowest eigenfunction can be chosen strictly positive. The logarithmic trial is sufficient for existence; the measured frequency below uses a solution of the radial equation.

This argument compares with the *vector* bulk threshold. The scalar masses in the same vacuum must be tested separately. With $q=H/\sqrt2$, $\tilde q=H^*/\sqrt2$ on the real slice, write $f=1+h$ near the vacuum. The quadratic potential in photon units is $(h+ws)^2+s^2$. The two eigenvalues give
$$
     m_{\rm bulk,\pm}=m_\gamma\,[\sqrt{1+w^2/4}\pm w/2].          \tag{11}
$$
The other real components supply the conjugate chiral pair and the scalar in the massive vector multiplet. One gauge phase is removed. Unbroken vacuum $\mathcal N=1$ matches the fermions to these same bulk masses. There are eight bosonic physical polarizations and eight fermionic ones. Thus for small positive $w$ the lightest *one-particle* bulk threshold of the retained fields is $m_{\rm bulk,-}$, not the photon. The massive charged degrees of the ultraviolet theory are parametrically heavier in this patch. A comparison with such heavy thresholds cannot replace this check of the light vacuum spectrum.

At criticality the lowest radial frequency is $\nu_0=0.8817442$ and its separation from the bulk mass is about $0.11826m_\gamma$. Quantities measured in photon units extend continuously to this limiting problem as the centered background is deformed. In the canonical action $f$ is even in $w$, so $\nu_b=\nu_0+O(w^2)$. By contrast the light chiral mass has a negative linear slope. There is an open interval at weak breaking in which the odd pole lies below *all* one-particle bulk masses. At $w=0.001$, for example, solving (6) and (10) gives $m_b/m_\gamma=0.8817440$, while (11) gives $m_{\rm bulk,-}/m_\gamma=0.9995001$. The core momentum is about $0.01131|\Lambda|$ by (4). This last check identifies a member with a hierarchy between core and microscopic scales; the same ordering persists at parametrically smaller $w$. This is a tree-level test supported by a finite limiting gap. It gives neither an exact resonance energy at the quoted digits in the interacting four-dimensional theory nor a finite numerical upper bound on the neglected loop correction.

At larger effective deformations a perfectly valid solution of the SQED equations can describe a different ordering. The earlier working calculation placed $m_b=m_{\rm bulk,-}$ at $w\simeq0.27974$, after which (10) continued to bind relative to the photon continuum. In (4) that value corresponds to $m_\gamma/|\Lambda|>3$. There is no corresponding controlled critical deformation of the microscopic electric string. Even in constant-coupling SQED, an invariant linear vector sector and a lighter scalar bulk particle do not imply a nonzero linear decay amplitude into that scalar; parity and angular momentum still apply. This is a concrete instance of two independently required checks, the spectral threshold of the operator being solved and the validity of the reduction from the parent.

## 6. An insertion and a residue for the electric flux sector

The local matrix element has a direction in the spectrum, which is relevant if another level becomes degenerate with the vector. Define
$$
 \mathcal I=\int d^2x\,B^c\psi,\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{g\mathcal I}{m_\gamma}=2\pi\int_0^\infty a' u\,d\rho.
                                                                    \tag{12}
$$
The Maxwell equation implies $b'=-f^2(1-a)/\rho$ with $b=a'/\rho$. The positive unit solution has $0\le a\le1$. A negative minimum or positive overshoot of $1-a$ is excluded by its regular linear equation and its axis and exterior values. The magnetic field has one sign, and the nodeless radial ground state gives $\mathcal I>0$. The observable $\int F^c_{12}F^c_{03}\,d^2x$ has at linear order a matrix element proportional to $m_b\mathcal I$ for a canonical oscillator at rest. Up to an orientation it is part of the integral over the transverse plane of $F^c\wedge F^c$, linearized about this background with the specified mode. Charge reversal of the dual convention changes the signs of both factors and preserves the insertion. The same matrix element can in principle be selected by displacing the tube with two waves in a fixed polarization, which does not require a four-dimensional *local* ultraviolet interpolating operator for the dual field.

To see how the source couples to translations, pull the static connection back with $x^i-X^i(t,z)$. Two normal displacements supply a sheet field strength proportional to the static magnetic flux times $\epsilon_{ij}\partial_a X^i\partial_bX^j$. Expanding the Maxwell action to one power of the internal field strength $G_{ab}$ gives the cubic term $-\mathcal I G^{ab}\epsilon_{ij}\partial_a X^i\partial_bX^j/2$. Transporting the neutral scalar along with the charged core is essential for the kinetic energy of $X$ at finite breaking, but adds no term linear in $G$. A boost of the straight stationary solution gives $\sigma$ for the norm of each displacement. With $y^i=\sqrt\sigma X^i$, the squared center-of-mass energy of counterpropagating phonons is $s_{\rm cm}=4E_R E_L$. At $s_{\rm cm}=m_b^2$ the magnitude of the ordered perpendicular two-wave amplitude is $\mathcal I m_b^3/(2\sigma)$. Interchanging the polarization labels changes its sign. This factor of the *physical* mass follows from using the sheet electric field of a normalized Proca particle rather than a scalar coordinate with an arbitrary kinetic coefficient.

Denote by $Q_b$ the dimensionless on-shell coefficient of $\vartheta\varepsilon^{ab}\epsilon_{ij}K^i_{ac}K^{jc}_{b}$, where $K^i$ is the second fundamental form and $\vartheta$ the canonical odd scalar. Matching at the massive pole gives
$$
 |Q_b|=\mathcal I/m_b,\quad
 R_A=\frac{\mathcal I^2m_b^6}{2\sigma^2},\quad
 \mathcal C=\frac{|Q_b|m_b}{\sqrt\sigma}=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\pi\tau}} .
                                                                    \tag{13}
$$
Here $R_A$ is the positive residue for the normalized antisymmetric ordered pair. The orientation-independent positivity refers to the product of amplitudes in a diagonal scattering channel; individual ordered amplitudes retain their relative minus sign. At the critical normalization $g|Q_b|=1.20101$, $m_b/\sqrt\sigma=0.497471g$ and $\mathcal C(0)=0.597469$. These dimensionless vortex data follow from the magnetic/string calculation of [6]. Equation (13) now concerns the flux fixed by a fundamental *electric* source through its Dirac pairing. It assigns the source and the validity range and makes no claim that an arbitrary microscopic topological density has the same normalization.

There are different choices of the regular analytic terms in an effective worldsheet action. A field redefinition can add vertices proportional to $s_{\rm cm}-m_b^2$ and exchange them for contact interactions. The factorizable residue in (13) is invariant under such a change provided the four-dimensional translation and mode have the same normalized one-particle legs. This is one reason to use $\mathcal C$ as the comparison rather than assigning meaning to the sign and absolute value of a fitted off-shell curvature operator by themselves. The sign of a single real massive eigenfield also can be reversed. The vanishing of the odd contribution to the transverse trace, and the relative sign of two exchanged transverse indices, are independent tests that survive these choices.

The full leading bosonic action has other singlet degrees of freedom with the same frequency at criticality. Identifying a single one of them in a traced Wilson loop requires a projector which does not rely on gauge-variant components. An external-state construction can place two widely separated wave packets on the central segment of a long string created by fixed ends, send them toward each other, and use the trace or antisymmetric combination under interchange of their normal polarizations as source and detector. At leading order mixing through the cubic vertex gives the same pole residues as on the periodic sheet in the limit of remote ends. The endpoint forces and the displacement normalization are part of the experiment, and they cannot simply be read from a local operator on empty space. In the limit $R\to\infty$ one must still specify the distance to endpoints and the times of the packets so the endpoint reflection does not return during the interaction. Equivalently a long periodic tube of fixed center flux provides translation eigenstates with no ends. A relation between these two probes assumes locality on the scale of the packet and separation from their extra boundary spectra.

For orientation on the size of the first odd response, put $x=w^2$ and solve the linearized static equations for $f_2=\partial_x f|_0$ and $a_2=\partial_x a|_0$. The source in the first radial equation includes $f_0(s_1+s_1^2)$ with $s_1=(1-a_0)/2$; the linearized Maxwell equation has term $-2 f_0(1-a_0)f_2$. Differentiating the normalization of (10) yields $\langle u_0,u_2\rangle=0$. Hence
$$
 \partial_x\nu_b^2|_0=\langle u_0,2 f_0 f_2 u_0\rangle,\qquad
 (L_{f_0}-\nu_0^2)u_2=(\partial_x\nu_b^2|_0-2f_0 f_2)u_0 ,
                                                                    \tag{14}
$$
with $\langle v,u\rangle=2\pi\int \rho v^*u\,d\rho$. Both the magnetic field and its test wave contribute to $\partial_x\alpha|_0=2\pi\int(a'_2u_0+a'_0u_2)d\rho$. For reference the previous collocation and constrained radial response give
$$
 \nu_b=0.8817442-0.15343w^2+O(w^4),\qquad
 \mathcal C=0.5974688+0.0146w^2+O(w^4).
$$
The comparatively small coefficient in $\mathcal C$ contains opposite contributions from the magnetic overlap and the tension. An estimate for the full microscopic soft coefficient would have to include terms outside the fixed coupling action. The numerical solution of (14) supplies the reference against which the second odd state is compared in §7; deducing the separation of two almost degenerate poles by subtracting results calculated in *different* schemes would be unreliable. ## 7. A second odd state and its constant-coupling displacement

Why does the leading vector residue fail to specify all the microscopic odd pole data? At zero deformation the imaginary part of $Z$ also satisfies (10). The equality of its critical mass with that of $b_a$ survives the change from unspecified Abelian probes to a genuine fundamental Wilson sector. Reflection parity matters for the possibility of subsequent mixing. In our rephased variables define $I(q,\tilde q,Z)=(\tilde q^*,q^*,Z^*)$, with $A$ fixed. Define $C_D(q,\tilde q,A,Z)=(\tilde q,q,-A,Z)$. Within the leading action (3) each internal transformation is a symmetry. A longitudinal reversal extending to $r_z I$ and a transverse reversal extending to $r_2 C_D I$ preserve the chosen straight unit string up to a harmless phase convention. The pullback $r_2$ reverses the normal-plane orientation, and $C_D$ reverses the flux a second time. The physical $F^c_{03}$ and an imaginary neutral fluctuation both have negative signs under these two operations. Thus they are rotation scalars with the same two odd parities. The constant-coupling symmetry $I$ alone separates their quadratic problems. It need not remain a symmetry of derivative interactions in the true local gauge theory, even when the two space-time reflection symmetries survive.

We first keep (3) exactly and compute the separation that is present without a varying gauge kinetic function. Write
$$
 q=(H+J)/\sqrt2,\quad \tilde q^*=(H-J)/\sqrt2,\quad
 J=v e^{i\theta}(j_R+i j_I),\qquad Z/(gv)=s+i n_I
$$
in the relevant rotation-singlet modes. Both $H$ and $J$ carry charge $+1$, and $J=0$ on the real static branch. In particular $J$ is not another charged condensate. Expanding $q\tilde q=(|H|^2-|J|^2+JH^*-H J^*)/2$ gives an imaginary F-term proportional to $f j_I+w n_I$. The real D-term depends to first order on $f j_R$. Thus the imaginary charged phase mixes with the imaginary neutral displacement; its real partner is a separate block. This decomposition keeps track of the fields omitted by a description with a single charged modulus and a gauge field. The inherited Gauss constraint does not remove $j_I$: the total gauge phase of the nonzero condensate is a variation in $H$, whereas $J$ is an orthogonal charged field with zero background. Its linear sheet current vanishes in these coordinates.

With the same radial kinetic measure for the two components, the squared-mass operator in the $(j_I,n_I)$ sector is
$$
 \mathcal L_I(w)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  -\Delta_\rho+(1-a)^2/\rho^2+(1+f^2)/2-ws+s^2 & wf\\
  wf & L_f+w^2
 \end{pmatrix}.                                                      \tag{15}
$$
At the axis $j_I=O(\rho)$, $n_I'=0$. Both are required to decay in a discrete state. For example the negative contribution $-ws$ in the charged diagonal can be traced to $-|J|^2$ in $q\tilde q$ contracted with the nonzero real F-term. The positive $s^2$ comes from the neutral scalar mass of the charged kinetic multiplet. Leaving out either is inconsistent even though both begin at order $w^2$. At infinity (15) has the same vacuum chiral thresholds as (7) of [1], or (11) here. In particular its lighter pole at finite breaking in vacuum is not a new copy of a massless sheet translation.

At the critical point set $E=\partial_\rho-(1-a_0)/\rho$. Integration by parts with the regular boundary data gives for the charged diagonal
$$
 K_J=-\Delta_\rho+(1-a_0)^2/\rho^2+(1+f_0^2)/2=1+E^\dagger E .
$$
This identity uses $a'_0/\rho=(1-f_0^2)/2$. Thus $K_J\ge1$. The legitimate potential well for $n_I$ has eigenvalue $\lambda_0<1$, while the charged $j_I$ block has no eigenfunction at that frequency. Unlike the *real* scalar sector in the 1992 doublet, the linear term $wf$ couples this normalized neutral wave to a separated sector. It produces a first-order wave admixture and a *quadratic* mass change. Confusing these two charged fields would incorrectly give a duplicate of the linear trace splitting in the odd sector.

Let $\lambda_b(w)=\nu_b(w)^2$ in (10) and $\lambda_n(w)$ be the eigenvalue of (15) which equals $\lambda_0$ at $w=0$. The first row of (15) determines $j_I=-w(K_J-\lambda_0)^{-1}(f_0u_0)+O(w^3)$ in the leading normalized neutral eigenvector. This uses a choice of real phase with $n_I=u_0+O(w^2)$; its small normalization change enters the eigenvalue only at higher order. Schur elimination of the charged block in the neutral eigenvalue equation gives
$$
 \lambda_n(w)-\lambda_b(w)
  =w^2(1-\Xi_J)+O(w^4),\qquad
 \Xi_J=\langle f_0u_0,(K_J-\lambda_0)^{-1} f_0u_0\rangle .         \tag{16}
$$
The static background change of $f$ shifts both diagonals by the same expectation from (14). It cancels in the difference, which can consequently be calculated from the *critical* profiles alone. The positive complement resolvent in (16) is bounded by $1/(1-\lambda_0)$. It ensures a finite susceptibility but does not force $\Xi_J$ to be smaller than one. The direct $+w^2$ and the virtual charged contribution can compete. This is a different measurement of the radial geometry from $\mathcal I$. It cannot be inferred from $\nu_0$ or from the equality of the trace and odd residues at criticality.

We obtain $\Xi_J\simeq1.57746$ for the unit tube. Thus the mostly neutral odd state lies slightly *below* the unmixed longitudinal vector in the constant-coupling quadratic problem. Their leading squared-mass difference is $-0.57746 w^2m_\gamma^2$. The lighter state is dark to two translations in this particular bosonic action by $I$, while the vector coupling in (13) is nonzero. The ordering of the two eigenvalues therefore differs from the ordering one would infer by looking for the first significant odd line in a source that couples only to the vector. Once a physical operator reduces that internal symmetry, even a weak interaction can reveal both. The bulk threshold from (11) remains a finite distance above the pair at sufficiently small $w$.

## 8. What the magnetic kinetic function can mix

The special geometry gives a physical example of a term that distinguishes the separate internal symmetry of the constant-coupling calculation from the two space-time reflections. In the normalization of [2] the topological part of the dual gauge action can be written, up to a common orientation sign, as
$$
       \frac{\mathrm{Re}\,\widetilde\tau_D(Z)}{16\pi}
           F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu},
       \qquad \widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}.
                                                                    \tag{17}
$$
A constant coefficient integrates to a boundary term on a closed smooth gauge configuration. Its *derivative* with respect to a dynamical fluctuation multiplies a different operator. On a background with $F_{12}\ne0$, the part of $F\widetilde F$ linear in a longitudinal electric fluctuation is proportional to $F_{12}\delta F_{03}$. Thus (17) can give a quadratic fluctuation term bilinear in an imaginary neutral field and the one-dimensional electric polarization. It preserves the two physical odd parities identified above. Inverting the full field to a theory without explicit monopoles before expanding (17) would make the coefficient spuriously singular at the vacuum.

For completeness we separate the universal logarithm from one convention for its finite analytic part. Let $k=d/(2+d)$ with $d$ as in (2). Use the parameter convention $K(k)=\int_0^{\pi/2}(1-k\sin^2 t)^{-1/2}dt$ for the complete elliptic integral. The derivatives of the two periods are periods of the holomorphic differential of the curve. Mapping its three finite roots to an elliptic modulus gives $\tau_D=iK(1-k)/K(k)$ on the monopole-side real branch, up to cycle choice and a real constant topological shift. At $d\downarrow0$ this convention has positive imaginary logarithm. Expanding $K(k)=\frac\pi2(1+k/4+O(k^2))$ and the logarithmic solution at the other endpoint gives
$$
 \frac{\pi K(1-k)}{K(k)}=\log(16/k)-k/2+O(k^2).
$$
One way to check the first subleading coefficient is to keep the two terms $L+(k/4)(L-1)$ in $K(1-k)$, $L=\log(4/\sqrt{k})$, before dividing by the regular period. The $kL$ terms cancel. Expressing $k$ in terms of $d$ then gives $\log(32/d)+d/4+O(d^2)$. The further change from $d$ to $a_D$ in (2) affects the coefficient at this same order: the logarithm contributes $-(-2i a_D/\Lambda)/16$. Combining terms gives
$$
 \tau_D=\frac{i}{\pi}
   \left[\log\frac{16i\Lambda}{a_D}-\frac{3i a_D}{8\Lambda}
                    +O(a_D^2/\Lambda^2)\right].                       \tag{18}
$$
This expansion uses the Coulomb theory with the monopole integrated out. Its singularity is required by the unit monopole monodromy. The linear coefficient would be different if the Taylor correction in the coordinate change were dropped. It is the same normalization issue as in the soft superpotential, now appearing in a logarithm rather than a power series.

To define the local comparison, subtract the logarithm of a charge-one hypermultiplet of mass $\sqrt2|a_D|$ at a matching scale $M_{\rm match}$. Adopt a subtraction with no additional analytic term linear in $a_D$. Then the constant imaginary part of $\widetilde\tau_D$ defines $4\pi/g^2$; the precise finite constant inside its logarithm depends on the matching convention. The specified regular linear coefficient is $3/(8\pi\Lambda)$ in the period coordinate. Write $Z=g(\varphi_R+i\varphi_I)/\sqrt2$, where $\varphi_R,\varphi_I$ are four-dimensional real canonical fields. For real positive $\mu,\Lambda$ one can take $a_D=-i Z$ and rephase the monopoles to obtain (3). Consequently $|\partial_{\varphi_I}\mathrm{Re}\,\widetilde\tau_D|=3g/(8\sqrt2\pi|\Lambda|)$. This identifies the fluctuation with the correct sign under the physical reflections. Keeping a different analytic counterterm would require matching the rest of the action and the interpolating operators in the same scheme.

Let the neutral component of the normalized odd scalar eigenmode be $m_\gamma u_n(\rho)n(t,z)$ in $\varphi_I$. The charged admixture in (15) is included in its unit norm. Write the normalized vector profile as $m_\gamma u_b(\rho)b_a(t,z)$. In (17) there is a factor $g^2$ converting the two integer-charge field strengths to canonical ones. The contraction of the background and the fluctuating field gives a factor four, with a sign fixed by space-time orientation. Thus in magnitude the projected term is $h\,n G_{03}$ with
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 |h|&=\frac{g^2}{4\pi}|\partial_{\varphi_I}\mathrm{Re}\,\widetilde\tau_D|
           \left|\int d^2x\,B^c\,(m_\gamma u_b)(m_\gamma u_n)\right|,\\
 \frac{|h|}{m_\gamma}&=\frac{3g^2}{32\sqrt2\pi^2}
       \frac{m_\gamma}{|\Lambda|} J_{bn}
       =\frac{3g^2}{4\pi^2}w J_{bn},
 \qquad J_{bn}=\left|2\pi\int_0^\infty a' u_b u_n\,d\rho\right| .
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{19}
$$
Here $u_n$ need not have unit norm by itself at finite $w$ because its charged partner carries some of the probability. The sign of $h$ can be reversed by a phase of one normalized eigenstate. The normalization and the power of $g$ in (19) instead have observable consequences. For the critical ground state the two waves agree and $J_{bn}\to J_B\simeq0.2161$. The integral is positive for a nodeless wave and fixed-sign flux, so there is no symmetry cancellation setting this operator contribution to zero. This computation uses only the specified Wilsonian term and the independently normalized radial profiles. It gives a sensitivity of the nearly degenerate sector to a matched interaction. It is not a determination of the complete nonprotected splitting of the quantum electric string.

The term in (19) illustrates a nonuniform approximation. Put $\ell=4\pi^2/g^2$ at the light scale. In leading-log normalization $\ell$ grows as $\log(c|\Lambda|/m_\gamma)$ for a finite matching constant $c$. The constant-action diagonal separation in squared-mass units is approximately $0.57746 w^2$. The off-diagonal in the inverse propagator at rest, in the same units, has size $|h|\nu_0/m_\gamma\simeq3J_B\nu_0 w/\ell$. It follows that discarding analytic gauge-kinetic terms can be excellent for the mass of the cluster relative to the bulk threshold and poor for the eigenvectors *inside* that cluster: their ratio scales as $1/(w\ell)$. Along the microscopic trajectory this ratio increases without bound as $w\to0$. The conclusion is about the sensitivity to this operator. Loop corrections to diagonal masses or vertices, and other terms allowed in the low-energy action, can have different sizes and require their own matching. An off-diagonal estimate alone does not justify ignoring them because it exceeds one particular tree-level difference.

The same inequality can be expressed in terms of core momenta. The dimensionless local gradients are of order one in photon units and of order $m_\gamma/|\Lambda|$ in microscopic units. Operators suppressed by the heavy scale can therefore give small absolute shifts compared with the finite bulk gap. To predict how the strength divides between two lines separated only by $O(w^2)$ one needs an accuracy set by that much smaller difference. This is the mechanism by which a more inclusive observable can remain controlled when a nominally small correction alters the identity of an individual state. The next section states precisely which matrix elements that argument concerns.

## 9. The projected odd response and its range of validity

Use $m_b^2=m_\gamma^2\lambda_b$ and $m_n^2=m_\gamma^2\lambda_n$ from the constant-coupling radial problem as the diagonal entries. To isolate the effect of (19), keep just this extra projected interaction and retain the two isolated modes. At rest the quadratic Lagrangian has canonical kinetic and mass terms for $b=b_3,n$ and a term $h n\dot b$. For real fields and a real coefficient the equations are $\ddot b+m_b^2 b+h\dot n=0$, $\ddot n+m_n^2 n-h\dot b=0$. The coupling is linear in frequency. Inserting a constant off-diagonal squared mass in its place at all frequencies would give different residues and the wrong spectral sum. The conjugate momentum of $b$ is $\dot b+h n$; the Hamiltonian is a sum of positive squares for $m_b,m_n>0$. This checks that the derivative mixing has not introduced a negative-norm particle.

Writing $s=E^2$ only in the following propagator equations, the two poles and the $bb$ response obey
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 (s-s_-)(s-s_+)&=(s-m_b^2)(s-m_n^2)-h^2s,\
 G_{bb}(s)&=\frac{s-m_n^2}{(s-s_-)(s-s_+)}
             =\frac{Z_-}{s-s_-}+\frac{Z_+}{s-s_+} .
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{20}
$$
Thus $s_-s_+=m_b^2m_n^2$, $s_-+s_+=m_b^2+m_n^2+h^2$, and
$$
 Z_- =\frac{m_n^2-s_-}{s_+-s_-},\quad
 Z_+=\frac{s_+-m_n^2}{s_+-s_-},\qquad Z_-+Z_+=1 .                 \tag{21}
$$
For $h\ne0$ the secular polynomial evaluated at $m_n^2$ is $-h^2m_n^2<0$, which places $m_n^2$ between the two positive roots. Each $Z$ is therefore positive. The vector weight is transported among the two positive poles as the mixing is changed. The linearized flux insertion has an extra factor of frequency from $G_{03}$, so its two spectral residues include $s_- Z_-$ and $s_+ Z_+$ as well as the magnetic overlap squared. In the degenerate limit their mass factors agree. Identifying the $Z$ themselves with scattering cross sections at separated masses would omit the derivative vertices. Equations (20)–(21) concern a specified vector probe and a projected calculation.

If $m_b=m_n=m_*$, the roots in mass units are $\sqrt{m_*^2+h^2/4}\pm|h|/2$. In that limit each $Z$ tends to one half as $h\to0$ through nonzero values. At $h=0$ with nonzero detuning there is one vector pole; the other has zero weight in $G_{bb}$. The order of these limits matters just as it did for the trace source in the 1992 theory, but the origin of the mixing and the source are different. The zero in (20) is fixed at $s=m_n^2$ because of the numerator. This statement is specific to the two-mode propagator. A complete defect response may also have direct couplings to $n$, analytic backgrounds or extra cuts that move or fill that zero.

An illustrative calculation fixes the finite logarithmic convention by $\ell=\log(|\Lambda|/m_\gamma)$ in the coefficient of (19). We use it only for the two smallest tabulated values with $m_\gamma/|\Lambda|<0.1$. At $w=0.001$, $\ell=4.482$, and the isolated operator gives $|h|/m_\gamma=1.447\times10^{-4}$. The two rest masses in photon units become $0.881672$ and $0.881816$. The vector-field weights from (21) are $0.49883$ and $0.50117$. In the same finite radial discretization with $h=0$, the mostly neutral level lies lower, but its vector weight is zero. Both calculations place the entire cluster well below the one-particle bulk threshold near $0.99950$. Hence a correction of this size can rearrange the observable internal structure without threatening transverse binding. The numerical choices illustrate mechanism and normalization; taking the unknown finite matching and loop shifts as zero in a real measured spectrum would be unjustified. There is no attempt to fit a physical charge to attain equal observed weights.

In the constant-coupling action, the signs under the separate operation $I$ exclude a direct translation source for $n_I$. In a more general effective action this special selection may disappear. Consider a compact interval enclosing the critical localized odd eigenvalue but ending a fixed distance below the one-particle bulk threshold in scaled mass units. For the *linear transverse operator*, a sufficiently small relative perturbation in the low-energy problem preserves its isolated spectral projection. The norm of the flux source on that projection is a continuous function, nonzero at zero deformation. This implies nonzero total subthreshold odd weight. It does not require the individual eigenvectors of a degenerate multiplet to depend continuously on every possible choice of perturbation direction. It is the appropriate level of the local bound-state argument when entering a microscopic completion. The reduction to a spectral projector presupposes a small perturbation of the low-energy operator, including the kinetic norm. A bound on higher-derivative terms used far outside the energy range of the effective theory is not part of that argument.

The full interacting quantum Hamiltonian on an infinite sheet has continua from massless translations and, in the supersymmetric problem, localized fermions. The above isolated projector cannot simply be transferred to that Hamiltonian in an infinite-volume Hilbert-space norm. Its physical meaning in the weakly coupled theory is a leading semiclassical contribution to a parity-resolved scattering spectral density below the bulk particle threshold. Higher-order self-energies turn the approximation of an isolated transverse level into a resonance description. A rigorous statement about exact quantum poles on specified sheets of the energy plane would require a matching and analytic control of those self-energies, including their massless cuts. The microscopic charge and the leading string tension do not perform that calculation.

## 10. How well can the even corollary be resolved?

There is a second scale comparison involving the real trace pair. Define the physical mass gap between its localized members at tree level by $\Delta m_T=m_{T,+}-m_{T,-}$. Let $\Gamma_S$ denote the partial width into translations of the *bright critical trace direction* $S$, with all other channels omitted. By [1, Eq. (32)] and the odd matching [6], one obtains
$$
 \Delta m_T/m_\gamma=w+O(w^2),\qquad
 \Gamma_S/m_\gamma=0.01104\,g^2\nu_0
$$
in the corresponding leading expansions. The second equation fixes the limiting normalization of the translation channel, measured on the critical branch. It is not a total width of a microscopic particle. Comparing these two nonzero scales on the matching trajectory gives
$$
 \frac{\Delta m_T}{\Gamma_S}\ \sim \frac{w}{0.01104\nu_0 g^2}
 \ \propto\ \frac{\sqrt{|\mu/\Lambda|}}{g}
 \ \longrightarrow 0 .                                             \tag{22}
$$
Thus a microscopic limit that becomes parametrically weakly coupled also approaches the critical degeneracy parametrically fast. The two inequalities $\Gamma_S\ll m_\gamma$ and $\Gamma_S\ll\Delta m_T$ are not equivalent. For independent parameters in local SQED it is easy to choose $g$ so small that both hold. The Seiberg–Witten trajectory fixes the relation in (22). Small radiative mass shifts or other couplings can matter on the scale of $\Delta m_T$ even when they are negligible on the scale that isolates the cluster from the bulk.

For example choose $\ell=\log(|\Lambda|/m_\gamma)=4\pi^2/g^2$ solely to show the scale dependence. Combining this illustrative leading-log choice with (4) gives $w=e^{-\ell}/(8\sqrt2)$ and $g=2\pi/\sqrt\ell$. At $\ell=10$, the translation-only ratio estimated from (22) is about $1.04\times10^{-4}$. At $\ell=20$ it is $9.48\times10^{-9}$. The absolute numerical values depend on the matching convention and use no pole shift beyond the local tree action. Their extremely rapid decrease follows from the exponential dependence of the soft parameter on $\ell$ and the algebraic dependence of the coupling. An order-one change in the finite constant in the logarithm changes the finite examples without changing this parametric distinction. The existence of an isolated *classical transverse* mode therefore cannot by itself be used to claim two visibly separated lines in a quantum electric-string experiment at asymptotically tiny adjoint mass.

One can make the interpretation more explicit with a deliberately restricted two-state calculation. In the critical basis $(S,N)$, neglect differential real loop shifts and all decay channels except the common translation channel. Approximate the self-energy in its narrow energy interval by its critical on-shell imaginary part with the dispersive shift absorbed into the common reference mass. Let $d=\Delta m_T/2$ at tree level. The two complex energies relative to that reference then solve
$$
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 E+i\Gamma_S/2 & -d\\ -d & E
 \end{pmatrix}=0,\qquad
 E=-i\Gamma_S/4\ \pm\sqrt{d^2-\Gamma_S^2/16}.                        \tag{23}
$$
The placement of the imaginary term in just one entry follows from the critical source vector. A two-phonon intermediate state supplies an outer product of the two production amplitudes. At $w=0$ that product has rank one. Diagonalizing just the real mass matrix and then assigning *independent* half-widths misses the off-diagonal absorptive part in the mass eigenbasis. Equation (23) is an elementary resummation with specified inputs, useful because it exposes a noncommutation of two physical limits.

For $|d|\gg\Gamma_S/4$ the two lines have separated real energies and each has width approximately $\Gamma_S/2$. This is the regime in which each tree residue can be read as a separate partial width at leading order in $g$. For $|d|\ll\Gamma_S/4$, the two-state model has a state of width $4d^2/\Gamma_S$ to leading order and another of width $\Gamma_S-4d^2/\Gamma_S$. This follows by expanding the square root before multiplying the imaginary energies by $-2$. The narrow width comes from the small amplitude to reach the one decaying direction. It is a *conditional* resolution diagnostic. In the actual theory, differential real shifts, energy dependence of loop kernels, a direct neutral two-phonon amplitude away from criticality and especially independent fermionic or other decay channels can change this conclusion. Adding a positive width of the neutral direction, for example, removes the vanishing-width limit at $d=0$. No value for such an additional width has been derived here.

The periodic experiment of [1] has a related finite-volume scale which involves one fewer power of $g$. At circumference $L_j=4\pi j/m_*$ a right-left pair of translation momenta $\pm2\pi j/L_j$ is degenerate at leading order with the critical particle at rest. The matrix element of the normalized pair and $S$ is $v_S$, with $2|v_S|/m_*=0.08385g/\sqrt j$ for the specified pair at the unit vortex. By contrast the mixing between the two critical internal directions is $d\simeq w m_\gamma/2$. Thus at fixed $j$ the bare trace pair of particles cannot be treated as independent avoided crossings when $w/g\to0$. The appropriate leading finite-dimensional block *if the three states are isolated* is
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}0&d&v_S\\ d&0&0\\ v_S&0&\Delta\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Delta=4\pi j/L-m_* .                                       \tag{24}
$$
At the central length it has energies $0,\pm\sqrt{d^2+v_S^2}$ relative to $m_*$. The dependence on the soft splitting is principally through a second-order change of these eigenvalues when $|v_S|\gg|d|$. The eigenvector at the middle energy changes its phonon content at first order in $d/v_S$. A measurement of source dependence can therefore distinguish situations whose central eigenvalues differ by less than their individual fitting uncertainties. Formula (24) uses a *single specified pair*. At large $j$ the other partitions of the same free phonon energies require a degenerate calculation of their own.

This identifies an obstruction to a common attempted remedy for unresolved levels. Reducing $|v_S|$ by going to much longer strings at successive crossings would formally require $j\gg(g/w)^2$ at fixed action to achieve isolated two-state crossings. Along the microscopic weak-breaking trajectory this lower bound grows as $|\Lambda/\mu|$. At such high mode indices the free string has many partitions at the same chiral excitation level. One cannot obtain a pair of clean avoided crossings by substituting the large $j$ into a first-level formula. An additional projection or an actual interacting multi-level calculation would be needed. The infinite-volume and finite-volume resolution issues have different origins: the former involves couplings to continua and widths, the latter involves level normalization and the density of near-degenerate finite-string states.

Fermion counting gives another reason for stating the experiment conditionally. At the limiting BPS vortex in the leading extended Abelian theory, four of the eight real supercharges are broken by a straight half-BPS solution. Acting on it gives fermion variations localized on its fields. At nonzero adjoint mass the microscopic vacuum has four real supercharges, and the non-BPS string breaks them all. The corresponding four real fermionic variations on an infinite string are localized because the fields approach a supersymmetric vacuum exponentially. Boundary conditions and a spin structure determine how these modes occur in a finite periodic spectrum. They can provide additional channels on the infinite sheet. We have not calculated their cubic couplings or diagonalized their multi-particle states at the lengths of (24). Consequently a two-phonon partial width is controlled in its channel, whereas a total line shape or a finite-volume energy gap in the full supersymmetric theory has further physical inputs. The existence of fermion zero modes is compatible with a gapped *bulk* vacuum. Section 11 derives their finite-length consequence for the two spin structures.

## 11. Spin structures and the massless fermions of the unit string

The four fermion variations at the end of §10 have a consequence that needs neither their cubic couplings nor the massive line shapes. QCDS-86/1 [7] derived the energy difference between periodic and antiperiodic fermions on a closed tube for a free fermion of fixed mass and left open which microscopic theory realizes both sectors with common local matching data (its prediction P21). The unit electric string supplies such a theory. We first derive the contribution of the four variations, which follows from the exact supersymmetry alone. We then state what the *total* coefficient additionally requires: a count of the full gapless fermion kernel. We prove the bosonic half of that count, reduce the fermionic half to one stated hypothesis, and control the deformation away from the BPS point. Units are $\hbar=c=1$ and lengths are in $m_\gamma^{-1}$ unless shown.

### 11.1 The comparison

Fix the adjoint mass $\mu\ne0$, hence $w>0$, and the microscopic couplings. Wrap a unit electric-center-flux tube around a noncontractible *spatial* circle of circumference $L$; a winding fundamental Wilson operator prepares the odd center sector, which adjoint fields cannot screen. Keep all bosons periodic around the circle. Make **all microscopic fermions** either periodic (R) or antiperiodic (NS) around it. These are two spin structures of the same four-dimensional theory; no worldsheet fermion is added and no sum over spin structures is taken. Euclidean time is sent to infinity in each energy. For each spin structure subtract its own zero-flux vacuum,
$$
\mathcal E_\alpha(L)=E_{{\rm odd\ flux},\alpha}(L)-E_{{\rm zero\ flux},\alpha}(L),\qquad \alpha=R,NS, \tag{25}
$$
first in a transverse box of area $A$ and then with $A\to\infty$. Subtracting an R vacuum from an NS flux state would retain a term proportional to $A$, the bulk spin-structure energy, and would not measure a string property.

The bulk is gapped at fixed $\mu\ne0$. Changing the fermion boundary condition therefore leaves the infinite-line tension and local matching coefficients unchanged up to winding terms of order $e^{-m_{\rm bulk}L}$. A localized massive field of mass $m$ contributes winding terms of order $e^{-mL}$, given for a free field by the formulas of [7]. Only the gapless defect fields give inverse powers of $L$.

### 11.2 Four Goldstinos and their transverse normalization

At $w>0$ the vacuum keeps four real $\mathcal N=1$ supercharges and the non-BPS string breaks all four. A broken variation $\delta_\epsilon\Psi$ of the bosonic solution satisfies the linearized fermion equation, $\mathscr D_\perp\,\delta_\epsilon\Psi=0$, and decays exponentially because the core fields approach a supersymmetric vacuum exponentially. The four modes exist for every $w$ on the branch because of the *remaining exact* supersymmetry; no general persistence of zero eigenvalues is used.

Their profiles are explicit at the BPS point $w=0$, which governs the leading order. Rotate the linear superpotential term of (1) by the $SU(2)_R$ symmetry of the leading Abelian theory into a Fayet–Iliopoulos term; one complex component $\phi$ of the monopole hypermultiplet then winds and its partner vanishes. In the units of this section ($e=1$, $v^2=1/2$, so that $m_\gamma=1$) write $\phi=f(\rho)e^{i\theta}/\sqrt2$ and $A_\theta=a(\rho)/\rho$, with the Bogomolny equations $D_{\bar z}\phi=0$ and $F_{12}=(1-f^2)/2$. The broken variations are proportional to $F_{12}$ in the gaugino and to $D_z\phi$ in the hypermultiplet fermion, times constant spinors. These are the components of the covariant translation
$$
e_T=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T/2}}\Big(\frac{i F_{12}}{\sqrt2},\,D_1\phi\Big),\qquad T=\int d^2x\,\big(F_{12}^2+|D_i\phi|^2\big)=2\pi v^2=\pi , \tag{26}
$$
in the variables $(B,\eta)$ defined below; $\|(iF_{12}/\sqrt2,D_1\phi)\|^2=T/2$ by $D_2\phi=iD_1\phi$. The program `code/spin_structure.py` evaluates $T/\pi=0.9999994$ and the norm ratio $1.0000000$ on the distributed critical profile of [6], with Bogomolny residuals below $4\times10^{-10}$. The Goldstino transverse wavefunctions are $e_T$ and $ie_T$ in each of the two $SU(2)_R$ sectors of §11.4. Their worldsheet coefficients give two real left-moving and two real right-moving components, that is, **two nonchiral Majorana fields**. The residual longitudinal Lorentz symmetry fixes their speed to that of the translations. The normalization fixes the canonical worldsheet fields and cancels from every Casimir coefficient below.

### 11.3 Inherited spin structure and the Goldstino coefficient

The transverse wavefunctions do not depend on the coordinate around a straight winding tube, so the worldsheet coefficients inherit the microscopic boundary condition: $k_n^R=2\pi n/L$ and $k_n^{NS}=2\pi(n+\tfrac12)/L$. The NS condition removes global supersymmetry on the finite cylinder without giving an order-one local Goldstino mass: the infinite-line effective action keeps the nonlinearly realized supersymmetry, whose interactions are irrelevant at the free massless fixed point. The closed-circle contributions are

| Field | R energy | NS energy |
|---|---:|---:|
| One real boson, periodic in both comparisons | $-\pi/(6L)$ | $-\pi/(6L)$ |
| One nonchiral Majorana | $+\pi/(6L)$ | $-\pi/(12L)$ |

Hence the Goldstino part of the spin-structure difference is
$$
\mathcal D_G(L)=2\Big[\frac{\pi}{6L}+\frac{\pi}{12L}\Big]=\frac{\pi}{2L}+o(L^{-1}). \tag{27}
$$
Three checks are independent. (i) Regulated oscillator sums $\sum_n k_n e^{-\varepsilon k_n}$ at $L=1$, with the $1/(2\pi\varepsilon^2)$ area term removed, give finite parts $-0.5235985$ and $+0.2617992$ at $\varepsilon=5\times10^{-4}$, against $2\pi\zeta(-1)=-0.5235988$ and $2\pi\zeta(-1,\tfrac12)=+0.2617994$. (ii) The massive Dirac difference of [7, §5.4], $(4m/\pi)\sum_{w\ {\rm odd}}K_1(wmL)/w$, multiplied by $2L/\pi$, equals $0.93774$, $0.99365$ and $0.99936$ at $mL=10^{-1},10^{-2},10^{-3}$, tending to one. (iii) In the minimal massless content [13,14] $c_L=c_R=3$, two from the translations and one from the two Majoranas. NS ground weights vanish; in R each Majorana has $h=\bar h=1/16$. With $E=2\pi(h+\bar h-c/12)/L$ this gives $-\pi/(2L)$ in NS and zero in R. The R zero is a cancellation of ground-state energies at $c=3$, not an empty massless spectrum. These are closed-string coefficients and are not to be substituted into the open-string coefficients of P18.

The Goldstinos cannot be given the homogeneous dynamically generated mass of the separate Gross–Neveu sector of P22 while this breaking pattern holds, since their nonlinear shift symmetry forbids a mass. Exponential suppression of the *whole* spin-structure difference would need a change of the symmetry realization or of the microscopic deformation.

### 11.4 The gapless kernel at the BPS point

Equation (27) concerns four modes. The total $1/L$ coefficients need the whole gapless content. We treat the BPS point first and the deformed branch in §11.5.

*Bosons.* Use $B=(\delta A_1+i\delta A_2)/\sqrt2$ and $\eta=\delta\phi$, for which the kinetic metric is $\int d^2x\,(|B|^2+|\eta|^2)$. The static energy is a sum of squares plus a topological term [11], so its second variation at the solution is $\|X\|^2+\tfrac12\|Y\|^2$, where $X$ and $Y$ linearize $D_1\phi+iD_2\phi$ and $F_{12}+|\phi|^2-v^2$. Orthogonality to the gauge orbits $(\sqrt2\,\partial_{\bar z}\chi,\,i\chi\phi)$ is the background condition $G=-\partial_i\delta A_i/2-{\rm Im}(\bar\eta\phi)=0$. Combining $Y/\sqrt2$ with $\sqrt2\,iG$ gives a complex-linear deformation operator
$$
D\binom{B}{\eta}=\binom{X}{Z}=\binom{-i\sqrt2\,\phi B+2D_{\bar z}\eta}{-2i\,\partial_z B+\sqrt2\,\bar\phi\,\eta},
\qquad D^\dagger D=H+2G^\dagger G , \tag{28}
$$
with $H$ the Hessian. The program checks $D e_T=0$ on a Cartesian grid: with spacing $0.025$ the residuals are $1.9\times10^{-4}$ and $7.9\times10^{-5}$ of $\max|\eta|$, at the expected $O(h^2)$ level. The gauge orbits form an invariant subspace on which $D^\dagger D$ acts as $-\Delta+f^2$ on $\chi$; the condition $G=0$ removes it and leaves the physical Hessian. Using $D_{\bar z}\phi=0$ the cross terms of the partner operator cancel, and $2F_{12}=1-f^2$ gives
$$
DD^\dagger={\rm diag}\big(1+4D_{\bar z}^\dagger D_{\bar z},\ -\Delta+f^2\big). \tag{29}
$$
Both blocks are strictly positive, so $\ker D^\dagger=0$. The index of $D$ is $2n$ real dimensions for winding $n$ [8], so $\ker D$ is exactly the span of $e_T$ and $ie_T$, the two translations. The nonzero physical spectrum is the spectrum of (29) with one real copy of the second block, the other real copy being the gauge orbits. Below the continuum at one it consists of the eigenvalues of $-\Delta+f^2$. In angular momentum zero the lowest is $\nu_0^2$, with $\nu_0=0.881744$ from [6]; the finite-difference solver of this section gives $0.777464$ at spacing $0.02$. In angular momenta one to four the lowest level lies above one and approaches it as the inverse square of the box: for $\ell=1$, the excess is $8.8\times10^{-3}$, $2.3\times10^{-3}$ and $5.7\times10^{-4}$ for outer radii $40$, $80$ and $160$. These are continuum levels, and there is no further bound state.

The other bosons of the leading $\mathcal N=2$ theory do not add zero modes. The neutral scalar of the dual vector multiplet and the worldsheet components $A_0,A_3$ have the transverse operator $-\Delta+f^2$. The vanishing hypermultiplet component $\tilde\phi$ has the potential $(|\phi|^2+v^2)|\tilde\phi|^2$ and, for its opposite charge, $-D_iD_i=4D_z^\dagger D_z+F_{12}$; its operator is $1+4D_z^\dagger D_z\ge1$. At infinity every block tends to one, the common mass of the massive $\mathcal N=2$ vector multiplet. The gapless bosons of the BPS string are therefore exactly the two translations, and the bosonic transverse gap is $\nu_0 m_\gamma$.

*Fermions.* We use one hypothesis.

> **(H$_F$)** At the BPS point the transverse fermion operator in each $SU(2)_R$ sector, (gaugino, $\psi_\phi$) and (neutral fermion, $\psi_{\tilde\phi}$), is unitarily equivalent to $Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&D^\dagger\\ D&0\end{pmatrix}$, with its two entries carrying opposite worldsheet chirality.

Two facts support (H$_F$). The unbroken supercharges map a bosonic fluctuation $\delta$ into a fermionic one whose transverse components are the linearized Bogomolny combinations, that is $D\delta$. And the exact Goldstino profiles of §11.2 lie in $\ker D$. The index methods for vortex–fermion operators [9,10] count zero modes once the operator is fixed. We have not derived the operator component by component from the microscopic Lagrangian, and the map between nonzero bosonic and fermionic modes is not shown to be bijective here. Given (H$_F$), $\ker Q=\ker D\oplus\ker D^\dagger$ has one complex dimension per sector, four real modes in all, and these are the four Goldstinos. The nonzero fermion eigenvalues are $\pm$ the square roots of the spectrum of (29), so the fermionic gap on the orthogonal complement is $g_\perp=\nu_0 m_\gamma$. The projector onto the kernel is
$$
P_0=\sum_{\rm sectors}\big(|e_T\rangle\langle e_T|+|ie_T\rangle\langle ie_T|\big),\qquad {\rm rank}_{\mathbb R}P_0=4 . \tag{30}
$$

### 11.5 The deformed branch

At $w>0$ write the fermion operator as $Q_w=Q+V_w$. The soft terms enter as bounded multiplication operators: a constant neutral-fermion mass of order $w m_\gamma$ and the $O(w)$ change of the profiles. Hence $Q_w$ is self-adjoint on the domain of $Q$ (Kato–Rellich), and the straight interpolation $Q+tV_w$, $0\le t\le1$, has a common domain. On the circle $|z|=g_\perp/2$ the free resolvent obeys $\|(Q-z)^{-1}\|=2/g_\perp$, so
$$
\|V_w\|<\tfrac12 g_\perp=0.4409\,m_\gamma
\ \Longrightarrow\
P_w=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=g_\perp/2}(z-Q_w)^{-1}dz,\quad {\rm rank}\,P_w={\rm rank}\,P_0=4 , \tag{31}
$$
because the circle stays in the resolvent set along the interpolation and the rank of a Riesz projector is constant on such a path [12]. The exact supersymmetry of §11.2 supplies four zero modes at every $w>0$; they exhaust ${\rm rank}\,P_w$, so no further fermion has $|\lambda|<g_\perp/2$. Since $\|V_w\|=O(w)$, condition (31) holds for $w<w_*$ with some $w_*>0$. We have not evaluated the norm of the profile response, so $w_*$ is not quantified. Along the matching trajectory of §10, $w=e^{-\ell}/(8\sqrt2)$ in the leading-log illustration, about $4\times10^{-6}$ at $\ell=10$. Extra flavours, semilocal size modes, relative positions of higher windings, or a closing gap lie outside this argument.

### 11.6 Total coefficients, length tests and the order of limits

On the minimal unit-string branch, and **conditional on (H$_F$) and $w<w_*$**,
$$
\mathcal E_R(L)=\sigma L+c_R+o(L^{-1}),\qquad
\mathcal E_{NS}(L)=\sigma L+c_{NS}-\frac{\pi}{2L}+o(L^{-1}), \tag{32}
$$
where $c_R-c_{NS}$ is exponentially small in $L$. The difference involves only the spin-sensitive gapless fields. With the four Goldstinos it is (27); a further gapless fermion would add to it, and its absence is the content of (H$_F$) and (31):
$$
\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{2L}{\pi}\big[\mathcal E_R(L)-\mathcal E_{NS}(L)\big]=1,\qquad
\frac{\mathcal E_R(2L)-\mathcal E_{NS}(2L)}{\mathcal E_R(L)-\mathcal E_{NS}(L)}\longrightarrow\frac12 , \tag{33}
$$
The individual energies test the bosons as well. The three-length combination
$$
\widehat a_\alpha(L)=\frac{4L}{3}\big[2\mathcal E_\alpha(L)-3\mathcal E_\alpha(2L)+\mathcal E_\alpha(4L)\big]\longrightarrow a_\alpha \tag{34}
$$
cancels the tension and the offset. For $b$ periodic real gapless bosons and $f$ nonchiral Majoranas with speed one, $a_R=\pi(f-b)/6$ and $a_{NS}=-\pi b/6-\pi f/12$. The minimal branch gives $(a_R,a_{NS})=(0,-\pi/2)$; one extra neutral gapless scalar would give $(-\pi/6,-2\pi/3)$ with the same difference (27). The program evaluates (33) and (34) on model energies that contain a square-root remainder $-a_\alpha^2/(2\sigma L^3)$ with $\sigma=\pi$, localized massive fields at $\nu_0$ and bulk fields at one with balanced illustrative multiplicities. For the minimal content at $L=10,20,40$ it finds $\widehat a_{NS}/\pi=-0.50478,-0.50068,-0.50017$, $(2L/\pi)$ times the difference $1.00404,1.00063,1.00016$, and the length ratio $0.49830,0.49977,0.49994$. A massive Dirac field at $\nu_0$ contributes $7.3\times10^{-5}$ and $7.5\times10^{-9}$ to the difference at $L=10$ and $20$. The massless-interaction remainder dominates the approach; the massive winding terms are negligible beyond $L\simeq15$. These numbers illustrate the extraction with stated remainders and are not a measurement.

The order of limits is part of the statement. Matching gives $m_\gamma\propto g\sqrt{|\mu\Lambda|}$, so $\mu\to0$ at fixed $L$ destroys the long-string hierarchy. Equations (32)–(34) hold at fixed $\mu\ne0$ with $Lm_{\rm gap}\to\infty$, or in a joint limit in which that product diverges, with $m_{\rm gap}$ the smaller of the bulk and localized gaps, $\nu_0m_\gamma$ on the weak branch. No uniformity is claimed where a further gap closes.

### 11.7 What this section establishes

The Goldstino contribution (27) follows from the exact supersymmetry, the inherited spin structure and the free-field counting, checked three ways. The bosonic kernel and gap at the BPS point follow from (28)–(29), the index [8] and the numerical spectrum. The total coefficients (32) and the absence of further gapless fermions rest on (H$_F$) and on (31) for $w<w_*$. This realizes the massless limit of P21 on a microscopic confining string. Its massive diagnostic would be realized by the localized massive fermions at $\nu_0m_\gamma$, whose multiplicities and interactions are not calculated here, and its pure-QCD application is open. P18 concerns open strings and P22 a separate dynamical mass; neither is decided by this section.

## 12. What can be compared across gauge descriptions

The core spectrum now supplies more than a positive tension and a set of allowed representations. One can list, in order, the inputs that fix a measurable quantity. The fundamental charge and the monopole charge fix one period of flux. The linear coefficient of $\mu u$ fixes $v$, and canonical kinetic matching fixes $g$ and $m_\gamma$. The quadratic coefficient fixes $M$ and $w$. The nodeless wave of the resulting vortex fixes $m_b/m_\gamma$ and its magnetic overlap. Finally the controlled perturbation of the critical spectral subspaces fixes the local trace derivative and its source direction. The last two steps involve the actual radial wavefunctions of the tube. No regrouping of charges alone determines them.

A simple test for a proposed equivalence is to give the two descriptions the same tension and ask whether another dimensionless observable is also fixed. At leading small breaking, setting $\sigma$ fixes $|\mu\Lambda|$ up to our normalization. It does not separately fix the coupling at the photon scale. Even at this order $m_b/\sqrt\sigma$ varies as $g$ and $|Q_b|$ varies as $1/g$. Their product tends to $0.597469$ in the leading action. A coincident numerical value of the product in another theory would be one test of a proposed dictionary, while the separate tension-normalized mass, reflection sectors and residue partition would remain independent tests. This distinction matters if distant descriptions share a topologically protected flux number but differ in the neutral fields that can mix on it. The analytic relation (9) gives a further test within the local theory, and its small denominator explains its precision cost.

The gauge theory also determines the direction in parameter space through the weakly broken family. The detuning exercise in [1] changes a neutral squared mass at fixed string action coefficients independent of that shift. In the present microscopic construction there is no separately stipulated positive detuning of the real neutral field at leading order. A microscopic deformation or a correction to a kinetic term would have to supply one. In a spectroscopic analysis it matters whether such a term remains fixed, vanishes with $w$, or is parametrically smaller. For a real trace detuning $\delta$ in photon units, [1] gives a lower fraction $\frac12[1+d/(1+d^2)^{1/2}]$ in the double limit at fixed $d=\delta/(2\nu_0w)$. Hence a relative correction that is small on the scale $m_\gamma^2$ can still be large on the scale of the doublet mixing. Deriving a physical detuning in a proposed extension is more informative than appending an adjustable splitting to a fit.

For an electric-string measurement the direction of reflection of the tube must be carried through the identification of sources. The Wilson loop by itself is an average over its gauge orientation. Two transverse displacements with the same polarization are even under interchange of the normal directions after projecting the trace. Two with orthogonal polarizations in the antisymmetric combination are odd. This information can be preserved in an effective description even if the local dual field that best interpolates the odd excitation has no simple elementary representative in the microscopic theory. The squared amplitude of a gauge-orientation-even flux-weighted insertion is also well defined in the local model. Relating it to a particular local colour field strength in the ultraviolet would require short-distance operator matching. The charge dictionary proves the static flux assignment. It does not by itself carry arbitrary local operators through strong coupling.

The contrast with other gauge groups can be stated without borrowing an excitation spectrum. For rank larger than one there can be several magnetic Abelian fields and several condensates with correlated periods. Douglas and Shenker [3] analyze the distinct tension scales that can arise at weak breaking. For $SU(2)$ the unit fundamental tube considered here has a single light magnetic factor. Pairing two or more of its fluxes cannot generate an independent exact center charge. A proposed use of (8) for the $k$-strings of another group would have to identify the relevant vortex background, radial Hessian, degeneracies, and scale of its other light Abelian sectors. A relation among *static* tensions only supplies one part of this information. Even the fixed circular higher-winding theorem of [1] applies to profiles of a single Abelian connection, with the relative-position variations excluded. It is not a substitute for a rank-$N-1$ coupled calculation.

Finally consider continuation away from weak breaking. Holding the electric center charge fixed does not protect an internal radial level from entering a continuum, losing overlap, or mixing with another particle of the same parity. The mass $m_b$ is neither a central charge of bulk particle supersymmetry nor a topological charge of the string. The projected two-phonon matrix element is a dynamical overlap. On the present branch both can be computed because the core is wide in microscopic units, the dual coupling is asymptotically weak, and the relevant one-particle bulk threshold is separated in photon units. Increasing $|\mu|/|\Lambda|$ erodes these conditions. Conversely sending it to zero makes $m_b/\sqrt\sigma$ go to zero as $g$, while extra bulk states also become light in tension units. Hence this corollary gives a controlled electric-string example with a pseudoscalar response. It establishes no identification of this state with a finite-mass resonance in units of the tension of pure four-dimensional Yang–Mills theory.

## 13. Numerical reproduction and historical boundary

There are three independent numerical tasks in the calculation. The chromoelectric line assignment and the Taylor coefficients in (1)–(5) follow analytically. For the existing static and real even/odd *local* radial data we use the previously committed programs supplied with the [1994 vacuum calculation](../1994-monopole-string/code/soft_vortex.py) and [1992 trace paper](../1992-scalar-doublet/code/trace_spectrum.py). For the new imaginary sector we assemble (15) and solve both the inhomogeneous critical equation at a fixed frequency and the independent two-component eigenproblem at nonzero soft mass. For the mixing footprint we then use the algebraic propagator (20) with the computed radial overlap. These steps address distinct questions. The two-mode examples have no adjustable profile or fitted wavefunction after the choice of finite matching convention. A matching scheme and an operator truncation are still inputs to those examples.

More explicitly the static solver from [1] uses the full three-field background on a logarithmic grid. We evaluate it at Gauss points in a uniform element mesh on the radial interval. The positive weighted measure is $2\pi\rho d\rho$. Use continuous linear elements, remove the axis value of $j_I$, allow the neutral and longitudinal waves a regular free axis value, and remove all three values at the outer Dirichlet face. The $wf$ entry is a multiplication-operator mass matrix between these two *different* trial spaces. The susceptibility (16) is evaluated on the charged space after its boundary projection. The normalization of $n_I$ at finite $w$ includes the charged portion of the generalized eigenvector. The magnetic overlap (19) uses that normalized neutral component directly; renormalizing it a second time to unit neutral norm would overestimate the mixing. The vector, on the other hand, has a full canonical norm in its single radial slot. The mesh and boundary variations recorded in Table 1 and in the JSON printout check these operations.

The exact rank identities also provide checks on numerical stability. The ratio $(\lambda_n-\lambda_b)/w^2$ at finite deformation is compared with $1-\Xi_J$ on the corresponding critical mesh. At $w=0$ the code uses the complement solution directly, avoiding division of a difference by zero. For $h\ne0$ in the projected response the positive pole weights must sum to one at every choice of the comparison coupling. The roots are evaluated using their product to avoid loss of precision for the smaller one, and the squared-mass difference is evaluated as the norm of the diagonal detuning and the mixing contribution. That procedure avoids estimating a tiny difference by subtracting two nearly equal squared masses from a quadratic formula. The table of correlated tension and mass changes uses a different radial implementation for the finite even sector. None of these checks supplies an error budget for omitted microscopic operators or for the total string width.

With Python, NumPy and SciPy installed, the command `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 code/odd_cluster.py` from this paper directory prints all new data. The program imports the radial basis and logarithmic background calculation from the distributed 1992 article. It reads the earlier results for the independent tension consistency check; the critical susceptibility, finite neutral masses, overlap and two-pole projection are solved in this calculation. The plot uses the same stored data. The checks of §11 are printed by `python3 code/spin_structure.py`, which reuses the critical profile solver of [6] and writes `results/spin-structure.json`. The sources and definitions used for each historical comparison are described in the accompanying [source record](SOURCES.md). An independent textual referee and an author reproduction record specify what was checked.

The source of confinement in the monopole patch, the period formula, the dual description, and the possibility of nonuniversal tension scales at small soft breaking are established historical physics [2,3]. The general mechanism by which a neutral pseudoscalar can mix with a longitudinal gauge fluctuation through $F\widetilde F$ has a direct counterpart in Agia, §4.2. The special point at which both radial operators agree also appears there in an enlarged Abelian model. Here the neutral imaginary scalar is already present in the monopole multiplet, its zero-deformation norm is fixed, and the analytic term of the gauge kinetic function supplies a definite matrix element in a specified local subtraction. Recent Abelian-Higgs calculations obtain the critical odd/even coupling relation and the polarization dependence of wave collisions, and the QCD flux-tube spectrum has separate lattice evidence for an odd resonance. These sources delimit the comparison. The charged source, microscopic scale trajectory and threshold of the present electric string differ from those of a generic cosmological string or a pure Yang–Mills tube.

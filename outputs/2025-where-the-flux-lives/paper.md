# Where does the QCD string live before it breaks?

**QCDS-25/S1 · December 2025 · Revised odd-year article in the counterfactual QCD string series**

## Abstract

We follow the QCD string between a static fundamental quark and antiquark as it mixes with two static-light mesons. Thermal screening was seen in lattice QCD in 1998 and the two-flavour zero-temperature spectrum resolved in 2005. What would reveal an internal position of the tube before it breaks? On a flat circle two source profiles give identical forces and opposite midpoint electric-energy moments. In published QCD spectra a rank-one mixing condition predicts two reserved levels with residuals $+0.00304$ and $+0.00077$ in lattice units. In the published two-flavour midpoint profile at $1.2$ fm the action contrast falls to $0.552$ of its axial value by transverse distance $0.25$ fm. To perform the internal-position experiment we use a confining $SU(3)$ strip with scalar screening matter and two rows of unequal electric cost. We move both sources onto the more expensive row and calculate the local electric Casimir on the two lowest levels. At three bonds the ground-state tube still runs down the cheaper row. At five bonds its raw midpoint signal is $0.00040$ on the screened ground state and $0.99925$ on the first excitation, in units of a fundamental link. At the tuned avoided crossing the local signal is shared equally. Six local hops generate the leading mixing amplitude within the ordered-line variational space. A uniform strip and a strong-coupling calculation before variational projection test the interpretation. The strip demonstrates how a local probe separates source motion, pinning and screening. Its layer costs and scalar matter are model inputs. A relation between this internal observable and four-dimensional QCD requires independent matching.

## 1. Following a pair of colour sources

Place a static fundamental quark at $x=-L/2$ and its conjugate at $x=L/2$, with the same Euclidean time extent. Contract their indices into a singlet. The experiment concerns the energy of the gauge and light-quark fields in the presence of these sources. A heavy-quark rest mass can be changed by changing the regulator, so energy differences, derivatives with respect to $L$, and properly subtracted local fields are the natural outputs. At intermediate separations the energy climbs approximately linearly and a field measurement can resolve a tube. At larger separations a dynamical antiquark can screen the heavy quark and its partner can screen the heavy antiquark. The same external sources now admit two widely separated colour singlets. The lowest energy levels mix while their field distributions change. We will follow that system throughout the paper. The long-lived closed string of a pure-gauge calculation and a fundamental string drawn in an auxiliary geometry are relevant to it only through a specified correspondence.

Consider two questions one might put to this pair. Does its energy increase by $\sigma\,dL$ on moving one end a distance $dL$? Does its energy density at the midpoint concentrate around some internal position? An affirmative answer to the first question takes one real number at each $L$. The second asks for the expectation of an operator labelled by position, or at least for its response to a specified nonuniform probe. Even a complete knowledge of the first number at every $L$ does not supply a position label. The distinction matters when a proposed internal space has been designed to reproduce four-dimensional gauge charges. Charges constrain allowed transitions. The kinetic term constrains a weak gauge zero mode. The energy density of a strong-interaction state is a third datum. These statements refer to different matrix elements of the same hypothetical parent theory.

There is an immediate historical answer to whether this problem was overlooked. Witten explicitly asked how a type IIA string in the bulk could become a confining string bound to a fivebrane. Dvali and Shifman asked how a bulk confining medium could support a gauge field on a wall. Lattice calculations subsequently put charges on and off such a wall. Other simulations varied the compactification size in a non-Abelian theory and followed the resulting long string. In 2024 a holographic study allowed the classical string to vary along a coordinate associated with the different gauge groups of a quiver, as well as along the usual holographic radial direction. Each investigation specified both an object and a measurement. They give substantial literature for the question even though their measurements do not coincide. [Witten](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9706109) · [Dvali and Shifman](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9612128) · [Laine et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0404058) · [de Forcrand et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.4643) · [Giliberti et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04539)

String breaking supplies a control that can be realized with the same pair of external charges. At approximately the same source separation we can compare two eigenstates of one theory, each made out of the same static charges, at least one of which has a substantial overlap with a two-meson operator. We can ask which features of a candidate internal distribution follow the string-like component and which follow the static charges wherever their colour is screened. At very large $L$ there is a separate subtraction available from two individually measured mesons. A study using a single straight Wilson loop would miss both comparisons. A study that assigns a single position to a mixing angle would conflate a matrix element in a three-dimensional space of channels with a matrix element in physical space.

In the earlier QCDS-98/1 article the string carried an electric centre class in a theory with adjoint light fields. With dynamical fundamental quarks an external fundamental charge still has triality one. The light fields change which line operators can end and can neutralize the source. A finite line ending on light fields can appear in gauge-invariant correlation functions. There is consequently no conserved unbreakable fundamental tube between this pair. This observation does not weaken the QCD interpretation of the tube at separations below the crossing. It gives a way to remove the extended field while retaining the probes. A proposed bulk origin should account for that change of state, along with the representation of the endpoints. [QCDS-98/1](../1998-colour-across-dualities/paper.md) · [Bali et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0505012)

We use the term *internal position* only when it has an operational specification. On a circle it is a coordinate relative to the phase of a laboratory probe such as $\cos\theta$. On a domain wall it can be the proper distance from a wall identified by a scalar profile. In a string dual it may be specified by a boundary operator and a bulk reconstruction prescription. In ordinary four-dimensional lattice QCD there are Euclidean time and three spatial coordinates. There a midpoint flux profile refers to ordinary transverse space. Supplying a fifth label requires defining an extra operator or a theory with an extra coordinate. Gauge rotations of a four-dimensional colour field cannot supply such a label. The gauge-invariant energy of a field configuration is a colour singlet even when the source representation is fundamental.

We give a quantitative counterexample to inferring internal position from static forces. It uses a compact, completely soluble Maxwell problem, chosen so that the assumptions are inspectable. We then use measured QCD energies to test what *can* be reconstructed without spatial information. A rank-one condition on the separation dependence of the low-energy Hamiltonian suffices to make a nontrivial prediction between three separations. The condition comes from a published lattice parametrization and is testable without a Cornell interpolation. We evaluate the ordinary-space action profile from a published QCD figure, then carry out the source displacement and local field measurement in a finite, confining $SU(3)$ parent. The programme registered the choice of levels, the analytical predictions and the tolerances in a public parent commit before evaluating the first two tests. A separate public record fixed the strip calculation before diagonalization. We use the published 2024 spectra as existing data throughout. [Registration commit](https://github.com/arivero/qcdstring/commit/5a558ca8dbe9f715ec8a15a267e7261817db9dc0) · [Lattice spectra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00754) · [Strip registration](https://github.com/arivero/qcdstring/commit/827633aca323713e2e58e09c9769b6c03b602f2f)

## 2. The chronology of a breaking string

### 2.1 A threshold, an avoided crossing and a real-time event

A static-light meson has an energy $M_{Q\ell}$ in a given regularization. To a first approximation the onset of breaking can be estimated by solving $V_{\rm str}(L)=2M_{Q\ell}$ with all heavy self-energies in the same convention. An estimate of this type already appeared in the heavy-light lattice literature in the early 1990s. It compares the cost of supporting flux with the cost of screening each source. It requires an extrapolation if the initially rising string energy is measured only short of its intersection with the threshold. What anyone can observe from a variational matrix at fixed $L$ are the interacting levels, including the energy gap that forms where these costs become comparable. Alexandrou et al. provided an early estimate. Drummond subsequently examined the mixing mechanism in a lattice strong-coupling model. [Alexandrou et al., 1992 preprint and 1994 publication](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9211042) · [Drummond, 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9805012)

An observation of flattening in an equilibrium free energy, the approach of static energy levels to heavy-light thresholds at low temperature, and the rate at which a prepared moving pair fragments are three experiments. A thermal Polyakov-loop correlation includes a trace over states. Its free energy tends to the lowest energy only under the appropriate low-temperature and large-time limits. A zero-temperature variational calculation resolves eigenvalues. A real-time rate additionally depends on how the system is prepared and on transition amplitudes to outgoing hadrons. An avoided crossing already gives information about a transition matrix element in a low-energy Hamiltonian. Converting it into a fragmentation probability requires a trajectory in time and the accessible final states. We use *string breaking* in the static, spectroscopic sense unless an experiment is explicitly identified as thermal.

The slow progress of early simulations had a concrete cause. A Wilson loop creates a state with appreciable overlap onto a comparatively straight flux tube. The product of two well separated static-light mesons can have a very small overlap onto this same loop. If two levels contribute $A_0e^{-E_0 T}+A_1e^{-E_1T}$ with $A_0\ll A_1$ and $E_0<E_1$, the lower state dominates only for $T$ greater than roughly $\log(A_1/A_0)/(E_1-E_0)$. In the crossing region the gap in the denominator is small. Increasing $T$ suppresses the signal along with the excited state. Adding explicit meson-pair interpolators changes the accessible overlaps at a given $T$. This is why the earlier recognition of the threshold and the later resolution of the levels have different dates. In 1998 Philipsen and Wittig and, separately, Knechtli and Sommer obtained clear results in non-Abelian gauge theories with fundamental scalar matter. Those simulations gave an important demonstration of the method before the corresponding quantitative QCD calculation with sea quarks. [Three-dimensional gauge–Higgs simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9807020) · [Four-dimensional gauge–Higgs simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9807022)

In two-flavour QCD Bali et al. measured the two lowest levels, a minimum gap of about $51(3)$ MeV, and a characteristic distance of about $1.25$ fm at their sea-quark mass and scale setting. This was a 2005 publication. Two things about the numbers are instructive. The gap is detectable, so the exact eigenstates change continuously. The quoted distance belongs to that ensemble and to its definition of the crossover. Equality of *unmixed* energies and the minimum of the full gap agree in the constant two-state mixing approximation. A varying off-diagonal element displaces the minimum, and an additional strange threshold can displace it further. A year attached to a subsequent measurement should specify which of these quantities was added. [Bali et al., Eqs. (92)–(93)](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0505012)

There is also evidence in QCD available to the historical 1998 author. DeTar, Kaczmarek, Karsch and Laermann used Polyakov loops with dynamical quarks below the thermal transition and found screening in the static-pair free energy. The preprint appeared in August 1998 and the journal paper in 1999. This is a particularly helpful distinction for our series. A 1998 discussion could already cite a QCD signal, without attributing to it the subsequent zero-temperature measurement of the gap. The 2005 work also led to a spatial study. Prkacin et al. displayed the action-density distribution as the separation passed through breaking, with the static-source and static-meson channels available to the analysis. The established literature therefore goes beyond a potential measured at two distances. There are pictures of what happens in ordinary space when the QCD string breaks. [DeTar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9808028) · [Prkacin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0510051)

### 2.2 The second threshold and the available data

With two nearly degenerate light flavours and one strange flavour, a static source can form a static-light or a static-strange meson. In the flavour-singlet sector a string operator can mix with the appropriate combinations of the two light pair states and the strange pair state. The difference of the two degenerate light-flavour pairs is orthogonal to the flavour-singlet string and drops out in the isospin limit. If the coupling to each light pair is $g_\ell$, its coupling to the normalized singlet combination is $\sqrt{2}\,g_\ell$. This factor affects the width of a crossing even if its location is estimated from the diagonals alone. A valence strange propagator in a two-flavour ensemble and a theory with a dynamical strange sea have different path-integral weights. The later simulations can treat both sea and valence flavours together.

In 2019 Bulava et al. resolved the three lowest relevant energies in $N_f=2+1$ lattice QCD and identified two breaking scales. The pion was about $280$ MeV on the N200 ensemble. In 2024 the collaboration extended that study to two additional sea-quark masses at the same lattice spacing, and fitted the quark-mass dependence of the mixing parameters and the static potential. At the physical-mass extrapolation the intersections of the fitted string diagonal with the light and strange diagonals are $1.211(7)(11)$ and $1.336(7)(12)$ fm. The errors and the ensemble limitations belong with these numbers. In particular, the physical-mass extrapolation uses a single nominal lattice spacing of about $0.063$ fm. Section 7 below uses the finite-spacing N200 measurements in lattice units directly. We do not assign either of the two physical-mass numbers to that ensemble. [Bulava et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04006) · [Bulava et al., 2024, Fig. 4 and Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00754)

Consider the second of these crossings. By the time the bare string diagonal reaches the strange threshold, the light two-meson state is already energetically competitive. The strictly lowest state need not show another sharp change at this separation. The higher levels and the strange-pair interpolator matter. A claim to have followed the string by always following the ground-state energy would quietly exchange the string-dominated state for a light-meson pair. Tracking the eigenvalue continuously is a sensible prescription for the force in equilibrium. Tracking an extended distribution in space requires a matrix element on this state and preferably on the nearby excited states. The three-level dataset supplies a relatively economical place to examine what information survives on retaining only eigenvalues.

The geometry literature and the QCD literature have thus both addressed the string. The combination missing from the sources we examined is more specific. It would involve gauge-invariant operators attached to an independently specified internal region, a calibrated four-dimensional QCD sector, dynamical fundamental matter, and measurement of local matrix elements across the string-to-meson crossover in that same theory. A search confined to lattice papers with “string breaking” in the title misses much of the relevant gauge-localization literature. A search confined to papers with “brane” in the title misses the earlier spatial distributions of QCD. Neither incomplete search would support a claim of priority for asking where the fields go.

## 3. Four physical meanings of a dimension

### 3.1 The relative charge on a fivebrane

In the fivebrane construction take an ambient space $Y$ with one noncontractible circle and a connected fivebrane curve $\Sigma$ that winds around this circle $N$ times. An M2 surface extended along the macroscopic string has an internal cross-section which is a one-chain. Its boundary, if present, lies on $\Sigma$. A deformation of an open chain can slide its endpoints on $\Sigma$. A closed chain in $Y$ is also admissible. The relevant part of the exact sequence is
$$
 H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\ \longrightarrow\ H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\
 \longrightarrow H_1(Y,\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\
 \longrightarrow H_0(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\ \longrightarrow H_0(Y;\mathbb Z).
 \tag{1}
$$
Connectedness makes the last map an isomorphism. The image of the first map consists of multiples of $N$. A closed membrane cross-section of unit winding and an open interval whose ends lie on consecutive sheets therefore represent the same element of $\mathbb Z_N$. The first object reduces to an elementary type IIA string. The second can be shorter in the given metric. It gives the bound confining representative in the supersymmetric gauge construction. Witten exhibited a deformation between the representatives and discussed their very different energies. This answers part of the original question remarkably literally. A string can leave the brane while retaining information about the charge one would measure relative to the brane. Its ability to support a low-tension four-dimensional colour flux and its energy cost in the bulk are dynamical statements. [Witten, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9706109)

Several assumptions in this answer matter for QCD. The tension of the electric confining string in this example is unprotected, and the regime with a large smooth brane need not be the regime in which all extra modes decouple. The vacuum and the string have distinct supersymmetry properties. A supersymmetric parent may constrain which backgrounds and endpoint sectors exist even when an isolated confining string fails to saturate a supersymmetry bound. Four-dimensional QCD with light fundamental matter also allows screening that the pure adjoint construction excludes. An extra endpoint species can change the admissible relative cycles or the long-time Hilbert space. To connect this description to the physics of Section 2 one would have to exhibit such a species, identify its static-light energy, and specify a matrix element between the tube and screened states. The homology calculation alone contains neither scale nor matrix element. [Hanany, Strassler and Zaffaroni](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9707244) · [QCDS-98/1, charge and tension](../1998-colour-across-dualities/paper.md)

An instructive contrasting topology has $H_1(Y)=0$ and one connected admissible endpoint locus. Exactness of (1) then gives $H_1(Y,\Sigma)=0$. A closed curve called a fibre may exist at each point of a base and still be contractible in the total space, as on the Hopf fibration. Merely drawing the source on such a fibre cannot generate the $\mathbb Z_N$ quotient above. A nontrivial gauge bundle, a defect, several endpoint components or additional conserved flux changes the problem. For example, disconnected endpoint components can leave a kernel in the final $H_0$ map. That kernel distinguishes where a chain ends and can be free abelian. It does not by itself reproduce reduction of colour flux modulo three. Counting how many times an apparent circle fits around another circle works only after selecting the actual maps in (1).

### 3.2 A wall and a homogeneous periodic dimension

In the mechanism proposed by Dvali and Shifman the ambient non-Abelian vacuum confines, while a wall supports a different gauge phase. In a suitable example electric flux spreading along the wall costs less than pushing flux out into the bulk. Gauge fields can consequently mediate a lower-dimensional force between sources on the wall. The bulk already supplies a confinement scale. The energy cost of a flux line crossing the bulk helps localize a gauge field on the wall. This is a concrete answer to how a gauge force can stay near a lower-dimensional region. To use it as an explanation for colour confinement *on* a four-dimensional region one would still need the gauge group, light content, boundary probes and dynamics on that region to give the observed colour force and its breaking. A Coulombic wall gauge field would exhibit localization without providing that final dynamics. [Dvali and Shifman](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9612128)

Laine et al. made a particularly pertinent measurement in a $2+1$ dimensional gauge system with a wall. They placed static charges at specified distances from the wall, measured their force as a function of the separation parallel to the wall, and compared with the behaviour expected for localized gauge modes. A sufficiently thick wall relative to the bulk confinement length can support the characteristic lower-dimensional force. Other states remain important to the effective dynamics in the parameter range discussed. A static-force measurement here has an internal position argument because the simulation *puts in* a wall and allows the sources to be placed relative to it. Even then, translating both sources asks which force they feel. A field insertion at fixed endpoints would ask where their flux goes. The distinction survives in the continuum and has a simple calculation in Section 5. [Laine, Meyer, Rummukainen and Shaposhnikov](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0404058)

A homogeneous periodic direction instead gives a shift symmetry. An energy eigenstate of a translationally invariant closed system may have a uniform expectation of any translated density while still admitting a family of narrow localized profiles in states prepared relative to a source. A Wilson line at a specified internal point explicitly supplies such a marker. Simultaneously averaging the position of the marker and the density flattens any one-point profile. Measuring their relative position retains a possible signal. Conversely an extended Wilson source uniform on the circle can remove the linear coupling to all nonconstant harmonics even if an endpoint-localized source would excite them. Experiments that differ only in the choice of source can therefore find different apparent penetration lengths. The normalized zero-momentum coupling, the profile of a chosen mode, and the energy distribution of a physical pair require separate definitions.

On the lattice, anisotropy permits another possibility. Fu and Nielsen found a layered phase in an anisotropic gauge model where propagation within a layer and propagation between layers have different long-distance behaviour. In five-dimensional $SU(2)$ the interpretation of putative layered regimes has been sensitive to lattice volume and the order of the bulk transition. In particular Del Debbio et al. obtained evidence for first-order behaviour at a coupling where a continuous boundary of a layer phase had been considered. Localizing a force in a regulated effective theory can be a real observation. An interacting continuum limit supporting a four-dimensional colour string on an isolated layer is an additional requirement. Its existence cannot be inferred solely from a hierarchy of bare plaquette couplings. [Fu and Nielsen](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(84)90529-7) · [Knechtli, Irges and Rago](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0345) · [Del Debbio et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0752)

### 3.3 The short-distance circle and the long string

A particularly clean non-Abelian comparison is five-dimensional pure $SU(2)$ gauge theory with a compact spatial period. De Forcrand, Kurkela and Panero varied the bare parameters of an anisotropic lattice and measured observables including the energy of a winding flux tube in a large spatial direction. At distances large compared with the compactification scale, the tension describes a four-dimensional confining theory. The finite-length correction to a sufficiently long closed tube is consistent with the two massless transverse fluctuations appropriate to four spacetime dimensions. At shorter distances one can be sensitive to massive Kaluza–Klein (KK) excitations. The circle is homogeneous. The lattice result is evidence for dimensional reduction and a four-dimensional *long-distance string*. It provides no preferred place around that circle for the energy to accumulate and it contains no dynamical fundamental quarks to form the mesons in Section 2. [de Forcrand, Kurkela and Panero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.4643)

There is a different compact circle in the effective string theory studied by Meyer. Compactify one actual transverse spatial dimension of the target in which the tube fluctuates. Its displacement field on the world sheet becomes a compact boson. Vortices in that field can gap the corresponding long-wavelength fluctuation through a Kosterlitz–Thouless mechanism as the circumference is varied. A finite-size measurement of the world-sheet central charge can test for the disappearance of a massless displacement. The tension is itself a function of the compactification parameter not fixed by the counting of those modes. In particular, a loss of one world-sheet Casimir contribution does not measure how much local colour action sits at one angle of the compact displacement. One observable probes the spectrum of fluctuations around a tube. Another probes a matrix element of the microscopic fields in a state containing that tube. [Meyer](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0506034)

These two uses of compactification admit an instructive order of limits. If an effective theory is to make a long-string prediction, the external separation must exceed the inverse masses of excitations being discarded. If dynamical matter breaks the string at $L_b$, the nearly linear ground-state window also requires $L<L_b$ away from the mixing region. A hierarchy $M_{\rm KK}^{-1}\ll L\ll L_b$ permits a four-dimensional tube after compact modes have decayed. Such a tube may carry the memory of compactification through its renormalized coupling while direct exchange of a massive compact mode contributes negligibly to its force. If the two bounds overlap only marginally, a fit to a straight line over a few points cannot distinguish the two effects. The energy of a large winding string in the pure theory can provide a tension even when the theory with light quarks has no asymptotic unbroken fundamental string. Calling both numbers the tension requires specifying the window or the matching used.

### 3.4 A radial turning point and an internal flavour direction

In a classical string dual, let $x$ be the boundary separation direction and $u$ a radial variable. A time-independent world sheet can minimize the Nambu–Goto energy by descending to a radial region in which stretching along $x$ has a low cost. In string-frame metric components the local horizontal cost is
$$
 \mathcal T(u)=\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\sqrt{|g_{tt}(u)|\,g_{xx}(u)} .
 \tag{2}
$$
If the geometry ends smoothly and this cost has a positive accessible minimum, a long connected surface can acquire a segment there. After subtracting the energies associated with the endpoints, its leading energy is linear in $L$ with slope $\mathcal T_{\min}$. In the thermal-circle construction which models nonsupersymmetric Yang–Mills, the shrinking circle and the radial approach to its tip are different coordinates. The calculation relates a radial minimum to the force between boundary sources. Calling the radial position the place in an extra laboratory dimension at which QCD energy density peaks would require a dictionary for the corresponding local gauge-invariant operator. Classical control can also require KK masses of the order of the infrared scale. [Witten, thermal-circle construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9803131) · [Brandhuber et al., Wilson loops](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9803263)

Adding dynamical flavour changes the boundary conditions available to a string. Two separated configurations ending on flavour branes can compete in energy with a connected configuration. The comparison requires one heavy-source subtraction in the two calculations. If two different subtractions are chosen, an arbitrary constant per heavy end shifts the apparent crossing. Bigazzi, Cotrone, Paredes and Ramallo studied a backreacted theory with massive dynamical flavours. They could vary the number and mass of flavours and compare screening with the string tension and meson scales. Their analysis makes an interpretive problem concrete. On changing a theory, the answer to whether screening moves inward or outward can depend on which physical scale is used to compare the two members. A comparison in string-tension units and a comparison at fixed ultraviolet coupling answer distinct questions. [Bigazzi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.3399)

Giliberti, Fatemiabhari and Núñez allowed a static surface to explore both a radial coordinate and an internal $z$ direction related to the gauge and flavour nodes of a quiver. Boundary sources can be inserted at one node while dynamical flavour lives elsewhere in the quiver. As the separation increases, classical solutions can bend towards the flavour sources and the potential can saturate. The paper explicitly distinguishes that result from a world sheet that actually changes topology, detaches and reconnects in real time. Their minimization detects a change in the cost and shape of a connected surface. Additional string interactions govern the snapping amplitude. The node label also belongs to a particular holographic field-theory dictionary. There is no established operation on a four-dimensional QCD lattice configuration which reads out that same coordinate $z$. The calculation shows that allowing internal motion can change the result of a Wilson-loop calculation. Its internal profile is specific to this class of gauge theories. [Giliberti et al., §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04539)

The examples can be compared by asking what is held fixed. The fivebrane construction permits an internal chain to move with its boundary on a specified curve while preserving a relative charge. The wall calculation moves the *sources* relative to a wall. The compactified pure-gauge calculation changes an input circumference of the whole theory. The holographic quiver changes the permitted embedding while holding the boundary source node fixed. A QCD lattice action-density measurement changes none of these variables and inserts an operator at a point. These operations can coexist in a suitable theory. Substituting one for another would make a calculation appear to answer a question its observables cannot even express.

## 4. Which comparison of confinement scales is meaningful?

### 4.1 Matching a family of compactifications

Write $p$ for a shape or size parameter of a proposed higher-dimensional parent. Denote by $\mu$ a renormalization point below its heavy states, by $g(\mu,p)$ a four-dimensional coupling in one convention, and by $m_q(\mu,p)$ the corresponding renormalized quark masses. Below threshold the local effective action has the schematic form
$$
 {\cal L}_{\rm eff}=
 \frac{1}{4g^2(\mu,p)}F^a_{\mu\nu}F^a_{\mu\nu}
 + \sum_q \bar q(\not{D}+m_q)q
 + \sum_{i:\,d_i>4}\frac{c_i(\mu,p)}{M^{d_i-4}}{\cal O}_i .
 \tag{3}
$$
We use a connection convention in which the gauge coupling resides in front of $F^2$. Field renormalizations, the scale $M$ and the quark-mass scheme must be specified for numerical matching. At a fixed physical renormalization point a dimensionless infrared observable $X$ can depend on the generated strong scale $\Lambda$, on the ratios $m_q/\Lambda$, and on the dimensionless combinations of the higher-dimensional coefficients. Its variation cannot generally be inferred from the geometry of the bare kinetic term alone. A variation of $p$ can change all three sets of inputs.

For example, for a homogeneous circle of circumference $\ell$ and an unwarped bulk kinetic term, tree reduction gives $1/g_4^2=\ell/g_5^2$. In weakly coupled matching at $\ell^{-1}$, keeping only one-loop asymptotically free running, one finds
$$
 \log(\ell\Lambda)=-\frac{1}{2b_0 g_4^2(\ell^{-1})}
       +\hbox{higher-loop and matching terms},\qquad
 \mu\,\frac{dg_4}{d\mu}=-b_0g_4^3+\cdots .
 \tag{4}
$$
For pure $SU(2)$, $b_0=11/(24\pi^2)$. Equation (4) concerns the logarithm of an infrared scale and therefore amplifies uncertainties in matching. Fixing $g_5$ at a common regulator, changing $\ell$, matching again and comparing $\sqrt{\sigma}\ell$ is an intelligible experiment. Tuning the bare action at each $\ell$ to keep $g_4(\mu_{\rm ref})$ fixed at a common physical point is another. In the first experiment a change of the long-distance string tension can be caused by the ordinary four-dimensional running below threshold. In the second the leading change from the marginal coupling has been removed. The compactified $SU(2)$ simulations give a nonperturbative precedent for examining a hierarchy of scales in such a comparison. [de Forcrand et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.4643)

Threshold effects caused by internal geometry were investigated long before holographic QCD. Dixon, Kaplunovsky and Louis computed moduli-dependent one-loop gauge-coupling thresholds in heterotic orbifolds. Contino, Pilo, Rattazzi and Trincherini examined matching when massive KK states of a five-dimensional orbifold theory are integrated out. Precisely which terms are predictable depends on the symmetries, local operators and ultraviolet definition of the model. A boundary kinetic term allowed by the symmetries may contribute at the same order as a tower sum. A difference of two large formally divergent towers, evaluated with different prescriptions, can mimic a finite geometry dependence. Fixing the parent action, common subtraction conditions and independent low-energy inputs is the practical way to expose the calculable dependence. Neither source proposed a measured QCD string-breaking radius as the output of its own threshold calculation. [Dixon, Kaplunovsky and Louis](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(91)90490-O) · [Contino et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0108102)

### 4.2 An invariant test of a change of shape

Suppose a reference energy $H(p)$ is imported from an external calibration and the whole strong spectrum is rescaled with it. Then the light-channel crossing has the form $L_b(p)=\lambda_b/H(p)$ and the fitted intermediate string tension has the form $\sigma(p)=s\,H(p)^2$ for dimensionless constants $\lambda_b,s$. Their product obeys
$$
 L_b(p)\sqrt{\sigma(p)}=\lambda_b\sqrt{s}.
 \tag{5}
$$
A numerical change in a table of femtometres can therefore come from changing the ruler alone. Measuring a change in (5), or in the ratio of strange and light breaking distances at the same quark-mass ratios, would test a change in the *shape* of the strong spectrum. Keeping (5) fixed is compatible with either a purely common rescaling or compensating responses of several inputs. An independently calibrated observable, such as a force-defined distance or a mass ratio, distinguishes more possibilities. Reporting both the dimensionless quantity and the dimensionful calibration prevents two valid comparisons from being mistaken for different dynamical predictions.

The required inputs follow by differentiating an unmixed crossing condition. Let $u(L,p)$ be a string diagonal in a fixed channel convention and $d_\ell(p)$ its two-meson threshold. If a crossing is simple and the threshold in that convention is constant over the relevant $L$ interval, then
$$
 \frac{dL_b}{dp}=\frac{d_\ell'(p)-\partial_p u(L_b,p)}
                          {\partial_L u(L_b,p)} .
 \tag{6}
$$
For a threshold with residual separation dependence the denominator is $\partial_L(u-d_\ell)$. The formula is invariant if the same $p$-dependent constant is added to all energies. The numerator involves both a hadronic threshold response and a string response. Knowledge that the mass of a colour octet in a compact spectrum increases places no sign constraint on their difference without a matching calculation. The derivative of the *minimum-gap position* additionally involves the change of the mixing. These distinctions are consequential once a model uses physical quark masses, where the gap is appreciable.

The force at the compactification scale supplies another comparison which is directly calculable in many models. A static colour source can couple to massive vector modes with residues set by its wavefunction in the internal directions. A Yukawa potential $-c e^{-mL}/L$ contributes $c(1+mL)e^{-mL}/L^2$ to the attractive force magnitude. Heavy modes can thus affect $L^2 F$ around $mL\simeq1$, while their direct exchange is exponentially small at hadronic separation. Integrating out those modes can still alter local coefficients in (3). This is the distinction between exchange over a macroscopic separation and matching at a threshold. Using the exponential suppression of exchange as a bound on the entire threshold correction would miss the second effect. Conversely, fitting an infrared scale does not determine the residues at short distance. The next section computes both a force and a local diagnostic in a setting where every residue is known.

## 5. A circle with equal forces and opposite midpoint moments

### 5.1 A soluble measurement with fixed endpoints

Take flat space with three noncompact spatial coordinates $\mathbf x$ and an internal circle of radius $B$. Use normalized measure $d\theta/(2\pi)$ on the circle. At $\mathbf x=(\pm L/2,0,0)$ prepare static Abelian charges of opposite sign. Each has the same prescribed nonnegative density
$$
 s_a(\theta)=1+a\cos\theta,\qquad |a|\le1,\qquad
 \langle s_a\rangle_\theta=1 .
 \tag{7}
$$
Here $a$ is a source amplitude, distinct from the lattice spacing denoted by $a$ later. For fixed internal probes define the angle zero once. Two preparations with $a$ and $-a$ translate the pair halfway around the circle relative to those probes. Maxwell theory controls this calculation at every $L>0$ for classical sources. The leading colour-singlet Born exchange in a weak gauge background has the corresponding group factor under additional assumptions about the sources. We take no weak-coupling continuation into the strong QCD string. The calculation tests whether an observable inference can fail even with exactly known compact geometry.

Fourier expanding the static Green function produces a three-dimensional Yukawa kernel with mass $|n|/B$ for mode $n$. With $t=r/B$, and suppressing a common normalization fixed by the zero mode, it is
$$
 G(r,\delta)=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-|n|t}e^{in\delta}
       =\frac{1}{r}\frac{\sinh t}{\cosh t-\cos\delta}.
 \tag{8}
$$
To derive the second equality sum the two geometric series for $n>0$ and $n<0$ and add the constant. The normalization is fixed by $\langle rG\rangle_\delta=1$ for $r>0$, so the angle average reproduces ordinary Coulomb exchange. This representation also supplies a separate numerical route to the result by integrating the rational hyperbolic kernel over both sources without truncating a KK tower. The potential with heavy self-energies removed is $-C\langle s_a(\theta)G(L,\theta-\phi)s_a(\phi)\rangle_{\theta,\phi}$, where $C>0$ includes the charges. The constant coefficient of $s_a$ is one, and its coefficients at $\pm1$ are $a/2$. Writing $y=L/B$ gives
$$
 V_a(L)=-\frac{C}{L}\left(1+\frac{a^2}{2}e^{-y}\right),
 \qquad \frac{F_a(L)}{F_0(L)}
 =1+\frac{a^2}{2}(1+y)e^{-y},\qquad F_0(L)=\frac{C}{L^2}.
 \tag{9}
$$
Here $F=\partial_L V$ is positive for attraction. Despite its sensitivity to the nonconstant mode, this force has lost the sign of $a$. More generally it contains $\lvert s_n\rvert^2$ for a pair with identical profiles. Relative Fourier phases can be recovered in exchange with a *second, calibrated* source. An integral over two identical sources cannot recover them.

Now place a field probe at the ordinary-space midpoint $(0,0,0)$, with angular position $\theta$. The electrostatic potential of the opposite pair vanishes there at every angle by antisymmetry in $x$. Its longitudinal gradient is nonzero. Its angular gradient and the two other ordinary spatial derivatives vanish at the central point by the same antisymmetry and reflection symmetries. A finite slab about the midpoint generally has extra components. The contribution of one source to the magnitude of the longitudinal gradient at ordinary radius $r=L/2$ is proportional to the radial derivative of $-[1+a e^{-r/B}\cos\theta]/r$. The two gradients add. Dividing by the value for uniform sources gives
$$
 \frac{E_x(0,\theta)}{E_x^{(0)}(0)}=1+A\cos\theta,\qquad
 A=a(1+y/2)e^{-y/2}.
 \tag{10}
$$
Both differentiations in (9) and (10) act on the inverse ordinary distance as well as on the Yukawa exponential. Dropping the first derivative term changes the response near $L=B$. The factor of one half in the exponential is set by the distance from a source to the midpoint. No halving of the KK mass has occurred.

The local field energy here is proportional to $E_x^2$. A distortion of the first angular harmonic can be tested by its normalized first moment and, independently, by the induced second moment. Direct angle integration gives
$$
 M_1=\frac{\langle\cos\theta\,E_x^2\rangle}
            {\langle E_x^2\rangle}=\frac{A}{1+A^2/2},
 \qquad
 M_2=\frac{\langle\cos(2\theta)\,E_x^2\rangle}
            {\langle E_x^2\rangle}=\frac{A^2/4}{1+A^2/2}.
 \tag{11}
$$
These are moments against the unnormalized test functions $\cos(n\theta)$ using normalized measure. The linear sensitivity of $M_1$ to a source distortion follows from interference with the uniform electric field. If only the nonconstant field energy had been kept, one would have obtained $\cos^2\theta$ and lost the sign again. The sign requires an internal phase reference. Rephasing the probe together with the sources returns exactly the original experiment, as translation symmetry demands. On a homogeneous circle this is a *source-induced* profile. It is a useful counterexample precisely because we know no trapping by a special region of the vacuum is present.

At the registered point $a=+0.6$ and $y=2$, (9) gives $1.07308105$ and (11) gives $0.40225868$. Reversing $a$ leaves the former unchanged and gives $-0.40225868$. Direct integration of (8) on equal angular grids for the two sources, numerical differentiation of the integrated potential, and separate squaring of the midpoint field give these quantities within $6\times10^{-12}$. The specified tolerance was $10^{-7}$. At increasing separation the *excess* in (9) decays as a polynomial times $e^{-L/B}$, while the first moment decays as a polynomial times $e^{-L/(2B)}$. In the same theory a choice of observable has changed the decay length plotted against the external pair separation. Reading a compact mass from a decay without specifying the geometry of the measurement can therefore introduce a factor of two. ### 5.2 How much of this ambiguity survives without a symmetry?

A common translation is the simplest way of producing two equal forces. There are ambiguities even after taking the phase of the first harmonic as a reference tied to the sources. Consider the smooth family $s=1+a\cos\theta+b\cos(2\theta+\phi)$ with $a,b>0$ and $a+b<1$. Fix the origin by the maximum of the *first harmonic*. Changing $\phi$ retains every $\lvert s_n\rvert$. Equation (8) gives exactly the same pair force, including the second-harmonic Yukawa contribution, at all $L$. With this phase convention only reflection takes $\phi$ to $-\phi$. The profiles with $\phi=0$ and $\phi=\pi/2$ cannot be related by either a translation preserving the first harmonic or a reflection. Even the shape of the internal distribution is not uniquely specified by the full function $F(L)$ in this example.

Let $D=b(1+y)e^{-y}$ be the second-harmonic midpoint coefficient and keep $A$ from (10). The midpoint energy is proportional to $[1+A\cos\theta+D\cos(2\theta+\phi)]^2$. Orthogonality gives the angle average $1+(A^2+D^2)/2$. Its unnormalized first cosine moment is $A+(AD/2)\cos\phi$. The sine moment is $-(AD/2)\sin\phi$ in these conventions. For this second family the ambiguity is present in a moment measured relative to an origin that the first source harmonic itself supplies. A purely spectral determination of the masses and the positive exchange residues still leaves information that a local interference measurement recovers. Strong interactions invalidate the simple Fourier calculation as a model of QCD. They do not establish an inverse theorem by which the missing matrix elements in a specified gauge theory would follow from the energies alone.

There is a related ambiguity in interpreting a positive midpoint signal. Suppose a weak gauge mode has action weight $w(\theta)>0$ and a positive internal-gradient coefficient $v(\theta)$. Without a defect mass term, its zero mode on a connected periodic space obeys $-\partial_\theta(v\partial_\theta\psi)=0$. Multiplying by $\bar\psi$, integrating around the circle and integrating by parts gives $\int v|\partial_\theta\psi|^2=0$. In connection variables this mode is constant. Its normalized kinetic-energy weight is $w/\int w$. Thus a measured density that varies as $w$ could arise already from the massless gauge mode of the compactification. To isolate an *additional* polarization associated with a confining string, one may choose a test function $f$ obeying $\int wf=0$ and calibrate it in independent weak-field states. This orthogonality removes one background under the specified quadratic action. Quantum matching can mix the operator with others and can generate a renewed response that needs to be measured.

The direction of the source provides a second control. In a geometry with a definite defect location one can move the endpoints away from the defect while retaining the probe attached to it, and compare a segment well separated from either end. In a flat dimension, translating the source translates the weak signal. In a model that pins an extended tube to a defect, the midpoint may stay near the defect even as the near-end fields follow the source. The associated excess energy contains two connector segments. Their combined cost can be largely independent of $L$ once the central tube is long. A static force that differentiates with respect to $L$ suppresses precisely that diagnostic constant. A local profile, or a measurement varying the endpoint-to-defect displacement, retains it. If light quarks screen the sources before a central plateau forms, this particular test lacks a length window. The limitation is observable in the same spectrum that establishes breaking.

## 6. What three static energies at two separations determine

### 6.1 An inverse problem for the QCD string

Return to the operators that create the QCD string and the two static-meson pairs. Approximate the relevant energies on a finite interval by a three-dimensional Hermitian Hamiltonian. Assume there is one fixed normalized vector $|S\rangle$ such that
$$
 H(L_2)-H(L_1)=[u(L_2)-u(L_1)]\,|S\rangle\langle S|
 \tag{12}
$$
for all pairs of separations on the interval. In the physical interpretation $|S\rangle$ is the string channel. The statement is stronger than the existence of three reasonably smooth eigenvalues. It requires a common basis across the interval, fixed two-meson parameters and fixed mixing in that basis. Meson interactions, an extra low level or a separation-dependent transition amplitude can violate it. One can test spectral consequences of (12) even when the underlying identification of $|S\rangle$ needs more data. The low-energy fit of Bulava et al. has precisely this form, with a particular Cornell function for $u$. Here $u$ is allowed to be any real function.

The compression of $H$ to the plane orthogonal to $|S\rangle$ is a fixed Hermitian two-by-two matrix. Diagonalize it once, order its distinct eigenvalues as $d_1<d_2$, and choose phases so that the string couplings are real and nonnegative. Then
$$
 H_i=\begin{pmatrix}u_i&h_1&h_2\\h_1&d_1&0\\h_2&0&d_2\end{pmatrix},
 \quad Q(z)=(z-d_1)(z-d_2),\quad
 P_i(z)=\prod_{n=0}^2(z-E_{ni}).
 \tag{13}
$$
The labels light and strange apply when the compression agrees with the meson channels of the QCD fit. Mathematically its eigenvalues could belong to mixtures of those channels. Diagonalizing a two-meson submatrix with a constant off-diagonal entry would lead to the same inverse formula. Determining the flavour content then requires operators carrying that information. An independent rephasing of either meson channel changes a mixing sign and leaves every energy unchanged. The inverse can determine only magnitudes without further phase conventions and data.

Expanding the determinant in the first row yields
$$
 P_i(z)=(z-u_i)Q(z)-h_1^2(z-d_2)-h_2^2(z-d_1).
 \tag{14}
$$
Let $T_i=\sum_n E_{ni}$. Trace invariance gives $T_2-T_1=u_2-u_1$. If this difference is nonzero, subtraction gives a monic quadratic and its two residues,
$$
 Q(z)=\frac{P_1(z)-P_2(z)}{T_2-T_1},\qquad
 h_j^2=-\frac{P_i(d_j)}{Q'(d_j)},\qquad u_i=T_i-d_1-d_2 .
 \tag{15}
$$
The cubic terms cancel exactly. The coefficient of $z^2$ in the numerator equals $T_2-T_1$, an immediate sign and normalization check. Both choices of $i$ give the same $h_j^2$ since (15) makes $P_1(d_j)=P_2(d_j)$. Reality, distinctness and positivity are necessary conditions. They are also sufficient for an interpolating pair of Hermitian matrices in the form (13): with the inferred $u_i$ the difference of (14) from the observed monic cubic has degree at most one and vanishes at the two distinct $d_j$. This proves sufficiency for *the pair of spectra*. Many microscopic models with different local charge distributions produce the same pair.

Supply one energy $e$ at a third separation. For $e\ne d_j$ the secular equation solves explicitly for the new diagonal,
$$
 u_3=e-\sum_{j=1}^2\frac{h_j^2}{e-d_j}.
 \tag{16}
$$
All three levels of $H_3$ are now fixed. When $h_1h_2\ne0$, the function $z-u-\sum_jh_j^2/(z-d_j)$ is strictly increasing between its poles, with one root on each of the three open intervals. This identifies which observed energy can be used as $e$ and tests the ordering of a resulting prediction. A root very close to a pole makes (16) sensitive to small errors. The monotonicity also shows that no sign or quadratic-root ambiguity gives another value of $u_3$ in the nondegenerate case. This is a conditional prediction through an avoided crossing with no interpolation in external separation. A full unconditioned prediction of the third triplet would require a law for $u(L)$ or another measurement of its value.

For a state with eigenvalue $E$ the components of its normalized eigenvector in (13) satisfy $c_j=h_j c_S/(E-d_j)$. The spectral weight of the singled-out direction and the separation derivative of a level are therefore
$$
 W(E)=|\langle S|E\rangle|^2
     =\frac{1}{1+\sum_j h_j^2/(E-d_j)^2}
     =\frac{Q(E)}{P_i'(E)},\qquad
 \frac{dE_n}{dL}=W(E_n)u'(L).
 \tag{17}
$$
The second expression for the weight follows either from (14) at a simple zero or from the residue of the $SS$ resolvent. The weights are positive and sum to one, so the total of the three force slopes is $u'(L)$. The weight is also a condition number: $du_3/de=1/W(e)$. A state almost orthogonal to the string provides a poor measurement of its diagonal energy. The label *string weight* in (17) has a precise meaning in a rank-one Hamiltonian. A Wilson-loop overlap depends on the interpolating operators and smearing, and a fraction of the local chromoelectric energy in an internal region depends on a field insertion. Neither measurement is equal to (17) by definition.

### 6.2 When the inverse can fail

For an increasing $u$ a positive rank-one update gives the ordered inequalities
$$
 E_{01}\le E_{02}\le E_{11}\le E_{12}\le E_{21}\le E_{22}.
 \tag{18}
$$
Here the first index is the level $0,1,2$ and the second the separation $1,2$. One proof takes the variational upper bound on the $n$th shifted eigenvalue in the intersection of the span of the lowest $n+2$ original eigenvectors and the plane on which the perturbation vanishes. Monotonicity gives the other inequality. Equivalently, the two sets of secular roots move upwards within the intervals separated by fixed $d_1,d_2$, and the interlacing of a matrix with its compression gives the full chain. Strict inequalities hold for distinct levels and nonzero couplings under a strictly positive update. Measured central values need only satisfy them to the relevant joint precision. A pair that violates interlacing significantly already fails the hypothesis before any roots of a noisy quadratic are taken.

Three complete triplets permit an overdetermined test. The coefficients of $P_i$ must lie on a line in their dependence on $T_i$. Every well-separated pair must reconstruct the same $Q$, hence the same $d_1+d_2$ and $d_1d_2$. Translating every energy by the same constant translates the roots of $Q$ and the string diagonal by that constant and leaves $h_j^2$ unchanged. A different shift at each separation violates this property. On the lattice it is permissible to subtract twice a static-light meson energy measured on the same ensemble. Setting the lowest level individually to zero at each separation destroys the trace information. In data with a fluctuating common reference the correlations from that subtraction must enter any fit, even though a fixed common energy zero cannot affect the physical predictions.

Near-degenerate compressed thresholds require separate treatment. If $d_1=d_2=d$, a rotation in the meson plane produces one dark state of energy $d$ and a second state coupled with strength $\sqrt{h_1^2+h_2^2}$. Only that norm can be recovered from the two moving energies. If instead $d_1\ne d_2$ but one coupling vanishes, one energy remains fixed for all $L$. It supplies no information on $u_3$ if it is the only measured level at the third position. A very small trace difference likewise magnifies the uncertainty in both roots. Trying a longer interval improves the trace lever arm at the cost of a greater chance for the fixed-mixing approximation to break down. The empirical check below uses the preregistered interval and reports residuals rather than tuning separations to minimize them.

The condition (12) pertains to a three-state description. Microscopically the Wilson source has a tower of excitations. Eliminating a fourth state at energy $D$ generates terms of the form $v_i v_j^*/(E-D)$ in the effective three-state resolvent. If $D$ is far enough away and varies slowly, a constant correction can be absorbed in the three-state entries. As $E$ approaches $D$, the induced mixing and diagonal corrections become energy dependent. Then a failure of (15) does not identify which of the neglected states caused it. Increasing the operator basis and testing more levels, or fitting a controlled energy dependence, distinguishes this possibility from a genuine $L$-dependent meson interaction. This matters in QCD, where intermediate multi-hadron states can enter the relevant symmetry sector.

## 8. Measuring a position in the QCD string experiment

### 8.1 A local operator in the same transfer matrix

Construct interpolators $O_S,O_\ell,O_s$ in the static-source sector. The first joins the heavy lines by a spatial transporter. The latter are products of gauge-invariant static-light and static-strange meson operators at the two ends, projected on the same flavour and spatial symmetry channel. Let $C_{ij}(T;L)=\langle O_i(T)O_j^\dagger(0)\rangle$ in a theory with the desired sea content. Spectral analysis at zero temperature uses the factors $e^{-E_nT}$ multiplying products of overlaps. Now specify a local gauge-invariant observable $\mathcal O(\mathbf x,z,\tau)$ in a parent theory with internal volume element $d\mu(z)$. A nonuniform test function $f(z)$ specifies the internal question, and an external mask $m(\mathbf x)$ selects a central slab or a volume containing the whole pair. Define a Euclidean deformation of the action,
$$
 S(h)=S(0)+h\int d\tau\, {\cal I}_{m,f}(\tau),\qquad
 {\cal I}_{m,f}(\tau)=\int d^3x\,m(\mathbf x)
      \int d\mu(z)\, f(z)\,\mathcal O(\mathbf x,z,\tau).
 \tag{19}
$$
All insertions use the same normalization and regulator. Depending on the choice of $\mathcal O$, $h$ can have units. Time-independent support of the spatial probe permits energy differentiation. Support restricted between source and sink gives the same large-$T$ slope after contact and boundary contributions have been controlled. The use of a fixed defect, source or independently specified harmonic makes $f$ physically meaningful. In a generally covariant formulation one needs a relational construction or a specified holographic operator dictionary.

For a ground-state projected correlator $C$, differentiation of the normalized path integral at $h=0$ gives
$$
 -\partial_h\log C=
 \frac{\langle O(T)O^\dagger(0)\int d\tau\,{\cal I}_{m,f}(\tau)\rangle}
      {\langle O(T)O^\dagger(0)\rangle}
 -\left\langle\int d\tau\,{\cal I}_{m,f}(\tau)\right\rangle ,
 \qquad \partial_h E=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{-\partial_h\log C}{T}.
 \tag{20}
$$
The final equality assumes an isolated energy with a nonzero overlap and source renormalizations of subextensive time dependence, apart from matched static-line self-energies. For excited levels the corresponding statement uses derivatives of resolved principal correlators or fits to projected three-point functions in a controlled variational basis. A generic linear combination of interpolators taken to infinite time will select the lowest level onto which it overlaps. A finite, time-independent trial vector with a small ground-state component cannot by itself define the excited-state limit. Degenerate levels require diagonalization of the perturbation within their subspace.

In simulations one can place the insertion time well between the temporal endpoints and look for a plateau, vary the source-to-insertion separations, or sum over insertion times and fit the linear term. Vacuum subtraction removes the identity component of the local operator. Temporal contact contributions associated with the two ends of the correlator can change its overlap. A counterterm living along a static world line can instead grow with $T$ and mimic an energy derivative. A midpoint mask whose support excludes the lines helps remove these local contributions. Comparing with two matched one-meson sectors cancels them for a whole-volume probe provided the line convention and regulator are common. The corresponding practical distinction for a metric source is between a derivative at fixed bare parameters and one taken along a line of constant physics.

Specify also which density is measured. A scalar plaquette insertion or a derivative of the inverse gauge coupling measures a renormalized version of the action response. In a quantum field theory it differs from an energy-density insertion constructed from the properly normalized stress tensor $T_{00}$. Renormalization can mix gluon and quark operators. Neither a single electric component nor the Euclidean action alone equals total energy in an interacting state. The elementary static example (10) is deliberately a case in which the only local field component at the exact midpoint is electric. For QCD an action profile can test if a distribution depends on $f$ even when interpreting its integral as physical energy would be unjustified. To test an *energy* budget one needs the matched stress tensor and the spatial integral, with vacuum and self-energy subtractions. The spatial action-density visualizations of Prkacin et al. supply a concrete precedent for the first experiment in ordinary QCD. [Prkacin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0510051)

### 8.2 Which part follows the unbroken tube?

For an internal probe $f$ and a given state define a screened reference by placing separately measured static-light mesons at the same endpoints. Let their excess expectations over the vacuum be $B_{-,m,f}$ and $B_{+,m,f}$, with the mask kept fixed in physical coordinates. A connected response relative to those screened sources is
$$
 D_{n,m,f}(L)=
 \left.\partial_h E_n(L,h)\right|_0-B_{-,m,f}-B_{+,m,f}.
 \tag{21}
$$
The derivatives and $B$ are all measured relative to the vacuum in the same theory. At finite $L$ residual two-meson forces, pion exchange and finite-volume copies can leave a signal. At large separation and for a fixed central mask the meson tails fall according to the light spectrum, with the usual qualifications near a chiral or massless limit. For a whole-volume derivative the two one-particle energies contribute even at arbitrarily large separation. Subtracting a fitted constant threshold instead of two actual one-meson matrix elements would silently assume its local response. One can use a static-strange reference in a channel dominated by strange mesons and measure the light-strange difference as a control.

A central mask of fixed longitudinal thickness $\Delta$ and transverse extent large enough to capture the tube samples a piece of string. In a long approximately uniform section the local excess integrated over this slab is proportional to $\Delta$. Dividing it by the total source separation $L$ would artificially drive the measured density towards zero. A mask extending along the increasing length of the tube instead gives a leading term proportional to $L$. Extracting a tension response from such a mask needs a fit over an interval where endpoint terms and string breaking can be controlled. An energy derivative with respect to the whole compact metric will generally include a volume-wide coupling change. Equation (19) with a zero-average $f$, a localized $m$, and a separately measured uniform response makes it possible to record both effects. It does not remove all operator mixing or produce a universal shape observable without matching.

The field observable has a particularly transparent expression in the three-channel Hilbert space. If $K_{ij}(m,f)$ are matrix elements of the matched perturbation in that basis and $c_i^{(n)}$ the components of a normalized eigenvector, then
$$
 \partial_h E_n=\sum_i |c_i^{(n)}|^2K_{ii}
       +2\mathop{\rm Re}\sum_{i<j}c_i^{(n)*}K_{ij}c_j^{(n)} .
 \tag{22}
$$
Equation (17) is a special case with the perturbation proportional to the string projector. A local action perturbation can change the transition amplitude itself. At an avoided crossing the interference terms in (22) can be large even for a small perturbation. Interpolating a measured density between a pure tube and two pure mesons using only $W(E)$ omits these terms. In a two-state crossing with equal diagonals and real mixing the eigenvectors are the equal-weight sums and differences. Their local responses are $(K_{SS}+K_{MM})/2\pm{\rm Re}\,K_{SM}$. The two eigenstates have the *same* model string weight, and their spatial matrix elements can be different. Measuring on both levels determines an interference component under these assumptions. This gives a second explicit obstruction to recovering a local profile from string weights.

A measurement can target the research question in stages. First fix the static fundamental source, the allowed screening fields and the common energy subtraction. Verify a window with an appreciable extended component and extract the screened threshold with a meson basis. Then place the same zero-average internal probe on a stable defect or geometric feature and compare its response in a central slab, near an endpoint and in isolated screened mesons. Vary the preparation of the endpoints relative to the feature. A tube-dependent concentration would survive removal of the endpoint-localized background and remain tied to the feature over a central plateau. Measuring it as the eigenstates exchange their extended and screened character would then distinguish a persistent geometric preference from the tail of the external-source profile. A significant response of (5) under a separately matched change of shape would be independent evidence that the geometry affects strong *scales*. Either observation could occur without the other.

This proposal also specifies when an experiment can give a meaningful negative answer. If all profiles translate with the sources within a resolved distance range and vanish after subtracting their weak tails, then localization relative to that feature is bounded at the resolution provided by $f$ and $m$. Higher internal harmonics or another admissible operator may carry a signal which the chosen projection misses. If a stable zero-mode profile exhausts the measurement across string breaking, that result constrains an added concentration specific to the tube. If there is no physically defined map from $z$ to an operator, a null result in four-dimensional QCD puts no bound on displacement in that coordinate. The missing ingredient in the last case is explicit enough to be the target of the next calculation.

## 9. Moving the sources through a confining SU(3) strip

### 9.1 The parent Hamiltonian and the measured operator

We now carry out the experiment of Section 8. Take a spatial strip with one longitudinal direction of $L$ bonds, open boundaries, and an internal interval with two sites, called A and B. There is a rung at every longitudinal vertex. Put a static fundamental and antifundamental at opposite ends, initially on A, then both on B. Links carry $SU(3)$ matrices $U_{uv}$ and electric Casimir $E_e^2$. A group-valued scalar rotor $\Phi_v$ at each site transforms by a local colour rotation on the left and a common flavour rotation on the right. Fundamental and antifundamental rotor excitations screen the heavy sources. This finite Hamiltonian retains the colour representation and the Wilson-string to static-meson transition of interest. It has scalar screening matter, two internal sites and one extended spatial direction. The relation of this geometry to the light-quark dynamics of four-dimensional QCD is a separate matching question.

For an oriented link let $W_e=\Phi_u^\dagger U_{uv}\Phi_v$ and $A_e={\rm Tr}\,W_e+{\rm Tr}\,W_e^\dagger$. We use the unnormalized trace, $C_F=4/3$, and the projector $P_{0,e}$ on the electric singlet of link $e$. The Hamiltonian is
$$
 H=\sum_e j_e\,E_e^2/C_F +m\sum_v J_v^2/C_F
   -t\sum_e\big(A_eP_{0,e}+P_{0,e}A_e\big).
 \tag{24}
$$
Here $J_v^2$ is the rotor Casimir. The projector permits a hop between an empty link and fundamental flux in either direction. It excludes a direct hop between two occupied link representations. We specify this choice to fix the dynamics beyond the electric limit. There is no magnetic plaquette term. A flux segment has an electric cost proportional to length, and the local hopping operator can create a fundamental light pair, move a light endpoint or annihilate a pair. Gauge invariance holds on the full strip, including the static sources. Equation (24) is a strong-electric lattice model. Its hopping strength and row costs are independent parameters. [Hamiltonian lattice gauge theory](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395) · [Strong-coupling string breaking](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9805012)

In common energy units take $j_A=1$, $j_B=2$ on horizontal links and $j_\perp=1.25$ on each rung. The light endpoint costs $m=3.25$. This gives the strip a known feature fixed at A, even when both sources move to B. We also calculate with $j_A=j_B=1$ and with the labels and costs reflected. The measured local operator $N_\alpha$ is $E_e^2/C_F$ on the central horizontal link of row $\alpha$, or the average on the two central links for even $L$. A perturbation $hN_\alpha$ changes only those link coefficients. In the retained electric representations a single fundamental segment has $N_\alpha=1$. For eigenstate $n$ we calculate the reference-subtracted variational diagnostic
$$
 D_{\alpha n}=\langle N_\alpha\rangle_{Q\bar Q,n}
 -\langle N_\alpha\rangle_{Q}
 -\langle N_\alpha\rangle_{\bar Q}
 +\langle N_\alpha\rangle_{\rm vac}.
 \tag{25}
$$
The single-source and vacuum brackets use their ground states on the same strip with the same couplings. Their variational restrictions depend on the source sector, an effect quantified below. The last term accounts for the vacuum present in each single-source bracket. Multiplying this occupation by $j_\alpha$ would give the corresponding electric-energy contribution. We quote the Casimir insertion so that comparing A and B does not fold the imposed factor of two into the answer.

At $t=0$ there is an exact result for the gauge Hamiltonian. Without screening, the support of nontrivial electric flux must connect the sources. Otherwise a cut around one source would violate triality conservation. Every nontrivial link costs at least the fundamental Casimir and the one-line singlet realizes the minimum. From B the shortest routes are the direct one of energy $2L$, and the route via A of energy $L+2.5$. A route spending only part of its length on A cannot improve the latter once it pays for both rungs. Two local mesons have energy $2m=6.5$ and zero midpoint flux. At $L=3$ the minimum for displaced sources is $5.5$ on the route via A. At $L=5$ the minimum is the screened pair at $6.5$ and the extended route has energy $7.5$. Moving one screening endpoint onto its neighbouring rung costs $1.25$, leaving this extended route below that screened excitation. From A, the ground state is extended at both lengths. These energies explain the choice of a source displacement for which pinning and breaking can be distinguished. They were used to register quantitative expectations at finite hopping. ### 9.2 A colour-singlet calculation across the crossing

To compute the finite-$t$ response, retain all simple paths from left to right and every subset of their directed links. Each path crosses any longitudinal cut exactly once, since the cut has two edges and the number crossed is odd. A retained subset consists of one or more segments, or no flux. Cuts create dynamical light endpoints. Multiple cuts, rerouting over rungs and competing unbroken paths are included. We retain one ordered colour contraction for each subset. Extra closed loops, independent flavour contractions and other representations are absent from this variational space. The amplitudes for mixing follow from (24).

For a given ordered list $e_1,\ldots,e_k$, the normalized matrix wavefunction in the pair sector is
$$
 \Psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Phi_L W_{e_1}\cdots W_{e_k}\Phi_R^\dagger .
 \tag{26}
$$
Take the trace over external indices in the inner product. Adjacent transporters on a continuous segment cancel the intermediate rotor factors. At an open end a fundamental rotor remains unless a static source saturates the index. Distinct electric link patterns are orthogonal under normalized Haar measure, and unitarity gives norm one. A gap costs $2m$ in this sector. For a single static source omit the opposite external factor in (26), leaving its flavour index open. Starting from the source side, take the union over both possible terminal rows on the other boundary and all cuts. For the vacuum, sum over both boundary choices and use the global singlet $\mathrm{Tr}(W_{e_1}\cdots W_{e_k})$ for a nonempty list and the unit function for the empty list. Duplicate subsets are counted once in every sector.

Adding a link between fixed factors in (26) has hopping amplitude $-t/3$. In the inner product the neighbouring matrices cancel, and the remaining integral is $\int dW\,\mathrm{Tr}(W^\dagger)\mathrm{Tr}(W)/3=1/3$. In the vacuum sector the first link created out of the unit state has amplitude $-t$ because its normalized wavefunction is the character. Between two nonempty vacuum lists the amplitude is again $-t/3$. The diagonal electric and rotor energies count occupied links and unmatched endpoints. Thus the numerical Hamiltonian is specified with no direct parameter for the conversion of a whole string into two mesons. For displaced sources at $L=5$, the pair, single-source and vacuum matrices have dimensions $6683$, $9149$ and $12555$, respectively. We solve for the first two pair levels and the three reference ground states. The calculation is exact within each of these finite spaces. The energies obey the variational principle. Local expectation values have no corresponding one-sided error bound. [Hamiltonian construction](code/strip.py)

### 9.3 Resolving the transfer rather than drawing a crossing

At $L=4$ with both sources on B the electric cost of the minimal string equals $2m$ at $6.5$. Ordinary virtual hops move that crossing at order $t^2$. To resolve the mixing on a strip with integer lengths we vary $2m$ at this *fixed* separation until the gap between the two lowest levels is minimal. We find its position from equality of the two mass derivatives, which measure the number of light endpoints divided by two. In the ordered-contraction variational space, at the registered hopping the crossing is at $2m_*=6.567329$, with gap $\Delta_{\min}=5.8193\times10^{-6}$. At $2m_*\pm\Delta_{\min}$ both gaps are 1.4141 times the central value at $t=0.45$. The sampled gaps fall towards this stationary point from both ends of the mass interval $[6.15,6.85]$. An evaluation at the unshifted mass of $6.5$ would miss nearly the entire crossover. The length scan in the table held $m$ fixed. This mass scan resolves the narrow avoided crossing that lies between its measurements.

The matrix element responsible for this variational gap can also be derived in the same ordered basis. The minimal path uses the ordered link costs $(1.25,1,1,1,1,1.25)$. Starting from the linkless screened state, every one of the six links must change representation. A traced local hop changes only one. For a nonempty proper subset $S$ of these links already present, let $b(S)$ count the runs of missing links, including runs touching a static endpoint. At the unperturbed crossing $D=2m=6.5$ the intermediate excess energy is
$$
 \delta(S)=\sum_{e\in S} j_e+D[b(S)-1]>0.
 \tag{27}
$$
If all links in a run are filled, adjacent light endpoints annihilate. Filling nonadjacent links first creates additional pairs, with the extra cost recorded by $b(S)$. For each ordering $\pi$ let $S_k$ denote its first $k$ links. Perturbation theory gives the leading minimum gap
$$
 \Delta_{\rm lead}=2(t/3)^6 C,\qquad
 C=\sum_{\pi}\prod_{k=1}^{5}\delta(S_k)^{-1}
   =\frac{71991296}{258084225}=0.27894497\ldots .
 \tag{28}
$$
Every denominator in a given path ordering has the same sign in the resolvent, so the contributions add in magnitude. A subset recursion and an independent sum of all $720$ orders in rational arithmetic give the displayed coefficient. Within the ordered basis the next connecting paths have at least two extra hops since each hop changes the parity of the number of occupied links. Retuning diagonal self-energies also gives a relative correction at order $t^2$. At finite hopping the ratios $\Delta_{\min}/\Delta_{\rm lead}$ are $0.916$, $0.961$ and $0.978$ at $t=0.45,0.30,0.225$. The successive effective exponents are $5.88$ and $5.94$. ### 9.4 A check outside the ordered-line approximation

The zero-hopping comparison is exact in (24) before the variational projection. We can also check the first finite-hopping correction to the local diagnostic without choosing any ordered flavour contractions. Take a central link of cost $j$ away from the sources, on a nondegenerate connected path in the electric limit. A hop can remove its fundamental flux into the singlet with amplitude $-t/3$ and cost $2m-j$. To see the colour factor, projection of the occupied Wilson matrix times the conjugate hopping character gives $\int dU\,U_{ij}\overline{\mathrm{Tr}\,U}=\delta_{ij}/3$. This follows by summing $\int dU\,U_{ij}\bar U_{kl}=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}/3$ over $k=l$. The parallel orientation vanishes by triality. Removing the link saves $j$ and leaves one fundamental rotor of cost $m$ at each end. The normalized raw Casimir changes by the lost occupation probability. In the vacuum there are two orthogonal ways to occupy an empty link, with the fundamental characters of opposite orientations, each of unit norm and energy cost $2m+j$. Their norms and orthogonality follow from $\int|\mathrm{Tr}\,U|^2=1$ and $\int(\mathrm{Tr}\,U)^2=0$. Each links two initially neutral rotor sites, costing $m$ at each end as well as $j$ on the link. The same two states occur at a central empty link far from both screened sources. Hops on other links cancel in the second-order normalized expectation of the local occupation. Combining the reference sectors gives
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 D_{A,\rm string}&=1-\frac{(t/3)^2}{(2m-j_A)^2}
                    -\frac{2t^2}{(2m+j_A)^2}+O(t^4),\\
 D_{A,\rm screened}&=0+O(t^4).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{29}
$$
Here the connected level is followed away from the degeneracy, as at three or five bonds above. At the unused central row the connected-state excess also vanishes through order $t^2$. The absence of odd powers follows from conjugating (24) with the parity of the number of occupied links. Substituting the stated parameters gives $0.99206$ for the connected signal through this order, and zero for the screened signal through the same order. This is an expansion about $t=0$ at fixed nonzero electric gaps. We do not know the $t^4$ remainder at $t=0.45$. The code evaluates the displayed second-order formula arithmetically. A separate finite SU(3) Weyl-matrix sum verifies the Haar moments of degree $(1,1)$ and the centre selection used in the derivation. The disagreement of the small background with the ordered-basis estimate identifies a truncation effect. The transfer of an order-one central signal is already present in the full Hamiltonian at small hopping.

We can repair the reference subtraction exactly on an isolated central link with distant, decoupled screened sources. The three vacuum states are the unit function, $\mathrm{Tr}\,W$ and its conjugate. Hopping connects the first state to each character with amplitude $-t$. The gap before turning on hopping is $D+j$ with $D=2m$. Tensor these states with each external spectator meson. Complete product contractions then give the same local eigenstate in the vacuum, single and pair sectors. For $k=\sqrt{2}t$ its occupation is
$$
 p(k)=\frac12\left[1-\frac{D+j}{\sqrt{(D+j)^2+4k^2}}\right].
 \tag{30}
$$
Consequently the exact subtracted electric density and the interaction energy vanish for this separated problem at every hopping. Keeping only the ordered contraction in sectors with a spectator gives a spurious $p(\sqrt2t)-p(\sqrt2t/3)$ instead. The calculation with one orientation gives the corresponding difference $p(t)-p(t/3)$. With $t=0.45$ the spurious subtracted signals for one and two orientations are $0.003162$ and $0.006250$. Independent matrix diagonalization with the missing products restored removes both residuals. This explains how spectator truncation contaminates very small midpoint values and why the measured order-one transfer is more informative. The separated-link repair does not give a numerical error bar on the whole connected strip. [Colour and subtraction checks](results/strip-local.json)

Equation (24) deliberately supplies a cheaper row and omits spin, magnetic plaquettes and propagation in two further ordinary spatial directions. The calculation determines when the tube gains enough longitudinal energy to pay for two connectors, and whether screening intervenes before that can be seen. An extra coordinate of four-dimensional QCD would additionally need an independently specified operator dictionary and matched dynamics that produce the row preference. There is a definite prediction in the opposite truncation, where only a constant internal gauge connection survives and its kinetic term has known weight $w(z)$. Suppose the matched probe couples only to this gauge operator at the order retained. Then its projection onto four-dimensional QCD factorizes,
$$
 {\cal I}_{m,f}^{(0)}
 =\left(\int d\mu(z)\,w(z)f(z)\right)
   \int d^3x\,m(\mathbf x)\,{\cal O}_{g}^{(4)}(\mathbf x).
 \tag{31}
$$
For $\int w f=0$ its diagonal and transition matrix elements vanish on both the tube and the meson states, regardless of their nonperturbative four-dimensional mixing. An internal profile proportional to $w$ can still be present in a probe with nonzero weighted mean. A tube-specific signal in the orthogonal probe therefore requires a contribution outside this factorized action, such as a matched nonconstant mode or another local operator. The numerical results here apply to the regulated strip. The midpoint profile in Section 8.3 and the light- and strange-channel spectra in Section 7 are the measurements that apply to QCD.

## 10. Conclusion

We identify the QCD string through a rising static-source energy and an extended field distribution over the separations where the flux tube is appreciable. In full QCD its mixing with static-meson pairs makes the assignment of a channel to an eigenstate separation dependent. DeTar et al. reported screening of the finite-temperature static-pair free energy in 1998. Bali et al. resolved the transition to static-light mesons spectroscopically in zero-temperature two-flavour QCD in 2005. The 2019 and 2024 calculations separated light and strange channels and studied their quark-mass dependence. A local action-density study had already followed the field distribution through breaking in ordinary space in 2005. This history provides both a clear physical target for higher-dimensional accounts and concrete measurements against which such accounts can be judged.

The flat-circle example proves that even perfect knowledge of a static force can fail to identify the sign or shape of an internal energy distribution. A specified local probe resolves it in the very same theory. The calculation with measured QCD energies establishes a different positive result. Given the rank-one hypothesis, two triplets of levels determine the two compressed thresholds and the mixing magnitudes, and one additional level predicts the other two at its separation. In lattice units the two differences on N200 are $0.00304$ and $0.00077$. Testing their statistical significance requires correlated data. Under (12) the algebra recovers the spectral weights of the varying direction in channel space. Its identification with a physical string requires the source and meson operators. An operator that resolves an internal position remains an independent ingredient.

We performed the internal-position experiment for a specified confining $SU(3)$ Hamiltonian. Moving both sources off the preferred row leaves its ground-state midpoint occupied over the shorter separation. Beyond breaking, that signal passes to an excited level. At a resolved avoided crossing both levels share it. The homogeneous control moves the midpoint with the sources. The comparison measures an effect of the imposed row costs. It does not derive them from an internal compactification. The small residual background and the failure of the variational energies to subtract cleanly between two distant mesons prevent a precise interpretation of the screened tail. The leading unprojected local response independently resolves the order-one change. In four-dimensional QCD we can already follow the ordinary-space redistribution. Establishing an internal preference there requires a matched parent with a physically defined probe, light-quark dynamics and independent control of its strong scale.

## Appendix B. Operator normalization and a sum-rule check

The uniform derivative in (20) has a familiar antecedent in lattice sum rules for static potentials. Differentiating chosen action coefficients brings down sums of plaquettes with their lattice anisotropy and scale factors. Relating a particular linear combination to energy rather than action involves the response of the regulator coefficients. An energy sum rule can provide a normalization check by comparing an integral over the entire field distribution with an independently measured static energy after subtraction of the static line masses. Agreement of that integral fixes a normalization in one channel. Adding to a distribution a term of zero integral leaves the check intact, so a uniform sum rule cannot specify a nonuniform internal response. The path-integral differentiation used here is the Euclidean form of Feynman–Hellmann theory. Its extension to contact terms, separated insertions and transition matrix elements has a substantial lattice literature. [Michael](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9504016) · [Bouchard et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06963)

For a theory with metric backreaction one must state which parameters define the probe. A derivative of the generating functional with respect to a metric perturbation couples to the stress tensor in a fixed operator prescription. A perturbation of a scalar-dependent gauge kinetic coefficient measures the corresponding action operator. A common change of the size of the internal region generally modifies other sectors and requires retuning sources and quark masses to implement a prescribed line of constant physics. These three measurements can differ already at first order. If the feature being tracked is a geometric neck, for instance, one can define $f$ by a scalar invariant or by proper distance from the neck and specify how that definition is transported under a family deformation. A coordinate interval declared fixed in an arbitrary parametrization would instead respond to a coordinate change. This construction has to exist before a fit of the QCD spectrum can be assigned any geometrical localization.

## Appendix C. Memory and provenance

**Published mechanisms re-derived here.** The relative charge argument of Witten, the compact propagator, the Euclidean source derivative and the determinant of an arrowhead matrix are established ingredients. The original QCD papers measured the static-meson systems and published the spectra. Our checks use the published fit as an implementation control and the reported finite-spacing measurements as data. The coincidence of a wrapped type IIA string and a bound strip in relative homology was already known in 1997 and was recovered by this programme in QCDS-98/1.

**Published work that selected the question.** Lattice studies of walls and of compactified non-Abelian gauge theory motivated the distinction between a localized force and dimensional reduction. The work on compact transverse fluctuations motivated the distinction between an internal profile and a world-sheet mass gap. QCD measurements of a spatial action density and the 2024 holographic computation motivated the use of a local probe on both sides of breaking. No future-dated source or independent model calibration enters the historical calculation.

**Results whose support is the derivation or comparison in this paper.** P28 registers the equal-force, opposite-moment test and its two distance dependences for specified sources. P29 registers the conditional recovery of two held-out levels under (12), applied here to specified N200 markers. Rank-one eigenvalue theory and the physical three-channel Hamiltonian predate this paper. We make no priority claim for either mathematical technique. The inverse and the source calculation narrow the proposed inference from a force to the location of the QCD string. P30 registers the displaced-source signal, the transfer to the first excitation and the six-hop gap in a finite $SU(3)$ strip. These are derived and tested in the model in Section 9. Its imposed anisotropy, truncation background and incomplete QCD matching limit the physical inference. A continuum QCD comparison and a covariance-based spectral test also remain open.

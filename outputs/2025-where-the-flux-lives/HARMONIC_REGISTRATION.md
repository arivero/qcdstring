# P31 · The first meridional mode in the static-source experiment

Registered before solving the signed overlap problem or extracting the other two ensemble panels for this revision. Earlier editions already used the N200 panel and the physical-mass fit. The primary measurements were public in 2024.

Hold the geometry and the colour coupling fixed. On the axial sphere with area measure proportional to f² dt let ε=exp(−b), v(0)=v′(0)=0, p=ε exp(−v), f²=2 exp[−v−ε(exp(−v)−1)] and v″=f²(1−p). The specified matter modes have right probability proportional to exp(p/2) relative to area, and left probabilities proportional to exp(p/2) sqrt(p) exp(±t). Normalize each probability separately. Take the first odd scalar eigenfunction of −φ″=λ f²φ, positive for t>0, and the first positive even one, positive at the equator. Both have unit area mean square.

**Predictions.** The odd mode has zero right-handed overlap. Its signed left-handed overlaps, and the corresponding scalar Yukawa-link overlaps divided by their uniform-source values, are +c and −c with c>0. In the normalized round limit λ₁→1 and c→1/√3. For the even mode the two link overlaps agree. The trace-free light mass source with additive amplitudes (1,−1,2) for the odd profile produces the ordinary (1,1,−2) flavour direction with coefficient c, for the assignments (+,−,−) to up, down and strange. These are specified external insertions in the linear light-mode projection. For a metric-connection external curvature-squared probe the first-mode tree coefficient involves the sum of the two inverse squared octet pole masses. In the two-flavour degenerate case the odd colour insertion is isovector and gives zero between isosinglets, including the two mixed string and meson eigenstates. Record the even channel and the unmatched strange-current contribution.

**Tests.** At b=0.001 compute signed overlaps and eigenvalues by two different solvers, with target absolute agreement 0.0003 on the overlaps and relative agreement 0.0005 on each nonzero eigenvalue. Check the signs, round limit and integrated mode normalization. For QCD report the finite-separation response of each of the three levels relative to twice the static-light mass. Use the outer D200 and N203 ensemble markers of Fig. 4 of Bulava et al. at r/a=17,19,21 if present. Use μ_l=3mπ²/(mπ²+2mK²) as the path coordinate, preferably the corresponding displayed abscissae from the original mass-dependence figure, with the rounded Table 1 masses as a cross-check. Express responses per unit uniform-source calibration in μ_l. Use the N200 points for a linear-interpolation diagnostic, with no assumption of an exactly linear mass dependence. Keep graphical and statistical errors distinct. Missing points and volume or covariance limitations must remain visible. This test uses a spatially integrated flavour scalar. A midpoint response to a colour-field insertion is a different observable.

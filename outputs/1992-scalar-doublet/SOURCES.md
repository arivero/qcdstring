# Sources and chronology for QCDS-92/1

The 1992 argument defines a local Abelian action. The specific supersymmetric electric-confinement embedding appears in the retrospective. The three main numerical inputs are the winding, the dimensionless deformation, and the optional neutral mass detuning. No later mass, residue or splitting slope sets a boundary condition in the solver.

- The background flux and the critical first-order equations follow from the local kinetic terms and energy. The Nielsen–Olesen and de Vega–Schaposnik papers give the historical vortex solution. The 1991 package records the extent of the source readings used in the preceding work. [Nielsen–Olesen](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(73)90350-7) · [de Vega–Schaposnik](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.14.1100) · [Earlier source record](../1991-vortex-pseudoscalar/SOURCES.md)
- Arodź, especially pp. 516–518, gives the localized vector and its flux-overlap precedent. The programme's 1988, 1990 and 1991 articles supply the scalar and antisymmetric polarization conventions. The neutral extension and the derivative of the trace source are calculated in the present action. [Arodź](https://ruj.uj.edu.pl/server/api/core/bitstreams/9c989e24-b7c6-4852-8608-2ed28f1defc6/content) · [1991 pole match](../1991-vortex-pseudoscalar/paper.md)
- Fayet (1976) and Fayet–Iliopoulos (1974) provide historical context for the Abelian multiplet and auxiliary-field terms; their bibliographic records were consulted. The paper displays the full bosonic potential used in the proof. Kato gives the general isolated-spectral-subspace argument. The two-dimensional logarithmic trial and the operator domains are specified directly. [Fayet](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(76)90458-2) · [Fayet–Iliopoulos](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(74)90310-4) · [Kato](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-12678-3)

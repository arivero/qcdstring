# Independent second-line referee report — QCDS-99/S1
**Major revision** — The quadratic spectrum is convincing, but the registered bulk claim, the proposed threshold decay, and the finite-length interpretation require substantive corrections.

## Scientific findings

**Bosons and units.** Equations (5) and (8)–(11) are consistent. Expanding the proper-distance metric gives the smooth cigar period and the quadratic variation of the redshifted tension. Dividing the tension Hessian by the cigar kinetic coefficient gives \(m_c=M_{\rm KK}\) in boundary time. Equation (10) follows for the stated \(p=2,\ldots,6\) soliton ansatz, with \(g_{\tau\tau}=|g_{tt}|f\) and \(g_{UU}=1/(|g_{tt}|f)\) in the chosen coordinates. It is a useful reformulation of the shared warp-factor dependence. The sphere kinetic coefficient is \(R_{\rm tip}^2/(2\pi\ell_s^2)=\lambda/(6\pi)=(9/4)\sigma/M_{\rm KK}^2\). The two translations, charged cigar doublet and four classically massless sphere coordinates account for all eight transverse bosons. The cigar doublet realizes the \(g_{\rm mix}=0\) case of P23, with zero pole residue in the quadratic displacement correlator.

**Fermions.** With the first term in (12) understood as the fully antisymmetrized five-gamma product, the flux contraction gives \(K_a=(f/12)\Gamma_a\Gamma_{(4)}\) off the sphere and \(K_m=-(f/6)\Gamma_m\Gamma_{(4)}\) on it, up to the overall flux orientation. The integrability conditions give \(R_A=6/|f|\) and \(R_S=3/|f|\) in the supersymmetric product used to fix the normalization. The soliton uses that local flux operator and breaks the inherited supersymmetry through its circle boundary condition. In a smooth Cartesian frame the pulled-back spin connection vanishes on the straight tip embedding. The three membrane tangents then give \((f/4)\Gamma_{(4)}\). The \(\kappa\) projection leaves 16 real off-shell components in the \(y\)-independent sector, equivalent to eight two-component Majorana fields. The on-shell kernel has eight physical polarizations. Multiplying \(|f|/4\) by \(r_0/R_A\) gives \(M_{\rm KK}/2\). The spin lift of the cigar rotation gives charges \(\pm\tfrac12\), paired by the Majorana reality condition. Table 6 counts joint eigenspaces on the complexified projected space. The ten-dimensional paragraph identifies the correct surviving flux term but leaves its numerical coefficient implicit.

**Sum rule and threshold.** Equation (17) cancels the logarithmic divergence of the quadratic transverse vacuum energy. A finite one-loop tension shift remains possible. Equation (18) gives an infinite-line tree-level mass coincidence and a match of KK charge. The other charges matter. At a chosen point of \(S^4\), the \(\kappa\) projector ties the sign of the flat transverse spin to the sphere chirality at fixed cigar charge. This follows from the eleven-dimensional volume element, which fixes the product \(\Gamma_{34}\Gamma_{56}\Gamma_{(4)}\) on the projected space. Two same-cigar-charge fermions with opposite flat spins therefore have opposite sphere chiralities and form no \(\mathrm{Spin}(4)\) singlet. With equal flat spins they carry nonzero flat transverse spin. The cigar boson is a singlet of both groups. This excludes the proposed local vertex involving just one cigar boson and two physical fermions in the straight-tip expansion. The separately expanded spin connection and pullback gamma matrices are insufficient evidence for a physical amplitude. Channels involving additional quanta and the radiative shift of the cigar level remain open. At finite \(L\), the NS two-fermion level also has a momentum cost from the half-integer modes.

**Sphere gap.** For \(t=g^2\), the two-loop coefficients in (11) are \(\mu\,dt/d\mu=-(N-2)t^2/(2\pi)-(N-2)t^3/(4\pi^2)+O(t^4)\). They give the power and exponential in (19), and (20) gives the exact gap of the continuum \(O(5)\) model in terms of \(\Lambda_{\overline{\rm MS}}\). Table 1 agrees with the stored evaluation at \(C=1\). A finite one-loop shift of the inverse coupling produces a finite multiplicative shift of the gap, so §6 has no basis for \(C=1+O(g^2)\) when starting from the tree-level coupling. The parametric window is credible given weak matching with a bounded finite shift. Its numerical endpoints are illustrative. The six-field plateau requires \(m_FL\gg1\), \(m_SL\ll1\), and weak running sphere coupling at the scale \(1/L\). For the closed-string expansion one also needs \(L\sqrt{\sigma}\gg1\). The infrared coefficient approaches two after the sphere sector gaps. The equality \(M_{\rm KK}=\sqrt{\sigma}\) does not close the window. Table 5 itself illustrates this.

**Spin structures and finite lengths.** The signs, factors of two, massless limits and large-\(L\) prefactor in (22)–(25) agree with the free determinants and the stored printout. The entries in Tables 2–3 agree with their corresponding stored rows. Integrating the fivebrane entropy law gives the \(-VN^3\beta^{-5}\) action, and comparison of the two exchanged solitons gives (26), with a tie at \(L=2\pi/M_{\rm KK}\). This is a comparison of those classical saddles. Below the crossing, the NS determinant on the \(\tau\)-cigar describes the competing saddle and cannot supply the dominant NS winding-string energy. P21 has a microscopic realization at quadratic probe order on the common \(\tau\)-cigar branch. The energy comparison should subtract the zero-flux vacuum separately in each spin structure, as in QCDS-95/S1. The formal short-length limits are checks of the free sums.

The three-length coefficients \((-\pi,-\pi)\) in the six-field plateau, \((-\pi/3,-\pi/3)\) in the deep infrared and \((0,-\pi/2)\) for the stated 1995 branch are correct leading coefficients. The finite-length test is still missing. The ledger prescribes an estimator at \(L,2L,4L\), and the tabulated rows supply no complete triplet on the common dominant branch. The mass-dependent contamination of the estimator can be obtained from the same determinants used for Tables 2–3.

**Bulk spectrum and P32.** Equation (27) follows from the minimally coupled operator in (4). The stated tip and ultraviolet powers are correct, with the regular constant branch selected over the logarithm at \(n=0\). Table 4 follows the stored roots and uses \(M/M_{\rm KK}=\sqrt{M^2}/3\), including for \(n=2\). The neutral change of variable agrees with the second-line benchmark. The lower bound in this sector is stronger than the numerical check. The radial quadratic form for \(M^2-9n^2\) has the positive derivative term and the potential \(9n^2r^3/(r^6-1)\). Thus a normalizable charged mode in this operator has \(M>|n|M_{\rm KK}\).

The printed Eq. (3.3) of [3] is reproduced by multiplying the radial kinetic coefficient and weight of the neutral minimal operator by \(r^3\) before expanding the derivative and dividing out that factor. This gives \(10r^6-4\) in place of \(7r^6-1\). With the coordinate \(\rho\propto r^2\) of [3], the ratio of the measures is \(\rho^{3/2}\), proportional to \(e^{2\phi}\). It is the ratio of the unweighted string-frame measure to the \(e^{-2\phi}\)-weighted measure. The agreement of the two printed mass ratios at the precision of [3] is a numerical control for that operator.

Section 8 establishes a kinematic exclusion for emission in the computed polarization. The registered P32 clause concerns **the lightest bulk scalar of unit charge**. Even if a charged scalar is shown to obey (27), that supplies one scalar tower and leaves the lower bound on the other coupled scalar sectors open. The all-clauses-reached statements therefore overstate the registered result. The tensor benchmark explains a massive normalizable polarization of the compactification, while the fermion calculation gives a physical consequence of its supersymmetry-breaking boundary condition. These are positive results for the second line. The full tensor operator and coupling dictionary, the charged bulk spectrum and the pure four-dimensional polarized spectrum remain open.

**String units and the comparisons.** The conversions in Table 5 and the stored P08 values \(\lambda\simeq8.50\) and \(4.34\) are consistent. The first value places the scalar in the degenerate minimal tower at the scalar target. Its identification with the lightest scalar is unestablished. The two values illustrate the failure to reach both target ratios with that degenerate pair in a controlled regime. The pseudoscalar target is untested. The \(4\pi/L\) curve is a two-phonon benchmark at zero longitudinal momentum. Comparing infinite-line masses to that curve does not establish a finite-volume ordering or a sphere continuum starting at \(m_S\).

Equation (28) is sound as a statement about two plateaux of the closed-string Casimir coefficient, after the relevant energy subtractions. The open-string coefficient requires a separate boundary argument. The bulk spectrum alone gives neither the boundary conditions of the internal fields nor the massive reflection spectrum. The three-dimensional \(Z_2\) value \(0.99(3)(9)\) is quoted correctly for the fit and its tested extra-field hypotheses. The four-dimensional \(SU(2)\) comparison contains a factual error. QCDS-78/1 §6 reports that the fixed \(\pi/12\) fit was acceptable at \(\beta=2.5\) only, and leaves the Casimir share of the fitted \(1/R\) term unresolved. These measurements are useful period comparisons with their respective dimensionality, geometries and fit assumptions attached.

**Historical and novelty judgments.** The dated inputs identified in the submitted derivations are available by the cutoff according to the source record. The modern checks and Appendix D comparisons should remain verification and retrospective material. The bosonic D4 result is already credited to [5], and the general soliton identity is a compact extension. The D4 fermion spectrum is the strongest result of the historical reconstruction. Appendix D should distinguish the November 1999 D3 example from the later full D4 result. The sum rule and threshold equality are consequences of the spectrum. The application of the \(O(5)\) gap is a plausible distinct synthesis with incomplete matching and open literature priority. The saddle comparison is a direct application of the fivebrane entropy law. The P21 realization and the charged tensor levels are specific additions to the programme. These judgments concern the supplied source record and do not establish external priority.

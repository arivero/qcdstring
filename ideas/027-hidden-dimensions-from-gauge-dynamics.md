# 027. Hidden dimensions from gauge dynamics: two decoupling conditions
**Status:** in progress
**Depends on:** 002, 004, 020, 022
**Predictions:** P18 bears on the worldsheet condition; no compactification prediction registered

## Claim

The special weak-coupling ultraviolet status of four-dimensional Yang-Mills
can motivate suppressing extra-dimensional propagation above the confining
scale. It does not prove that only free theories exist for D > 4 or that
compactification is necessary. A viable realization needs both a
four-dimensional gauge regime and a flux tube without unwanted light
internal modes. These are distinct conditions.

## Available ingredients by 1986

* Engineering dimension [g_D] = (4-D)/2 and four-dimensional asymptotic freedom.
* Wilsonian scaling: negative mass dimension about the Gaussian point is not
  a theorem excluding every interacting continuum fixed point.
* A formal 4+epsilon countertest gives beta(g_hat) = epsilon g_hat/2 - b g_hat^3
  and a candidate ultraviolet fixed point; this does not establish D = 5.
* Fu-Nielsen (1984): an anisotropic compact U(1) layer construction with
  Coulomb behaviour within layers and strong coupling across them. It is not
  isotropic non-Abelian dimensional selection.
* In a simple product compactification, g4^2 = gD^2/V before thresholds.
  If unwanted zero modes are lifted, weak matching permits
  Lambda4 << M_KK << Lambda_UV. Compactification does not remove the parent
  ultraviolet problem; torus components of the gauge field include scalar
  zero modes that must be dealt with.
* A free compact target scalar still has local worldsheet central charge one.
  Small target radius is not itself a worldsheet gap.
* Section 8.4 now supplies a periodic pinning model with stiffness kappa =
  sigma r^2 and tree mass squared u/kappa. Its weak cosine has scaling dimension
  1/(4 pi kappa), so shrinking r does not guarantee quantum localization.
  For this minimal model, weak pinning cannot remain relevant at a
  parametrically large M_KK/sqrt(sigma); strong or differently matched models
  are not excluded.

## What the memory does and does not contribute

Later geometric descriptions of confinement motivate the question but do not
supply a background or gap here. No remembered string mass or modern proof
enters the derived matching conditions. No exhaustive novelty claim is made.

## Test

1. Specify a background or anisotropic gauge action that suppresses propagation
   in the additional directions; calculate the suppression scale.
2. Stabilize its radius or localization scale and identify every surviving
   scalar, fermion and gauge zero mode.
3. Demonstrate a four-dimensional strong-coupling scale separated from the
   extra-dimensional and ultraviolet scales, with stated matching errors.
4. Compute the internal fluctuation operator of its long tube. Require the
   unwanted modes to be gapped while preserving transverse translations.
5. Check anomalies and physical projections before assigning critical-string
   internal coordinates a hadronic interpretation.

## Expected outcome, and what would refute it

An explicit example meeting both decoupling requirements would support that
construction. An unavoidable light internal mode, absence of a stable
suppression scale, inconsistent anomaly matching, or inseparable gauge and
compactification scales would reject the proposed realization. None would
prove that every possible realization fails. Power counting alone cannot
select exactly four dimensions over lower-dimensional interacting theories.

# 013. A pseudoscalar lives on the four-dimensional worldsheet
**Status:** open
**Depends on:** 012
**Predictions:** P04

## Claim
In d = 4 the two transverse modes of the string carry a U(1) charge
under rotations about the string axis, and the worldsheet admits a
topological density: the self-intersection number, built from
epsilon^{ab} epsilon_{ij} d_a d_c X^i d^c d_b X^j. A confining SU(N)
flux tube carries one extra light worldsheet particle, a pseudoscalar
coupled to that density with a strength Q near 0.37 and a mass near
1.85 sqrt(sigma). It appears in the closed-tube spectrum as an
anomalously light 0^{--} level and shifts all pseudoscalar-channel
levels. In d = 3 there is no such particle and the spectrum is pure
Nambu-Goto to the measured accuracy.

## Known in 1974
* Nothing. The closest 1974 object is the theta-angle of the gauge
  theory, which does not exist yet either (instantons arrive in 1975).

## What the memory says
* Athenodorou-Bringoltz-Teper 2010: lattice closed flux tubes in SU(3)
  and SU(5) with one level, 0^{--}, far from any Nambu-Goto prediction.
* Dubovsky-Flauger-Gorbenko 2013: that level is a resonance of a
  pseudoscalar coupled to the transverse modes; fit gives the mass and
  Q. Dubovsky-Gorbenko 2015: Q = sqrt(7/(16 pi)) = 0.373 makes the
  one-loop worldsheet theory integrable; the lattice value is within
  errors of it.
* Athenodorou-Teper 2017: direct measurement, mass about 1.85 sqrt(sigma)
  in SU(3), mildly N-dependent.
* The pseudoscalar is plausibly the worldsheet remnant of the gauge
  theory's topological charge density, i.e. the eta' of the string.

## Test
1. `[pencil]` Write the most general low-energy action for two
   transverse bosons plus a pseudoscalar with the symmetries of the
   problem; identify the unique leading coupling and its relation to the
   (26-d) term of 012.
2. `[pencil]` Argue for the coupling's value from the anomaly in the
   worldsheet axial U(1) when the string is embedded in the gauge
   theory. This is a conjecture in the memory and would be a result here.
3. `[lattice, later]` Closed-tube spectra in d = 3 versus d = 4 with the
   same statistics; the difference is the prediction.

## Expected outcome, and what would refute it
A 0^{--} level near 1.85 sqrt(sigma) in d = 4 and none in d = 3.
Refuted if the d = 4 spectrum is pure Nambu-Goto, or if the extra state
is a scalar rather than a pseudoscalar.

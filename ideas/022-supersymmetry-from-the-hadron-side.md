# 022. Supersymmetry from the hadron side
**Status:** open
**Depends on:** 016, 003
**Predictions:** P16, P17

## Claim
Spacetime supersymmetry is what the superstring needs in flat ten
dimensions to lose its tachyon. For the QCD string the same structure
appears in two weaker, testable forms. First, at large N the bosonic
sector of QCD with one Dirac flavour in the two-index antisymmetric
representation coincides with that of N=1 super Yang-Mills (planar
equivalence, Armoni-Shifman-Veneziano 2003); for N = 3 the antisymmetric
representation is the antifundamental, so this is one-flavour QCD. The
supersymmetric string duals therefore describe a cousin of QCD up to
1/N. Second, hadronic supersymmetry: the diquark is the superpartner of
the antiquark, baryon and meson trajectories pair (016), and the
Regge slopes of mesons and baryons are degenerate for the same reason
(Armoni-Patella 2009). A tachyon-free non-supersymmetric string must
in any case have an asymptotically Bose-Fermi degenerate spectrum
(Kutasov-Seiberg 1991), so mesons and baryons must share their
Hagedorn slope.

## Known in 1974
* Miyazawa 1966, 1968 (local copy of 1966, `phys4/sources/miyazawa-1966.pdf`).
* Hagedorn's spectrum, without separating mesons from baryons.

## What the memory says
* Armoni-Shifman-Veneziano 2003: planar equivalence; "SUSY relics in
  one-flavour QCD".
* Armoni-Patella 2009: meson-baryon slope degeneracy in the orientifold
  theory, with the baryon as a string ending on a... the baryon of the
  orientifold theory is a string with an antisymmetric-quark end.
* Kutasov-Seiberg 1991; Dienes 1994: misaligned supersymmetry.
* Broniowski-Florkowski 2000 fit different Hagedorn temperatures to
  mesons and baryons from the PDG tables; a 2004 update finds them
  closer. The question is open in the memory and is a 1974 computation.
* Catto-Gursey 1985, 1988 (local copies in `phys4/sources`);
  Brodsky-de Teramond-Dosch 2015 (local copy).
* A 2024 letter, Rivero, "An interpretation of scalars in SO(32)"
  (EPJC 84, 1058; local copy `phys4/sources/rivero-so32-2024.pdf`),
  reads the SO(32) string's scalar sector as the diquark and meson
  content of a five-flavour hadronic supersymmetry. Single-author,
  recorded as a lead, to be examined on the same footing as the others.

## Test
1. `[code]` Separate the 1974 meson and baryon tables; fit the
   cumulative level density of each to exp(m/T); compare slopes (P16).
2. `[pencil]` Planar equivalence at the level of the string: the
   orientifold projection on the worldsheet maps the adjoint fermion to
   the antisymmetric one; show the bosonic sectors coincide at leading
   order in 1/N.
3. `[lattice, later]` SU(N) with one antisymmetric Dirac flavour versus
   N=1 SYM at N = 3, 4, 6: glueball and meson masses (P17).

## Expected outcome, and what would refute it
Equal Hagedorn slopes within errors once the tables are complete to the
same mass; lattice degeneracies at the percent level by N = 6. Refuted
by a robust difference in slopes, or by O(1) differences between the
orientifold theory and SYM at N = 6.

# 008. Quarks at the ends: massive endpoints and string breaking
**Status:** open
**Depends on:** 001, 003
**Predictions:** P12

## Claim
Mesons are open strings whose endpoints carry mass, spin, flavour and
colour charge. A rotating string with endpoint masses m has a Regge
trajectory that bends at low J,

    J = alpha' E^2 [ 1 - c (m/E)^{3/2} + ... ],  c of order 4 to 5,

so strange and charmed trajectories deviate from straight lines in a
calculable way. A long string between static sources breaks when
sigma R exceeds the energy of a light quark pair at its ends, at about
R_b ~ 1.2-1.3 fm, and the same breaking, applied to a string stretched
by a fast quark pair, is the mechanism of hadron production in jets.

## Known in 1974
* Chan-Paton factors; Bardakci-Halpern quarks; Nambu 1974 puts quarks
  at the ends of the flux tube explicitly.
* Chodos-Thorn 1974: the string with massive endpoints, classically.
* Artru-Mennessier 1974: multiparticle production from a breaking
  string.
* Casher-Kogut-Susskind 1974: pair creation screens the linear potential
  in 1+1 dimensions.
* Jets in e+e- are expected from the parton model (and will be seen at
  SPEAR next year).

## What the memory says
* Bardeen-Bars-Hanson-Peccei 1976: quantisation of the string with
  massive ends.
* Lund model 1983: string fragmentation as the standard hadronisation
  picture; it works.
* Sonnenschein-Weissman 2014, Sonnenschein 2016: massive-endpoint fits to
  the whole meson and baryon table, with endpoint masses close to
  constituent values and a universal slope.
* Bali et al. 2005: string breaking observed on the lattice at 1.25 fm.
* Sakai-Sugimoto 2004: chiral symmetry and its breaking come from the
  surfaces on which the ends live joining in the interior. This is the
  part that has no 1974 language yet.

## Test
1. `[pencil]` Rederive the classical trajectory of a rotating string with
   massive ends and the small-mass expansion; get c.
2. `[pheno]` Fit the K*, phi and (from next week) the charmonium and
   D* trajectories; extract m_s, m_c. Compare with the constituent-quark
   values 0.5 and 1.5 GeV.
3. `[pencil]` Pair-creation rate in a flux tube from the Schwinger
   formula with the chromoelectric field sigma; predict R_b and the
   multiplicity per unit rapidity in jets.
4. `[lattice]` Later: V(R) with dynamical quarks flattens at R_b.

## Expected outcome, and what would refute it
Trajectory bending of the right sign and size; endpoint masses near the
constituent values; R_b near 1.2 fm. Refuted if the strange and charmed
trajectories need a different slope, or if endpoint masses come out far
from constituent values with no explanation.

# 010. Glueballs are closed strings
**Status:** open
**Depends on:** 003, 006
**Predictions:** P08

## Claim
The pure gauge theory has a spectrum of flavourless states, the closed
strings, with masses set by sqrt(sigma): the lightest scalar near
3.3-3.7 sqrt(sigma), the tensor near 4.8-5.3, the pseudoscalar near
5.0-5.5. With sqrt(sigma) = 0.44 GeV that is about 1.5-1.6, 2.1-2.3 and
2.2-2.4 GeV. Their interactions with mesons are suppressed by 1/N, so
they are narrow and mix weakly. Their leading Regge trajectory is the
Pomeron (006).

## Known in 1974
* Nothing is observed. Gluons exist in the theory (Fritzsch, Gell-Mann,
  Leutwyler), and there is no reason their bound states should be absent.
  The MIT bag predicts gluon bound states too.
* The closed-string sector of the dual model exists (Shapiro-Virasoro)
  and its lowest states, after the tachyon, are at the Pomeron
  intercept.

## What the memory says
* Freund-Nambu 1975, Fritzsch-Minkowski 1975: glueballs proposed.
* Isgur-Paton 1985: glueballs as closed flux loops in a flux-tube model.
* Lattice, 1998-2021 (Teper; Lucini-Teper-Wenger; Athenodorou-Teper):
  the numbers above, stable in N, with 1/N^2 corrections.
* Witten 1998: glueball masses from normal modes of a capped warped
  geometry; the ordering 0^{++} < 2^{++} < 0^{-+} comes out.
* Experimentally the scalar sector near 1.5-1.7 GeV (f_0(1500),
  f_0(1710)) is where the lightest glueball hides, mixed with q qbar.

## Test
1. `[lattice]` In Phase 2, SU(2) in d = 3 first (cheap); then SU(2) and
   SU(3) in d = 4. Correlators of plaquette operators; masses in units of
   sqrt(sigma) measured on the same lattice.
2. `[pencil]` Closed-string spectrum in the warped geometry with a wall:
   a Schroedinger problem in the size coordinate; compare the ordering.
3. `[pheno]` Where to look in 1974 data: isoscalar 0^{++} and 2^{++}
   states produced in gluon-rich channels (central production,
   radiative decays of the new heavy vector once it exists).

## Expected outcome, and what would refute it
Masses in the ranges above with the stated ordering. Refuted by a
lattice scalar below 2 sqrt(sigma) or above 5, or by a different
ordering.

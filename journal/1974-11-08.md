# 8 November 1974

A second group, working from the same premise without seeing this
repository, has sent its notebook: fourteen tests, three calculation
scripts, a set of standards. It is filed unmodified in `second_line/`.
All three scripts reproduce here.

They caught a real fault. Section 2.3 of the thesis said the massless
spin-2 state "is" the Pomeron. Weinberg's soft theorem of 1964 forbids
that reading in flat space: a massless helicity-2 particle with a
nonzero coupling couples universally, whatever it is called. The
thesis now says what it should have said: the claim concerns the
background. In a confining geometry the tensor equation has no
normalizable zero mode, the lowest tensor is a massive glueball, and
the Pomeron is the trajectory through such states. Their D4
calculation gives 1.5669 M_KK for the first of them. A different method
here gives the same number to ten digits, and the next three.

They are stricter about evidence, and that rule is adopted: a
remembered number may choose the next calculation and may sit in the
ledger as a dated prediction; it is never an input and never support.

They also bring targets this programme did not have: a single heavy
quark, a finite number of families, and the distance between the QCD
and Fermi scales. All three are now ideas, 024 to 026. Their honest
result on families is the interval three to eight, with three the
smallest member, under a truncation they state. The connection to the
flux tube is still missing, and both lines say so.

One number came from putting the two notebooks side by side. Inside the
D4 geometry, the remembered ratio of the tensor glueball to the string
tension needs a 't Hooft coupling near 4. The geometry is reliable only
for large coupling. Their "Kaluza-Klein obstruction" and our long march
meet at that number.

# Sources and their role

The entries below were checked against publisher, author or arXiv records while preparing this folder. No later experimental or lattice measurements are used as evidence. Post-1974 sources supply theoretical knowledge permitted by the counterfactual.

## Available by the end of 1974

**H1 — G. 't Hooft (1974), “A planar diagram theory for strong interactions.”**
Nuclear Physics B 72, 461–473.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(74)90154-0);
[author-hosted paper](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~hooft101/gthpub/planar_diagram_theory.pdf).
Role: color topology and the large-N starting point. It does not by itself construct the continuum string of four-dimensional Yang–Mills.

**H2 — K. G. Wilson (1974), “Confinement of quarks.”**
Physical Review D 10, 2445.
[Publisher](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.10.2445).
Role: lattice formulation, strong-coupling confinement and Wilson-loop observables. A strong-coupling lattice result alone is not a proof of confinement in the four-dimensional continuum limit.

**H3 — S. Weinberg (1964), “Photons and Gravitons in S-Matrix Theory: Derivation of Charge Conservation and Equality of Gravitational and Inertial Mass.”**
Physical Review 135, B1049.
[Publisher](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.135.B1049).
Role: assumptions and conclusion of the soft-spin-2 coupling argument.

**H4 — T. Yoneya (1974), “Connection of Dual Models to Electrodynamics and Gravidynamics.”**
Progress of Theoretical Physics 51, 1907–1920.
[Publisher](https://academic.oup.com/ptp/article/51/6/1907/1864759).
Role: the gravitational zero-slope limit is already an obstacle known in our chosen year.

**H5 — J. Scherk and J. H. Schwarz (1974), “Dual models for non-hadrons.”**
Nuclear Physics B 81, 118–144.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(74)90010-8).
Role: gauge and gravitational limits of ordinary dual models. The programme accepts those calculations while choosing a different physical research target.

**H6 — S. J. Brodsky and G. R. Farrar (1973), “Scaling Laws at Large Transverse Momentum.”**
Physical Review Letters 31, 1153.
[Publisher](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.31.1153);
[author manuscript record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/cs1y9-ezk42).
Role: hard-scattering constituent-counting diagnostic, subject to its assumptions. The longer 1975 treatment is not counted as a 1974 source.

**H7 — D. J. Gross and F. Wilczek (1973), “Ultraviolet Behavior of Non-Abelian Gauge Theories.”**
Physical Review Letters 30, 1343. [Publisher](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.30.1343).
Role: asymptotic freedom; the colour running used in the new conditional calculations.

**H8 — H. D. Politzer (1973), “Reliable Perturbative Results for Strong Interactions?”**
Physical Review Letters 30, 1346. [Publisher](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.30.1346).
Role: independent contemporary foundation for the perturbative strong-interaction beta function.

**H9 — P. Ramond (1971), “Dual Theory for Free Fermions.”**
Physical Review D 3, 2415. [Publisher](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.3.2415).
Role: fermionic string sectors and the Clifford-algebra route to spacetime spinors.

**H10 — J.-L. Gervais and B. Sakita (1971), “Field theory interpretation of supergauges in dual models.”**
Nuclear Physics B 34, 632–639. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0550321371903518).
Role: two-dimensional field interpretation of the early supergauge structure. Bibliographic record checked; publisher full text was inaccessible in this session.

**H11 — J. Wess and B. Zumino (1974), “Supergauge transformations in four dimensions.”**
Nuclear Physics B 70, 39–50. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0550321374903551).
Role: four-dimensional global supersymmetry, distinct from gauged spacetime supersymmetry and its gravitational sector.

## Modern theoretical imports

**M1 — J. Polchinski and A. Strominger (1991), “Effective string theory.”**
Physical Review Letters 67, 1681.
[Publisher](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.67.1681).
Role: a systematic route to noncritical long-string effective dynamics.

**M2 — O. Aharony and Z. Komargodski (2013), “The Effective Theory of Long Strings.”**
[arXiv:1302.6257](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.6257).
Role: universality, corrections and the limitations of extrapolating a long-string formula.

**M3 — N. Seiberg (1997), “Matrix Description of M-theory on T5 and T5/Z2.”**
Physics Letters B 408, 98–104.
[arXiv:hep-th/9705221](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9705221).
Role: nonlocal six-dimensional nongravitational theories with string-like excitations.

**M4 — O. Aharony (1999 preprint; 2000 publication), “A brief review of ‘little string theories’.”**
[arXiv:hep-th/9911147](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9911147).
Role: an explicit summary of the NS5 decoupling prescription and its qualifications. This is a review, used alongside the original construction M3.

**M5 — N. Seiberg, L. Susskind and N. Toumbas (2000), “Strings in Background Electric Field, Space/Time Noncommutativity and A New Noncritical String Theory.”**
[arXiv:hep-th/0005040](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0005040).
Role: the near-critical electric-field string limit.

**M6 — R. Gopakumar, J. Maldacena, S. Minwalla and A. Strominger (2000), “S-Duality and Noncommutative Gauge Theory.”**
[arXiv:hep-th/0005048](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0005048).
Role: decoupled open-string description and the nonplanar one-loop closed-pole test. Its explicit amplitude is a benchmark to reproduce, not a calculation completed in this folder.

**M7 — J. M. Maldacena (1997 preprint; 1998 publication), “The Large N Limit of Superconformal Field Theories and Supergravity.”**
[arXiv:hep-th/9711200](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9711200).
Role: a precise setting in which nongravitational field-theory observables have a bulk string/gravity description. Its conformal example is not pure confining Yang–Mills.

**M8 — A. Polyakov (1997 preprint; 1998 publication), “String Theory and Quark Confinement.”**
[arXiv:hep-th/9711002](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9711002).
Role: loop equations, zigzag symmetry and renormalization as constraints on a gauge-theory string. This supplies additional tests for a future microscopic construction, not a finished QCD string.

**M9 — J. Polchinski and M. J. Strassler (2001 preprint; 2002 publication), “Hard Scattering and Gauge/String Duality.”**
Physical Review Letters 88, 031601.
[arXiv:hep-th/0109174](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0109174).
Role: how warped-space string calculations can give hard physical amplitudes in specified confining dual models.

**M10 — C. G. Callan, D. Friedan, E. J. Martinec and M. J. Perry (1985), “Strings in background fields.”**
Nuclear Physics B 262, 593–609.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(85)90506-1).
Role: background-field equations from worldsheet conformal invariance.

**M11 — S. Weinberg and E. Witten (1980), “Limits on massless particles.”**
Physics Letters B 96, 59–62.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(80)90212-9).
Role: a separate obstruction to particular emergent massless higher-spin claims; audit its hypotheses rather than apply the name as a universal prohibition.

**M12 — C. Beasley, J. J. Heckman and C. Vafa (2008 preprint; 2009 publication), “GUTs and Exceptional Branes in F-theory — I.”**
[arXiv:0802.3391](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3391).
Role: local gauge/matter/Yukawa construction as a starting laboratory. A local patch is not our complete nongravitational finite-tension model.

**M13 — J. J. Heckman and C. Vafa (2008 preprint; 2010 publication), “Flavor Hierarchy From F-theory.”**
[arXiv:0811.2417](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.2417).
Role: leading rank-one flavour proposal. Read with M14 for the distinction between flux effects on wavefunctions and effects on holomorphic Yukawa rank.

**M14 — S. Cecotti, M. C. N. Cheng, J. J. Heckman and C. Vafa (2009 preprint; 2010 version), “Yukawa Couplings in F-theory and Non-Commutative Geometry.”**
[arXiv:0910.0477](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0477).
Role: section 3.3 supplies the local rank theorem and the gauge-flux qualification; section 4 studies three-form-induced changes. The numerical perturbation in our code is not a calculation of these string corrections.

**M15 — K. Ishiguro, T. Kai, S. Nishimura, H. Otsuka and M. Takeuchi (2023 preprint; 2024 publication), “Upper bound on the Atiyah-Singer index from tadpole cancellation.”**
JHEP 01 (2024) 200. [arXiv:2308.12421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12421); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12421v2).
Role: generation-index bounds in specified compact heterotic and Type IIB/F-theory settings. Some estimates use observed couplings; those numbers are not imported. Our separate uniform-limit test remains unproved in a physical construction.

**M16 — B. Pendleton and G. G. Ross (1981), “Mass and mixing angle predictions from infra-red fixed points.”**
Physics Letters B 98, 291–294. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(81)90017-4).
Role: antecedent for constant gauge–Yukawa ratios. The explicit N_g-dependent algebra and numerical integration in this package start from the beta functions printed in our note; no novelty claim is made for the interval obtained from them. Bibliographic identity verified; publisher full text was unavailable in this session.

**M17 — C. Quigg and R. Shrock (2009), “Gedanken Worlds without Higgs: QCD-Induced Electroweak Symmetry Breaking.”**
Physical Review D 79, 096002. [arXiv:0901.3958](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.3958); [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.79.096002).
Role: QCD-induced weak breaking as a control, with an order parameter at the hadronic scale.

**M18 — W. A. Bardeen, C. T. Hill and M. Lindner (1990), “Minimal dynamical symmetry breaking of the standard model.”**
Physical Review D 41, 1647. [Publisher](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.41.1647).
Role: top condensation and compositeness boundary conditions as an alternative mechanism. No numerical prediction from that model is adopted here.

**M19 — C. Quigg (1997), “Hadron Colliders, the Top Quark, and the Higgs Sector.”**
[arXiv:hep-ph/9707508](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9707508).
Role: antecedent for the top-threshold influence on hadronic scales. The 2/27 relation was also checked in the author lecture material in [CERN-2006-001](https://cds.cern.ch/record/585537/files/CERN-2006-001.pdf), equations 108–111. We derive the threshold formula independently and state what is held fixed; later experimental values in these sources are excluded.

**M20 — F. Gliozzi, J. Scherk and D. I. Olive (1977), “Supersymmetry, supergravity theories and the dual spinor model.”**
Nuclear Physics B 122, 253–290. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0550321377902061).
Role: the spacetime-supersymmetric projection and low-energy interpretation. Publication record and abstract checked; full publisher text was inaccessible in this session.

**M21 — L. Alvarez-Gaumé, P. Ginsparg, G. Moore and C. Vafa (1986), “An O(16) × O(16) heterotic string.”**
Physics Letters B 171, 155–162. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0370269386915248).
Role: a tachyon-free tree-level construction without spacetime supersymmetry. It retains gravity and is not our proposed nongravitational physical model.

**M22 — L. J. Dixon and J. A. Harvey (1986), “String theories in ten dimensions without spacetime supersymmetry.”**
Nuclear Physics B 274, 93–105. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/055032138690619X).
Role: non-supersymmetric modular constructions and the positive one-loop vacuum energy; tree-level tachyon freedom does not establish a quantum vacuum.

**M23 — E. Witten (1998), “Anti-de Sitter Space, Thermal Phase Transition, And Confinement In Gauge Theories.”**
[arXiv:hep-th/9803131](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9803131).
Role: a common geometrical setting for confinement and supersymmetry breaking by a spatial-circle spin structure; the weak-coupling/pure-Yang–Mills limit is a separate unresolved control problem.

**M24 — R. C. Brower, S. D. Mathur and C.-I Tan (2000), “Glueball Spectrum for QCD from AdS Supergravity Duality.”**
Nuclear Physics B 587, 249–276. [arXiv:hep-th/0003115](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0003115).
Role: the T4 tensor radial equation and its spectrum. Our calculation reproduces the lowest mode, converting their rounded squared eigenvalue 22.097 to m/M_KK≈1.5669. No lattice or measured masses from the paper are used.

**M25 — T. Sakai and S. Sugimoto (2004 preprint; 2005 publication, corrected version 2007), “Low energy hadron physics in holographic QCD.”**
Progress of Theoretical Physics 113, 843–882. [arXiv:hep-th/0412141](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0412141).
Role: explicit D4 metric and parameter conventions in section 3. Our benchmark uses that background without adding the paper's flavor D8-branes.

## Verification boundary

Bibliographic identities and the research claims used here were checked on primary publication records or author-hosted material, with the original NCOS discussion and little-string prescription inspected in their full-text PDFs. This initial package is not a line-by-line reproduction of every source.

The hard-wall tensor problem is derived from explicitly stated assumptions in our own note. Its result is not attributed to the original AdS/CFT paper as though that paper constructed this wall model.

For version 0.2, publication records and relevant full-text passages were checked for the added mechanisms, especially the flux/rank distinction and the hypotheses behind family bounds. M16 is identified bibliographically rather than claimed to have been fully inspected. The rank perturbation, positive quadratic budget, one-loop flow and matching results are explicitly delimited calculations; none is attributed to a paper as a completed realization of the whole research programme.

Version 0.3 checks primary records for the supersymmetry distinctions and full-text equations in M23–M25 for the smooth tensor benchmark. The radial eigenvalue is reproduced numerically and independently checked through its quadratic form. Worldsheet Casimir coefficients and supersymmetry multiplet counting are elementary controls derived with explicit assumptions. No proof of microscopic necessity, realistic supersymmetry breaking or a complete five-target construction is claimed.

# What the theory should explain

Version 0.3 — 6 November 2026. Counterfactual research date: end of 1974.

The programme has five central explanatory targets: an exceptionally heavy top quark, a limitation on the number of fermion generations, a relation between the strong-interaction and weak symmetry-breaking scales, the meaning of the spin-two object, and the origin and physical realization of supersymmetry. Confinement and hard scattering remain essential requirements. The tensor and supersymmetry must be explained positively; passing an absence-of-gravity check is not by itself a physical explanation.

The user's three targets are admitted as qualitative future validation conditions. We do not use a later measured top mass, select three generations in a scan, or fit a modern value of the strong coupling. “Top” names the putative heavy up-type state until a candidate supplies its complete multiplet and charges. Modern theory may suggest mechanisms; knowledge of the desired outcome cannot count as a prediction.

## The prediction contract

| Target | First meaningful success | Stronger result | What would only be accommodation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exceptional top mass | Derive one unsuppressed up-type Yukawa and protected suppression of the other quarks, including the bottom | Calculate the largest physical Yukawa and a mass ratio after normalization, running and threshold matching | Choose a large top entry in an otherwise arbitrary Yukawa matrix |
| Limited generations | Derive a finite upper bound within a precisely specified class of theories, surviving gravity decoupling | Derive the actual number, ideally three, without selecting that number beforehand | Choose an index or intersection number equal to three; identify three oscillator levels with families |
| QCD–Fermi proximity | Derive a relation between the scales with a stated residual parameter dependence | Predict a narrow dimensionless ratio after independent inputs are fixed | Choose two independent tensions, condensates or wall positions to match the two scales |
| Spin-two object | Derive its origin, physical operator or sector, mass and couplings, accounting for factorization and companion states | Explain its spectrum and residues through the same dynamics as confinement | Rename an interacting massless graviton or assign a tensor mass by hand |
| Supersymmetry | Derive the relevant worldsheet or spacetime charges and their physical realization, including any breaking | Explain why the structure is selected and calculate the resulting spectrum and breaking scale | Assume a worldsheet symmetry proves spacetime superpartners, or insert an arbitrary breaking scale |

Each candidate must list its discrete choices, continuous parameters and boundary conditions before comparison. Results selected after a scan must include the rejected alternatives and selection rule. A useful conditional prediction is permitted, but its conditions stay attached to it.

## A heavy top is a statement about Yukawas

In a one-Higgs-doublet low-energy theory, define

\[
v=(\sqrt2 G_F)^{-1/2},\qquad m_i(\mu)=y_i(\mu)v(\mu)/\sqrt2
\]

at tree level in a specified running convention; physical pole masses require matching. The leading target is one up-type Yukawa of order unity while the other quark Yukawas are small. The top may be the least suppressed quark, with the hierarchy residing mainly in the lighter states.

A concrete first mechanism is a rank-one leading Yukawa, with small controlled rank-lifting terms. A single transverse interaction point supplies a relevant local string-theory benchmark [M13, M14]. We must calculate kinetic normalization and explain the up/down asymmetry: obtaining one large eigenvalue in *each* charge sector does not yet explain the exceptional top. In a two-Higgs theory the independent ratio of Higgs expectation values is another input to audit.

The first comparison is between a geometric selection rule and infrared gauge–Yukawa attraction. The latter can reduce sensitivity to an initial coupling but cannot select a family by itself. The new calculations show that rank-one suppression works for two, three and four families, while the simple gauge–Yukawa flow admits an integer interval of three through eight. Neither result selects exactly three.

Top condensation is an alternative research branch [M18]. Its attraction is that the heavy quark and weak order parameter share dynamics. Its obligation is to derive the interaction strength and compositeness scale, rather than insert a near-critical four-fermion coupling and an unrelated cutoff. No top-condensation model is claimed to pass the targets here.

## Finite families must survive the nongravitational limit

Count chiral low-energy multiplets, not oscillator excitations. A string oscillator tower is normally infinite and contains changing spins. A net chiral index also leaves vectorlike pairs uncounted, so the full massless spectrum and its mass terms remain necessary.

Flux quantization and global charge constraints provide a concrete direction. Published bounds exist in specified compact string constructions [M15]. Their relevance here must be tested along the limit removing gravity. Decompactification can change normalizability, loosen constraints or introduce a continuum. A finite bound in each compact model need not give a uniform bound on the whole limiting family.

The proposed mathematical test is precise. For flux vector n, a positive charge budget nᵀQn ≤ 2L and chiral index ℓᵀn imply

\[
|N_{\rm chiral}|\leq\sqrt{2L\,\ell^TQ^{-1}\ell}.
\]

Determine whether the right-hand side remains finite as the gravitational coupling disappears, and whether the bound actually controls complete generations. This formula is a linear-algebra diagnostic for a chosen positive flux sector; we have not proved that a suitable string vacuum realizes all its hypotheses.

## Define the two scales before relating them

I interpret “nearness” as the modest logarithmic separation of hadronic masses and the Fermi scale, rather than their equality. The hadronic scale is roughly a fraction of a GeV and v is of order 10² GeV. The benchmark is a separation of a few orders of magnitude; its exact numerical value depends on which hadronic observable is chosen.

| Symbol | Meaning | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| v | Weak order parameter inferred from G_F | Historical low-energy input, or output if G_F is withheld |
| Λ_MSbar^(Nf) | Scheme- and flavour-dependent QCD running scale | Specify scheme, loop order and threshold matching |
| fπ or a hadron mass | Observable strong-interaction scale | Compute the conversion from Λ; do not equate them silently |
| √σ_color | Confining flux-tube scale in a specified regime | Distinguish pure-gauge tension from string breaking with dynamical quarks |
| M_s=1/√α′ | Microscopic string scale, if the candidate has one | Do not silently identify it with √σ_color or a Planck scale |
| M_KK, M_comp | Extra excitation or compositeness scales | Count them as independent until the model relates them |

Ordinary QCD chiral breaking can break electroweak symmetry, but gives an effective v of order fπ, far too small [M17]. Increasing the number of ordinary QCD-sized doublets cannot bridge the gap within the asymptotically free matter content considered here.

Dimensional transmutation is a more useful bridge. At one loop and with fixed active flavour content,

\[
\Lambda=M\exp[-2\pi/(b_0\alpha_s(M))].
\]

If the string construction determines α_s and the relation of M to v, this becomes predictive. If both remain free, the exponential is an explanation of a mechanism, not a prediction of the ratio. The numerical example α_s=0.12 is explicitly synthetic.

There is already a calculable link through heavy-quark thresholds. At fixed high-scale coupling, one-loop matching gives Λ₃ proportional to (m_t m_b m_c)^(2/27), with a separate dimensionful factor. Scaling all three heavy masses with v gives Λ₃ proportional to v^(2/9) while those thresholds stay heavy. This weak response does not determine v/Λ₃. See the full dimensional formula and assumptions in the derivations; the threshold idea has an established antecedent [M19].

## First construction to try

Begin with a local seven-brane or related nongravitational gauge sector whose zero modes and cubic couplings can be computed [M12]. Use it to test whether a rank-one up coupling, a bounded chiral spectrum and a restricted strong coupling can coexist. A local patch is insufficient: retain enough global information to test the family bound, boundary conditions and decoupling. The resulting gauge sector must also have a confining string description with the original hadronic properties.

The first deliverable is one explicit geometry or microscopic Hamiltonian, its full light spectrum, normalized couplings and parameter ledger. State its tensor interpretation and supersymmetry algebra at the same time. The next deliverable is the dependence of all five targets on the same data. If family number, top Yukawa, scale ratio and symmetry breaking can still be adjusted independently, document that failure and test the alternative construction. No existing construction has been shown in this folder to satisfy all five targets.

## Explain the tensor and supersymmetry together

The first closed-string tensor has a left/right oscillator origin. Its interpretation requires a spectrum and interaction calculation: a coupled physical massless helicity-two particle obeys the soft-theorem constraints, while a massive tensor bound state is compatible with a nongravitational physical theory. A holographic bulk metric can encode the latter; its gravitational bulk dynamics must remain explicit.

Supersymmetry needs its own physical dictionary. Distinguish two-dimensional worldsheet symmetry, global spacetime supersymmetry, local spacetime supersymmetry and any approximate symmetry of composite levels. Derive charges rather than inferring them from notation. If an exact spacetime symmetry is broken, calculate the resulting states and revisit all flavor and scale results that relied on it.

We now have a joint benchmark: the smooth Witten D4 geometry, with antiperiodic fermions on a spatial circle. Its tensor spectrum is gapped, its inherited spacetime supersymmetry is broken, and its long-string tension follows from the same geometry. Our radial calculation reproduces m_tensor/M_KK=1.5669021. The unwanted KK scale remains too close to the tensor scale for a controlled pure-QCD identification. The choice of microscopic background and the origin of its supersymmetric parent are not derived. This is a mechanism to develop, not completion of either explanatory target.

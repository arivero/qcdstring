# Research plan: strings as quantum matter

Our first commitment is to keep each scale attached to the phenomenon it describes. The color-flux tension must follow from strong-interaction dynamics. If a candidate also has a microscopic string scale, distinguish it explicitly and derive the relation between them. We do not move a hadronic tension to the Planck scale to reinterpret an unwanted state.

The main working hypothesis is that a string describes extended collective degrees of freedom of a quantum system. Color flux, trace ordering and renormalization may account for its worldsheets and apparent extra coordinates. A second hypothesis is that some of these string systems admit an autonomous ultraviolet definition after gravity decouples.

Both hypotheses have useful modern precedents. Neither supplies a complete four-dimensional hadronic string model. The research task is to find a construction that survives several tests simultaneously.

We already know modern theoretical benchmarks. Their reproduction serves as a control calculation when adapting them to a new candidate; it is not the research objective. The substantial work is to extend the constructions into a regime where their useful properties coexist.

## Explanatory targets from the first cycle

The central targets are an exceptionally heavy top, a derived limitation on generations, a relation between the QCD and Fermi scales, the physical meaning of the spin-two object, and the origin and realization of supersymmetry. T01/T05 must explain the tensor as well as audit it; T14 identifies the symmetry and its fate. These enter immediately alongside T10–T13. The prediction contract, scale definitions and historical convention are in [SUCCESS_CRITERIA.md](SUCCESS_CRITERIA.md).

The first candidate should tie these targets to shared microscopic data: a chiral zero-mode sector with a dominant up-type coupling, finite constraints on family replication, and a restriction on the gauge coupling and symmetry-breaking vacuum. Do not impose three generations before computing the allowed spectrum. Do not call a rank-one matrix a numerical top-mass prediction.

Two controls are already available. A one-Higgs, single-Yukawa, color-only one-loop flow permits both asymptotic freedom and a positive fixed Yukawa/color ratio for 3–8 generations. Separately, a positive flux budget can bound a chiral index, but a degenerating quadratic form may destroy a uniform bound in a limit. These are conditional calculations to guide construction, not a solution of the targets.

A third benchmark now relates tensor masses and supersymmetry breaking in one smooth background. The Witten D4 construction gives a gapped physical tensor spectrum and antiperiodic fermions on a contractible spatial circle. Our calculation reproduces its lowest tensor mass and derives the tension in the same conventions. The remaining KK states obstruct a controlled pure-Yang–Mills interpretation; the first task is to determine whether a modification can remove that obstruction without losing the shared mechanism.

## First target and competing approaches

Keep four-dimensional pure SU(N) Yang–Mills at fixed 't Hooft coupling as the control for the confinement calculations. Heavy external probes provide a clean setting for an area law, a stable long flux tube, glueball operators and a topology expansion. In the flavour construction, include dynamical quarks from the start and account for their thresholds and string breaking. The pure-gauge control is not the full physical candidate.

Allocate roughly one third of the pilot to the strong sector, one third to flavour and scale relations, and one third to decoupling and consistency. These shares are working choices, to be revised at each decision point.

| Approach | What it could establish | Principal obstacle |
|---|---|---|
| Long confining flux tube in four dimensions | A controlled string effective theory of gauge dynamics | An infrared expansion does not determine a short-string spectrum or a UV completion |
| Large-N worldsheet or loop construction | A microscopic origin for string topology and interactions | A genus expansion alone does not prove a local worldsheet continuum limit |
| Little string theory | Interacting finite-tension strings with gravity removed | Strong dynamics, six-dimensional starting point and excess supersymmetry |
| NCOS | An interacting open-string tower with decoupled bulk closed strings in a specified limit | Electric background, nonlocality and careful treatment of metric scaling and winding sectors |
| Holographic confining construction | One language for flux tubes, tensor resonances and hard scattering | Controlling string corrections and separating unwanted modes from the confinement scale |
| Local seven-brane or related chiral gauge sector | Computable zero modes and cubic couplings; possible common data for the three targets | Canonical normalization, a weak vacuum, global family constraints and a genuine decoupling limit |
| String-supported compositeness or top condensation | A common origin for a heavy quark and weak symmetry breaking | Deriving the binding strength, criticality and scales while retaining the required spectrum |

The last approach contains gravity in its bulk description. Its role is to test whether gravitational variables can encode nongravitational observables. If the intended claim were that no description anywhere may contain gravity, this branch would not meet it.

## The three hadronic tests one candidate must pass

The first test is confinement. Derive the potential from a rectangular Wilson loop, including the leading quantum fluctuation correction. For a long open flux tube with only two massless transverse modes in four spacetime dimensions,

\[
V(R)=\sigma R+\mu-\frac{\pi}{12R}+\cdots .
\]

The expansion is restricted to \(R\sqrt{\sigma}\gg1\), below string breaking when quarks are dynamical. Extra gapless worldsheet modes change the coefficient. A fit must therefore test the spectrum of worldsheet degrees of freedom, not simply add adjustable terms. The Wilson-loop starting point is historical; the systematic effective-string methods are imported [H2, M1, M2].

The second test is the physical tensor spectrum. Compute the transverse-traceless part of an appropriate gauge-invariant correlator, its poles, residues and boundary conditions. A massive \(2^{++}\) resonance can belong to a confining theory. A coupled, physical massless helicity-2 particle brings the soft-theorem constraints into play [H3]. The decisive quantities are the physical eigenmodes and their couplings, not the name of a bulk field.

The third test is short-distance behavior. A hadronic proposal must eventually address both Regge behavior and hard constituent scattering. Under the usual leading constituent-counting assumptions, exclusive fixed-angle processes scale as \(d\sigma/dt\sim s^{2-n}\), up to logarithms and qualifications concerning factorization [H6]. A fixed-tension flat-space string amplitude has the wrong generic high-energy fixed-angle softness for this task. Imported warped-space calculations show a possible mechanism for obtaining hard amplitudes [M9]; they do not finish a derivation for asymptotically free QCD.

The candidate, parameters and physical dictionary must stay the same across all three tests. Successes obtained from three unrelated models do not answer the main question.

There is a more microscopic discriminator before phenomenological fitting: the candidate Wilson-loop functional must satisfy the gauge theory's regulated loop equations and exact retracing identities. These constrain a proposed worldsheet more sharply than an adjustable spectrum. T04 includes this test, using the imported loop-based approach [M8].

| Weeks 1–2 | T01/T02 consistency audit; T10/T12 spectrum; T14 supercharges | One candidate, tensor/state dictionary, actual symmetry algebra, family counts, scales and decoupling path | Discard tensor renaming, unsupported supercharges and imposed family counts |
| Weeks 2–4 | T11 normalized cubic couplings and rank lifting; T03/T04 strong-sector controls | Physical Yukawa matrices, up/down selection rules and Wilson-loop benchmark | Retain only derived hierarchies and valid long-string claims |
| Weeks 3–5 | T12 family bound along the limit; T07/T08 as decoupling controls | Index bound, full zero-mode count and retained interaction table | Reject a bound lost in the limit or a sector rendered free |
| Weeks 5–7 | T13 weak vacuum; joint T05/T14 tensor and symmetry-breaking dynamics | Threshold-matched scale relation, tensor residues, partner spectrum and breaking scale | Identify independent fitted scales, unwanted KK states and lost symmetry protection |
| Weeks 7–9 | T06 Regge/hard amplitudes; T11 mass running with the actual spectrum | Two amplitude limits and a conditional largest Yukawa or mass ratio | Reject unsupported hard behavior or complete-RG-attraction assumptions |
| Weeks 9–11 | T09 and joint T05/T10–T14 audit | Anomaly, extra-state, parameter-dependence and gravity-leakage checks after symmetry breaking | Determine which predictions and protection mechanisms actually survive |
| Week 12 | Joint review of one candidate | Technical memo, reproducibility package and an explicit verdict on each target | Continue a construction with a real prediction, narrow the claim, or stop it |

This schedule is for a pilot. It does not promise to solve the worldsheet of four-dimensional Yang–Mills in three months. It promises a model, calculations and explicit reasons to retain or reject that model.

## First year

By month three, complete the first simultaneous consistency, flavour and scale audit. The included tensor, rank, flux-budget and RG calculations are controls: they are not yet an audit of one microscopic theory.

By month six, require at least one new result not inserted as a fit parameter: a protected top/light hierarchy, a family bound surviving decoupling and symmetry breaking, a restricted strong/weak ratio, or a controlled tensor/supersymmetry mechanism beyond the existing benchmark. Reproducing known results is validation rather than the final research contribution. A negative result identifying incompatible assumptions is publishable progress.

By month twelve, aim for a controlled construction linking more than one explanatory target through the same data, with a defensible strong-sector dictionary. Report which parts remain phenomenological. Exact three-family selection and a numerical top-mass prediction are stronger milestones than an upper bound or rank hierarchy; do not blur them.

The physical stakes are clear: a controlled strong-interaction string with no physical massless graviton would give the programme its desired interpretation. An autonomous little-string or NCOS model answers a related existence question even if it cannot describe hadrons.

## Rules for choosing the next calculation

Prefer a calculation that distinguishes mechanisms. Establishing the absence of a normalizable tensor zero mode is useful; noting that a metric is “emergent” is not enough. Showing a finite string interaction after a decoupling limit is useful; writing \(G_N\to0\) alone is not enough.

Check the easy failure modes early: tachyons, negative-norm states, loss of Lorentz symmetry in the intended physical sector, gauge anomalies, inconsistent projection, an infinite tower at the supposed low-energy scale, or a finite compactification that restores an unwanted graviton.

Use supersymmetric and nonlocal models as laboratories while keeping their costs visible. A construction that uses a near-critical electric field is not a Lorentz-invariant hadronic vacuum. An infrared string in four dimensions does not require the same free-field worldsheet description as a critical fundamental string.

The next construction must specify both its normalized cubic interactions and its actual supercharges. Follow its chiral multiplicity, tensor spectrum and couplings along the same limit that removes physical gravity and realizes the chosen supersymmetry breaking. Determine whether the family constraint and Yukawa protection survive and whether its gauge coupling is restricted enough for T13. Use the now-reproduced smooth D4 model as the strong-sector control, with its KK obstruction recorded explicitly.

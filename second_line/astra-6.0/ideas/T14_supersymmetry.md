# T14 — Explain the origin and fate of supersymmetry

**Priority:** P1 for explanation, P0 where a claim depends on exact supersymmetry. **Status:** structural distinctions and a smooth-background benchmark worked out; microscopic necessity and realistic breaking remain open.

State whether the candidate has worldsheet supersymmetry, global spacetime supercharges, local spacetime supersymmetry, or only an approximate algebra organizing bound states. Derive the action, constraints or charge algebra appropriate to that claim. Worldsheet fermions alone do not prove physical superpartner degeneracy.

For an RNS construction, compute the Ramond sector, the projections and the admissible spacetime supercharge operators. Check BRST invariance, mutual locality and modular/open–closed consistency. The non-supersymmetric heterotic control shows why a tachyon-free spectrum is not sufficient to infer spacetime supersymmetry; its quantum vacuum must still be audited.

If spacetime supersymmetry survives, calculate its multiplets and whether the vacuum breaks it. A massive four-dimensional N=1 spin-two multiplet contains spins (2,3/2,3/2,1). Follow its companions along the same limit as the tensor. Include a goldstino for spontaneous global breaking; account for the gravitino if local supersymmetry is involved.

If boundary conditions or a deformation break the symmetry, derive the breaking scale and its effects on the tensor spectrum, stability, tadpoles, quark couplings and family bound. Recheck any protection or positivity condition borrowed from a supersymmetric calculation.

**Executed benchmark:** in the Witten D4 background, an antiperiodic spatial circle removes fermion zero modes and closes smoothly in the bulk. The tensor equation has no regular normalizable zero mode; its lowest mass is 1.5669021 M_KK. This reproduces a known mechanism. Extra KK states obstruct a controlled pure-QCD identification, and the microscopic choice of background is not derived.

**Worldsheet-only alternative:** derive physical fermionic modes on the flux tube and their boundary conditions, then calculate the finite-size spectrum. The free-field Casimir controls in the new note distinguish bosonic, NS and R cases.

**Deliverable:** supercharges/constraints, their physical interpretation, a full state table, the origin of any breaking parameter, and a recalculated T05/T11–T13 analysis.

**Reject the explanation if:** two-dimensional and four-dimensional supersymmetry are conflated; partners are removed by fiat; a free parameter called “SUSY breaking” hides the weak scale; a nonsupersymmetric projection is accepted without checking its vacuum; or exact supersymmetric results are retained without an error estimate after breaking.

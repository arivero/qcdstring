# T03 — Construct the long confining string

**Priority:** P1. **Type:** historical target plus imported effective theory.

Use pure four-dimensional SU(N) Yang–Mills with heavy external probes. Define the static potential from a rectangular Euclidean Wilson loop:

\[
V(R)=-\lim_{\mathcal T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal T}
\log\langle W(R,\mathcal T)\rangle .
\]

After establishing an area-law regime, describe a long flux tube by transverse displacements. If they are its only massless fields, each contributes the open-interval Casimir energy \(-\pi/(24R)\). This gives \(-\pi/(12R)\) in four spacetime dimensions [H2, M1, M2].

**Calculation:** derive the quadratic fluctuation determinant, boundary counterterms and the leading corrections. Use the long-string expansion to compare a two-transverse-mode theory with candidates containing additional light internal modes.

For illustration, the Nambu–Goto ground-state expression is

\[
E_0(R)=\sigma R\sqrt{1-\frac{\pi(D-2)}{12\sigma R^2}}.
\]

It is an infrared benchmark, not an exact four-dimensional UV spectrum. The short-length imaginary square root cannot establish a physical tachyon in a regime where the effective expansion fails.

**Deliverable:** a prediction sheet giving the leading coefficient, expected validity range, extra-mode alternatives and the effect of dynamical-quark string breaking.

**Reject or narrow the route if:** an alleged universal coefficient depends on unexplained gapless degrees of freedom; or if an infrared fit is used to claim a complete short-string theory.

The elementary Casimir coefficient and large-length expansion are checked in the script. No Wilson-loop data have been fitted.

# T01 — Locate the spin-2 obstruction

**Priority:** P0. **Type:** historical consistency result; proposed amplitude audit.

The question is which assumptions turn a tensor excitation into gravity. Use an asymptotically flat Lorentz-invariant S-matrix with a physical massless helicity-2 state, ordinary soft factorization, and nonzero couplings.

Derive the leading soft factor and replace the polarization by a pure-gauge polarization. The resulting identity is

\[
\sum_i\eta_i g_i p_i^\nu=0.
\]

For generic interacting processes, momentum conservation makes this compatible with a common coupling. This is a restriction on a coupled physical massless spin-2 particle; it is not a statement that every extended object has such a particle [H3].

Work through the lowest closed-string tensor states and identify the transverse-traceless physical polarization. Gauge redundancy removes longitudinal polarizations, not the entire physical tensor. Use a tachyon-free string when discussing a stable vacuum.

The explanatory part starts with the independent left/right vector excitations and their tensor-product decomposition. In the ten-dimensional NS–NS sector, 8_v times 8_v gives 35 symmetric traceless, 28 antisymmetric and one trace polarization. Account for the two-form and dilaton as well as the tensor. A tachyon-free tree spectrum still needs a quantum-vacuum audit. See notes/SPIN2_AND_SUPERSYMMETRY.md for the state/operator interpretation and the joint supersymmetry test.

**Deliverable:** one explicit amplitude, its soft limit, the gauge variation, and a table saying which assumption each proposed alternative changes. The table should include a mass gap, decoupling, absence of an asymptotic particle, and a change in the physical spacetime dictionary.

**Reject the route if:** it retains the same massless state and coupling but merely renames it, or silently drops Lorentz invariance while claiming an ordinary relativistic vacuum.

The related Weinberg–Witten obstruction is a separate imported check. A Lorentz-covariant conserved stress tensor imposes restrictions on massless higher-spin particles carrying momentum. It does not exclude massive tensor bound states, and its hypotheses must be audited before applying it to an emergent description [M11].

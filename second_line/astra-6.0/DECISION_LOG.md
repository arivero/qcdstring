# Decision log

Initial entry: 6 November 2026; programme date end of 1974.

The physical target is quantum matter without a dynamical spacetime graviton. The first laboratory is pure SU(N) Yang–Mills in four dimensions, with heavy probes and a large-N expansion. This is a scope choice, not an assertion that the full hadronic world is pure Yang–Mills.

We accept the standard soft-spin-2 obstruction. We will not spend time renaming the conventional graviton, shifting its intercept by hand, or dropping its factorization channels.

We retain two separate routes: a confining gauge-theory string and an autonomous finite-tension string obtained by decoupling gravity. Their achievements are not interchangeable.

We permit holographic calculations. Bulk gravitational dynamics must remain explicit, while the physical boundary theory can have an external, nondynamical metric.

The initial hard-wall tensor model has a gapped normalizable spectrum with the selected boundary condition. Its gap is not a derivation of confinement from Yang–Mills. An alternative infrared boundary condition can restore a zero mode, which makes the missing dynamical input particularly visible.

Numerical work completed: color-topology factors; critical central-charge deficits; finite-size energy checks; elementary LST/NCOS scalings; hard-wall tensor roots and normalization/positivity checks. The results file records exact scope.

## Revision 0.2: flavour and scales enter immediately

The user's three success criteria supersede the previous decision to postpone flavour. They are central explanatory targets: an exceptional top mass, a limitation on generations and a QCD–Fermi scale relation. The initial hadronic and nongravitational tests remain in force.

Do not impose three generations in a search. An upper bound is a worthwhile distinct result. Admit the qualitative future targets but keep later measured masses and precision couplings out of numerical inputs. Keep M_s distinct from the color-flux tension.

Completed in this revision: thirteen total test briefs; the prediction contract; twenty-two additional checks of rank hierarchies, coupled gauge–Yukawa evolution, a positive flux-budget bound and its nonuniform limit, heavy-quark threshold matching, and the size of ordinary-QCD electroweak breaking. All checks passed. These controls do not constitute a string vacuum or mass prediction.

The most useful conditional result is the integer interval 3≤N_g≤8 for simultaneous color asymptotic freedom and a positive nonzero fixed Yukawa/color ratio in the stated one-loop, single-Yukawa approximation. It makes three the smallest allowed value without selecting it uniquely. Finite running need not reach the fixed ratio.

The next limiting issue is whether a compactification-based family bound remains uniform when physical gravity is removed. Our positive quadratic toy shows explicitly why this cannot be assumed. The top threshold provides a calculable but weak effect on QCD: ∂lnΛ₃/∂lnm_t=2/27 at fixed high-scale coupling.

## Revision 0.3: tensor and supersymmetry as explanations

The programme now has five explanatory targets. T01/T05 must account for the tensor's origin, physical operator, spectrum, interactions and companions. The new T14 must determine what supersymmetry acts on, why it occurs in the candidate, and how its realization produces the physical spectrum. Worldsheet, global spacetime and local spacetime supersymmetry are distinct claims.

Completed: the Witten D4 tensor radial problem, with smooth-tip and UV conditions. No regular normalizable zero mode exists. The lowest tensor mass is 1.5669021099 M_KK; three endpoint cutoffs and an independent Rayleigh quotient agree. The same metric determines the Wilson-loop tension. Its contractible spatial circle carries antiperiodic fermions and breaks inherited spacetime supersymmetry. This is a reproduced mechanism, not a new spectrum or a derivation of QCD.

The obstruction is explicit: unwanted KK modes remain at the tensor scale in the controlled classical approximation. The weak-coupling route to a clean Yang–Mills separation leaves that approximation. Neither the microscopic selection of the background nor the necessity of its supersymmetric parent is explained.

Additional analytic controls distinguish physical NS/R worldsheet-fermion effects on the long-string Casimir term and count the companions of a massive four-dimensional N=1 spin-two multiplet. A symmetry-breaking proposal must now recheck any flavor hierarchy or family bound that relied on supersymmetry.
